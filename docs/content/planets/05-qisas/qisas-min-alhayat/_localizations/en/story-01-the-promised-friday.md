## The Theme

**A promise that became heavy.**

## The Value Objective

The reader sees what **keeping a promise that became costly** looks like — without being told so, and without the cost being repaid at the end.

🔴 **And the story is not assessed.** No questions, no game, no mastery criterion. The objective is written **for the editor, not the reader**.

## The Arc

Nadim promised his sister in winter to teach her to fly the kite on **the first Friday with wind**. Weeks passed with no wind. And when the wind came, it came on the same Friday as the **club team's selection trial, once a year**. Nadim calculated a way out and did not find one. So he went with his sister. And the list was closed. **Then Ruba told him she could have waited.**

## 🔴 The distinctive decision: the one who was kept for **does not know the value of keeping**

| ❌ Rejected form | ✅ Form used |
|---|---|
| Ruba knows what he sacrificed and thanks him | 🔴 **Ruba says: I could have waited** |
| The coach makes an exception | 🔴 **The list was closed, and the trial is next year** |
| "And he understood that a promise is dearer than a chance" | 🔴 **No sentence** |
| The family knows and is proud | 🔴 **No one knows but Samer, and he says nothing** |

> 🔴 **Ruba's sentence is the whole story:** "**I could have waited for another Friday.**"
>
> And it is not ingratitude. It is **honest**: a seven-year-old did not know what was paid, and did not ask for it to be paid. This makes what Nadim did **his decision alone**, with no witnesses and no accounting.
>
> **And had she thanked him, keeping the promise would have become a completed transaction**, and the reader would leave thinking a promise is kept **because the other side appreciates it**. And most promises — their makers never know what they cost.
>
> 🔴 **And Nadim says nothing to her.** He does not correct, does not explain, and does not mention the club afterward. Any sentence from him here **turns the story into a complaint**.

## Full Narration Script for Recording

```
P1.  In the corner of Nadim's room is a paper kite with a green tail.
     He built it in January from reed and paper.
     And the tail Ruba cut herself, so it came out crooked.
     And on the nail behind the door, a long cord, carefully coiled.
P2.  He said to her that day: the first Friday with wind, we take it to the wide field.
     And I teach you how to fly it yourself.
     Ruba said: myself?
     He said: yourself.
P3.  Weeks passed, and the air was still.
     Every Thursday Ruba stands at the room door and asks with one word: tomorrow?
     And every Thursday Nadim says: no wind.
     So she goes back to her room and does not insist.
P4.  On Thursday, after afternoon, the curtain moved.
     Then it moved a second time, longer.
     Ruba stood at the window and said nothing.
     And she looked at the cord on the nail.
P5.  In the evening Nadim went down to the club to get his bag.
     On the door a new notice: the selection trial for the team, tomorrow, from nine to eleven.
     He read it twice.
     One trial a year, and no other.
P6.  Nadim has been training for a whole year.
     And the coach told him last week his name is on the list if he comes.
     And the wide field is on the other side of the neighborhood.
     And the wind there fades after two, he tried that twice.
P7.  He sat on the step and calculated.
     He goes to the trial at nine, and leaves at eleven.
     Then he returns, and takes Ruba at twelve.
     The road is forty minutes.
P8.  He erased the numbers and wrote them again.
     Arrival at one, and the wind fades after two.
     One hour is not enough for a seven-year-old to fly a kite herself.
     And the first Friday with wind is not repeated by a promise.
P9.  At seven in the morning Ruba was standing at the door.
     She wore her coat, the kite in her hand, its crooked tail on the floor.
     She did not ask, and did not say tomorrow.
     She was only waiting.
P10. Nadim looked at the cord on the nail, then took it.
     He said: come on.
     And he said nothing about the notice on the club door.
     And they went out.
P11. At nine they were in the wide field, and the wind pulled the paper.
     And at the club the list was being read.
     Ruba ran, so the kite rose off the ground stumbling, then steadied.
     And it was in her hand alone.
P12. Samer passed, returning from the club, and stopped at the fence.
     He said: they asked about you. Then they closed the list.
     And he said nothing more, and walked on.
     Ruba looked at her brother and asked: which list?
P13. Nadim said: nothing.
     The kite stayed high for seven more minutes.
     Then the wind faded, so it came down slowly onto the thistles.
     They gathered the cord, and no one spoke on the way.
P14. At home Ruba put the kite on the table, and pulled the crooked tail.
     Then she said a sentence she had heard from someone: I could have waited for another Friday.
     She said it looking at the tail, not at him.
     And Nadim did not answer.
P15. The list closed at eleven, and the trial is next year.
     No one opened an exception, and Nadim did not ask for an exception.
     And the year of training stayed a year of training.
     This is not fixed by another Friday.
P16. In the evening Nadim took out his notebook and counted the weeks to the next trial.
     Fifty-two.
     He wrote the number at the top of the page, and closed the notebook.
     And he heard Ruba in the other room explaining to their mother how to fly a kite herself.
P17. The two coats on the back of the chair, and in one pocket a thistle leaf.
     The kite on the table, its green tail shorter than it was.
     And the house is quiet.
P18. The cord coiled on the nail behind the door.
     And its green end touches the floor.
```

## Parent Guide

> **An 18-page story about a promise that became heavy**
>
> Nadim promised his sister in winter to teach her to fly the kite on the first Friday with wind. And when the wind came, it came on the day of the **club team's selection trial, once a year**. He calculated a way out and did not find one. So he went with his sister, and the list was closed.
>
> **🔴 And what the story was built on**
> **Ruba did not thank him.** She said: "I could have waited for another Friday."
>
> And she is honest: a seven-year-old did not know what was paid, and did not ask for it to be paid.
>
> And had she thanked him, keeping the promise would have become **a completed transaction**, and the reader would leave thinking a promise is kept because the other side appreciates it. **And most promises — their makers never know what they cost.**
>
> **And no exception at the end**
> The list closed. And the trial is next year. **And no one opened a door, and Nadim did not ask for a door.**
>
> **🔴 And no sentence at the end of the story says the meaning**
> The last page: a cord coiled on a nail, and its green end touching the floor. **And nothing else.**
>
> This is deliberate. A reader of eleven **smells the lesson from afar**, and when they smell it they pull back. And the meaning they extract themselves stays; the meaning told to them is used up.
>
> **🔴 So do not ask them "What did you learn from the story?"**
> The question turns the text into an exam, and asks them to **guess the answer you want**.
>
> **Ask instead — if you want to ask:**
> - "**Was there a way out Nadim did not see?**" *(page 8)*
> - "**If Ruba had said thank you, what would have changed?**" *(page 14)*
>
> And both are **without a right answer**.
>
> **And no questions and no game after the story**
> The whole series is without games and comprehension questions, **on purpose**, and the justification for that is written in the series bible.
>
> **And the default is "read myself"**
> Unlike this planet's stories for ages 3–8. The independent reader wants to read themselves, **and the narration is available** for whoever wants or needs it.
>
> **The link to the values planet**
> This story is a sibling of the episode [Saturday](../../../04-qiyam/mawaqif-wa-qararat/ep-04-saturday.md) — the theme is one, and the angle is different: there is **how keeping is planned for**, and here **what remains after it**.
