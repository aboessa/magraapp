## The Theme

**Telling the truth and losing credit you earned by your own work.**

## The Value Objective

The reader sees what **giving up a rank** looks like, because part of it was not his — without being told so, and without it being known that it was a giving-up.

🔴 **And the story is not assessed.** The objective is written **for the editor, not the reader**.

🔴 **And no adult praises Faris or scolds him.** The panel recalculates **as a procedure**, and does not say "well done." Any praise sentence **turns the giving-up into a successful transaction**.

## The Arc

Faris handed in **eight pages** for the portfolio contest, and was announced first. Then he read on the judging sheet: **number of works, nine**. So he opened his returned folder and found a ninth page: a drawing of a chair in charcoal, and in its corner a faint letter "N." **And it was the page given the highest score in the whole folder.** And Nadia is fourth, and with a page or without a page she is fourth. **So Faris went and said he had handed in eight.** So it was recalculated, and Raghad became first by a point, and Faris second. **And no one knew why the card changed.**

## 🔴 The distinctive decision: telling the truth **helps no one but a person who does not know**

| ❌ Rejected form | ✅ Form used |
|---|---|
| Nadia rises to a higher rank | 🔴 **Nadia is fourth before and after** |
| Nadia thanks him | 🔴 "**I was looking for it.**" |
| Raghad knows and appreciates | 🔴 **Raghad does not know what happened** |
| The panel announces Faris was honest | 🔴 **A white sticker over his name** |
| "And he understood honesty is dearer than a rank" | 🔴 **A number written in pencil on a folder cover** |
| He claims he does not want the rank | 🔴 "**I do not want to lose the rank. And I want it counted on eight.**" |

> 🔴 **Faris's sentence is the whole story**, and it is two sentences, not one:
> **"I do not want to lose the rank."** — he says his desire frankly, and does not pretend to be indifferent.
> **"And I want it counted on eight."** — and he asks for the procedure anyway.
>
> **And had we dropped the first**, Faris would become a person who does not want what people want, **and this resembles no one**. And honesty that pretends it costs nothing **cannot be taught**.
>
> 🔴 **And Nadia is fourth before and after.** The truth here **rescues no wronged person**, and fixes no wrong. It fixes **a number**. And this is the purest form of the matter: to say what does not benefit you, nor benefit the one you thought you were doing justice to.
>
> 🔴 **And Raghad does not know.** She became first, and thought — as everyone thought — that an administrative error happened. **And Faris did not explain.**

## Full Narration Script for Recording

```
P1.  Faris draws with charcoal, not with colors.
     His fingers are always black, and in his pocket a cloth to wipe them.
     And in his blue folder eight pages, chosen from seventeen.
     The best of them a drawing of a hand holding a cup, he labored on it four days.
P2.  The portfolio contest at the school once a year.
     Each entrant hands in a folder, and the works are read page by page.
     And the first has their drawing printed on the cover of the school magazine.
     And Faris wants the cover.
P3.  On handing-in day Faris counted his pages twice.
     Eight, and eight.
     Then he closed the folder and handed it in, and wrote nothing on the cover.
     And no one wrote.
P4.  After a week the results were hung on the corridor wall.
     Faris's name first, and Raghad second by a difference of two points.
     And beside the names the judging sheet, with columns in it.
     And in the "number of works" column, before his name: nine.
P5.  Faris went home and opened the returned folder.
     He counted: nine pages.
     The ninth a drawing of a chair in charcoal, not his.
     And in its lower corner a faint letter: N.
P6.  On the judging sheet a score for each work.
     And the highest score in his folder was for the chair drawing.
     And the difference between him and Raghad is two points.
     And without the chair the difference turns against him.
P7.  The folders were all returned, and no one will recount.
     And Nadia is in fourth place.
     And Faris calculated her scores: with a page or without a page, she is fourth.
     So the page takes nothing from anyone.
P8.  He thought to return the page to Nadia with no words.
     He puts it in her bag, or says: I found it in the corridor.
     And no one knows, and nothing changes.
     And the page returns to its owner, and this is correct.
P9.  But the score stays counted in the sheet on the wall.
     And the cover is printed next week.
     And Raghad is second by two points, gathered from eight pages all hers.
     And returning the page is not returning the two points.
P10. In the morning Faris stood before the wall for a minute.
     He read his name first, and read the number nine.
     Then he took the folder and the page, and knocked on the judging room door.
     And he went in knowing what he would come out with.
P11. He said three sentences.
     I handed in eight pages, and the ninth is not mine, and on it Nadia's letter.
     Then he said: I do not want to lose the rank.
     And I want it counted on eight.
P12. It was recalculated the same day.
     Raghad became first by one point.
     And Faris became second.
     And the cover was printed with Raghad's drawing.
P13. Nadia took her page from his hand in the corridor.
     She looked at it and said: I was looking for it.
     Then she put it in her folder and walked on.
     And her fourth place did not change.
P14. In the evening the card on the wall had changed.
     A small white sticker over Faris's name, and Raghad's name written over it in another pen.
     And under the sticker shows the edge of a letter from his old name.
     And Faris stood looking, and said nothing.
P15. No one knew why the card changed.
     Some said an administrative error happened, and the talk moved on.
     And Faris did not explain, and no one asked him.
     So he did not get the rank, and did not get what is said to whoever tells the truth.
P16. That night Faris took out his eight pages, and wrote his letter on the back of each one.
     One letter in pencil, in the same corner on every page.
     Then he opened the blue cover and wrote a number at its top.
     And that took twenty minutes.
P17. On the table a blue folder and a black cloth of charcoal.
     And beside it a drawing of a hand holding a cup, and it is the best in it.
     And the house is quiet.
P18. On the cover of the blue folder a number written in pencil.
     Eight.
```

## Parent Guide

> **An 18-page story about a truth that cost its teller and helped no one**
>
> Faris handed in eight pages, and was announced first. Then he read on the judging sheet: **nine**. And the extra page was a drawing of Nadia's, **and it was given the highest score in his whole folder**.
>
> **🔴 And the truth here rescues no one**
> **Nadia is fourth before and after.** Faris calculated that himself: with a page or without a page, **her rank does not change**.
>
> So telling the truth **fixes no wrong** — it fixes **a number**. And this is the purest form of the matter, and the hardest: to say what does not benefit you, nor benefit the one you thought you were doing justice to.
>
> **And he had a clean choice**
> To return the page to Nadia with no words. **And the page returns to its owner, and this is correct** — the story says so literally and with no sarcasm.
>
> And the difference Faris saw: **returning the page is not returning the two points.** The page is one thing, and the score counted on it is another.
>
> **🔴 And the sentence we want is two sentences**
> "**I do not want to lose the rank. And I want it counted on eight.**"
>
> **The first is necessary.** Faris **wants** the rank, and says so frankly, and does not pretend to be indifferent.
>
> And had we dropped it, he would become a person who does not want what people want — **and this resembles no one, and is not imitated**. And honesty that pretends it costs nothing **cannot be taught**.
>
> **Try it at home:** when your child does something hard, **do not ask them to deny it was hard**. "It was hard and you did it" is a sufficient sentence.
>
> **And he got nothing**
> Raghad became first **and does not know what happened**. And Nadia took her page and said: "I was looking for it." **And the card on the wall changed with a white sticker**, and some said an administrative error happened.
>
> **So he did not get the rank, and did not get what is said to whoever tells the truth.**
>
> On purpose. A story in which the honest one is praised **teaches that honesty is an investment**, and the lesson breaks the first time no one is praised. **And most honesty no one knows about.**
>
> **🔴 And no sentence at the end of the story says the meaning**
> The last page: a blue cover, and a number in pencil: **eight**. **And nothing else.**
>
> And the number **was not on the cover on page three**. The reader returning to page three is what this story does, **without asking**.
>
> **So do not ask them "What did you learn?"** nor "**What should Faris have done?**" — the second question especially **imposes a right answer on a story built on the fact that the other choice was clean too**.
>
> **Ask — if you want:**
> - "**Who benefited from what Faris did?**"
> - "**Delete the sentence 'I do not want to lose the rank.' What changes in him?**"
> - "**Page 3 and page 18 the same cover. What is the difference?**"
>
> And all of them are **without a right answer**.
>
> **And the practical detail on page 16**
> Faris came to write his letter on the back of each page, **and write the count on the cover**. Twenty minutes prevent what happened from happening again.
>
> And this is something you use at home literally: **a name on everything, and a count on every container.**
>
