## The Theme

**Being wrong in front of people.**

## The Value Objective

The reader sees what **correcting a mistake in the very place it was said** looks like — without being told so, and without the correction being rewarded.

🔴 **And the story is not assessed.** The objective is written **for the editor, not the reader**.

## Characters

| Character | Description |
|---|---|
| Hala | 12 years old · calculates in her head · they ask her and do not review her number |
| Muhannad | 12 years old · speaks in a low voice · 🔴 **is not avenged and does not rejoice** |
| The teacher | 🔴 **writes on the board and reads the tape, and does not comment or moralize** |

🔴 **No villain.** And Hala **is not arrogant**: she is the one who was right often, and that is what built her confidence and theirs.

🔴 **And the teacher is present because a school activity needs someone to run it** — her presence is **purely procedural**: she does not praise, does not scold, and does not draw a lesson. Any sentence from her **cancels the correction being Hala's decision**.

## The Arc

Hala is the one they ask for numbers. On activity day she estimated the flagpole rope at twelve metres, **and dismissed Muhannad who said nine**. Then she recounted before the measuring and knew she had erred. The tape came to nine metres and twenty centimetres. **So she stood in the very place and said her wrong number, the right number, and Muhannad's name.** And no one applauded. **Then she said less for two weeks.**

## 🔴 The distinctive decision: the correction in the **same place**, not in the margin

| ❌ Rejected form | ✅ Form used |
|---|---|
| She apologizes to Muhannad privately | 🔴 **She stands in the same place, audibly** |
| "My estimate was approximate" | 🔴 "**My estimate was wrong**" |
| "Nine is close to twelve" | 🔴 "**Nine is closer**" |
| She stays silent, so the measure showed everyone | 🔴 **She says her wrong number herself** |
| She does not mention Muhannad | 🔴 "**And Muhannad said nine before I measured**" |
| The class applauds her honesty | 🔴 **No one applauds** |

> 🔴 **Three parts in one sentence, and dropping any one loses its meaning:**
> 1. **Her wrong number** — because a correction that does not mention the mistake is not a correction.
> 2. **The right number** — because an admission with no alternative is an apology.
> 3. **Muhannad's name** — because she dismissed him publicly, so it is returned to him publicly.
>
> 🔴 **And the third part is the hardest**, and it is what separates **admitting a mistake** from **returning a right to a person**.
>
> 🔴 **And the measure would have shown everyone in two minutes anyway.** This is deliberate: the story **does not make honesty a necessity**, but makes it **a choice of method**. And whoever stays silent does not lie — they let the number speak for them. And the difference between the two is the story.

## Parent Guide

> **An 18-page story about being wrong in front of people**
>
> Hala is the one they ask for numbers. She estimated the flagpole rope at twelve metres **and dismissed Muhannad who said nine**. Then she recounted and knew she had erred. And the measure came: nine metres and twenty centimetres.
>
> **🔴 And what the story was built on: the three parts**
> "**My estimate was wrong. Nine is closer. And Muhannad said nine before I measured.**"
>
> 1. **Her wrong number** — a correction that does not mention the mistake is not a correction.
> 2. **The right number** — an admission with no alternative is an apology.
> 3. **Muhannad's name** — she dismissed him publicly, so she returned it to him publicly.
>
> **And the third is the hardest**, and it is what separates admitting a mistake from **returning a right to a person**.
>
> **🔴 And in the same place**
> She did not apologize to Muhannad privately. She stood **in the same spot** she said her first number, and in the same voice.
>
> And apologizing in the margin for words said in the middle **fixes her feeling and does not fix what people heard**.
>
> **🔴 And the measure would have shown in two minutes**
> This is deliberate. Had silence been a lie, the matter would be a duty, not a choice. **And whoever stays silent here does not lie — they let the number speak for them.** And the story is about the difference between the two.
>
> **And no one applauded**
> Rather a student said: "We always believe you" — without meaning to, and walked on. **And Muhannad did not rejoice** and said: "I was going to stay silent."
>
> On purpose. A correction that is applauded becomes **a performance**, and the matter would break the first time no one applauds.
>
> **🔴 And Hala came to say less for two weeks**
> This is the most honest result in the story, and it is **not a happy ending**. Whoever errs in front of people **pulls back for a while**, and this is natural and needs no quick fixing.
>
> **And if it happens to your child:** do not say "don't be afraid of the mistake" — the sentence is correct and unuseful. Say: "I think you'll say less for a while. This passes."
>
> **🔴 And no sentence at the end of the story says the meaning**
> The last page: a wound tape, its end sticking out a little, with the number nine on it. **And nothing else.**
>
> **So do not ask them "What did you learn?"** but — if you want:
> - "**Page 10 has three sentences. Which one, if deleted, would change everything?**"
> - "**Hala came to say less. Did she gain or lose?**"
>
> And both are **without a right answer**.
>
> **And the two columns on page 16**
> "I estimated" and "I measured." This is a practical detail you can use at home literally: ask about any number your child says: "**Is this an estimate or a measurement?**" — a question about the **source of the number**, not its correctness.
>
> **The link to the values planet**
> This story is a sibling of the episode [The Number I Wrote](../../../04-qiyam/mawaqif-wa-qararat/ep-02-the-number-i-wrote.md) — there a mistake **no one knows**, and here a mistake **everyone knows in two minutes**. And the difference between them is the difference between integrity and humility.
