## The Theme

**A friendship that changes because one of the two friends grew faster.**

## The Value Objective

The reader sees a friendship that **changes and does not end**, and that what remains of it is **smaller than it was and real** — without being told so, and without the friendship being restored as it was.

🔴 **And the story is not assessed.** The objective is written **for the editor, not the reader**.

## Characters

| Character | Description |
|---|---|
| Karam | 11 years old · measures and records · tries to keep things as they are |
| Wasim | 11 years old · grew in a year · 🔴 **does not abandon and is not arrogant, and gets tired** |
| The youth coach | 🔴 **mentioned and does not appear** — the move is an administrative decision, not a person's |

🔴 **No villain.** And no one did wrong in this story: **the schedule and the body did everything.** And that is the hardest thing in it and the truest thing in it.

🔴 **And no adult appears to explain friendship or growing.** And no father says "this is natural." The sentence is correct, **and saying it ends the story before it begins**.

## 🔴 A governing health limit — read before writing or drawing

| # | The rule |
|---:|---|
| 1 | 🔴 **No cause of growth is mentioned at all**: no genetics, no food, no sport, no sleep, no medicine |

| 3 | 🔴 **No doctor, no clinic, and no medical measurement** |

## The Arc

Karam and Wasim are friends, and their marks are on one door frame for nine years. In one year Wasim grew, so he moved to the youth training hour with the older ones. **Karam tried twice to keep things as they were, and it did not work.** Then he **struck out Wasim's last mark with the pen**, then restored it after three days. And Wasim saw it and did not ask, and said: "I did not choose this." **Then they agreed on one Thursday a month, and the long road home.** And Wasim forgets two out of four.

## 🔴 The distinctive decision: the strike-out — and that it is **restored and not erased**

| ❌ Rejected form | ✅ Form used |
|---|---|
| Karam is sad with a noble sadness | 🔴 **Karam strikes out his friend's mark with the pen** |
| Then he hides the strike-out | 🔴 **He redraws the line over it, and the strike-out stays visible** |
| Wasim asks him and they reconcile | 🔴 **Wasim sees and does not ask** |
| "Karam understood their friendship was stronger" | 🔴 "**I did not choose this.**" — "**I know.**" |
| They return to the same team | 🔴 **One Thursday a month, and Wasim forgets two out of four** |

> 🔴 **The strike-out is the story.** A small, ugly, honest act: Karam was angry **at his friend's height**, and he knows that is meaningless, **and was angry anyway**.
>
> **And the story does not name this feeling** — no envy, no jealousy, no "he felt." **The act shows it.**
>
> 🔴 **And redrawing the line over the strike-out is the second decision:** the strike-out is not erased, not apologized for, and not explained. **It stays under the new line, and is seen on the last page.**
>
> 🔴 **And "I did not choose this" / "I know" are two four-word sentences, and they are all the dialogue the story needs.**

P5.  In March they measured again.
     The difference was nine centimetres.
     And Wasim's shoe became size forty-one.
     And one shirt was no longer enough for two.
P6.  In April Wasim moved to the youth training, seven o'clock.
     The decision from the club schedule, not from anyone.

P7.  Karam tried to go at seven and stand outside the fence.
     He stood four times, and watched.

     So he went back alone before the training ended.
     And he did not tell Wasim he had been there.
P9.  Then he made one request of him: come at five once.
     Wasim said: I'll try.
     And he came the next Thursday.
     And he was taller than everyone in the field by a head.
P10. Wasim played very carefully so as not to collide with anyone.
     Then he sat on the edge more than he played.
     And at the end he said: I couldn't play.
     And he did not come the Thursday after.
P11. That night Karam stood at the door frame.
     He ran his finger over Wasim's last line to wipe it, and it did not wipe because it is in pen.
     So he took the pen and struck it out, two crossing strokes.
     Then he turned off the light.
P12. After three days he took out the pen again.
     And he redrew Wasim's line over the two strokes, in its exact place.
     And he wrote the date beside it, as he always does.
     And he did not erase the two strokes.
P13. Karam told no one what he did that night.

     Wasim's finger ran over the two old lines at the bottom, close to the floor.

P16. In the evening they agreed on one thing that needs no height.
     One Thursday a month, and the long road home, with no club.
     Karam wrote the agreement on a small paper and stuck it inside his cupboard.
     And Wasim wrote the date in his phone.
P17. They did not return to one team.
     And Karam did not go back to seven, and Wasim did not move to five.
     And the one shirt was no longer enough for two.
     And of the first four Thursdays, Wasim forgot two.
P18. Karam added on the door frame a small column of dates, with no names.
     So he would know when they measured, not how big the difference was.
     And he came to measure once a year, not four times.
     And the first time he measured alone.
P19. Another Thursday, and the long road, and little talk.
     At the door two shoes: one size thirty-eight, and one size forty-two.
     And the house is quiet.
P20. On the door frame many close lines at the bottom.
     And at the top one line, and under it two crossing strokes.

## Parent Guide

> **A 20-page story about a friendship that changed**
>
> Karam and Wasim are friends and their marks are on one door frame for nine years. In a year Wasim grew, so he moved to another training hour. Karam tried twice to keep things as they were, and it did not work.
>
> **🔴 And no one did wrong in this story**
> Wasim did not abandon, was not arrogant, and did not choose. **The schedule and the body did everything.** And the decision came from **the club schedule, not from anyone** — this is a sentence in the story placed on purpose, to prevent anyone being blamed.
>
> And this is the hardest thing in the story: **there is no one to fix things with.**
>
> **🔴 And the two strokes**
> Karam struck out his friend's mark on the door frame. He was angry **at his friend's height** — and he knows that is meaningless, and was angry anyway.
>
> **And the story does not name this feeling.** No envy, no jealousy, no "he felt." And page 11 **has no face**: a hand and a pen and two strokes. Because naming the feeling spares the reader from feeling it.
>
> **Then he redrew the line over the two strokes, and did not erase them.** And did not apologize, and did not explain. **And they are on the last page, under the high line.**
>
> **🔴 And four words are all the important dialogue**
> "**I did not choose this.**" — "**I know.**"
>
> Wasim saw the two strokes **and did not ask**. And the story needs no more than that.
>
> **And what remained is smaller than it was**
> One Thursday a month, and the long road, with no club. **And of the first four Thursdays Wasim forgot two.**
>
> On purpose. A friendship that returns as it was at the end of the story **lies to the reader**. And what remains is real **and smaller**, and this is enough.
>
> **🔴 And an important limit in this story**
> **There is no explanation of growth in the story.** No cause, no rate, no comparison to age, no doctor. Height is **seen on the door frame and not explained**, because the story is about a friendship and height in it is an event, not a subject.
>
> **And no sentence reassures Karam that he will grow.** And this is deliberate: "you too will grow" is **a promise we do not own**. And the story gives him instead something he owns: **one Thursday a month**.
>
> **🔴 And if your child is in Karam's place**
> **Do not say "this is natural"** — the sentence is correct and unuseful, and ends the conversation.
> **And do not say "you too will grow"** — it may be true, and may not be, and you do not know.
>
> **And if he is in Wasim's place:** do not ask him to make up for it. Ask him one practical question: "**What is the thing that needs no height?**"
>
> **🔴 And no sentence at the end of the story says the meaning**
> The last page: close lines at the bottom, one high line alone, and two strokes under it. **And nothing else.**
>
> **So do not ask them "What did you learn?"** nor "**What did Karam feel?**" — the second question especially **names what the story deliberately did not name**.
>
> **Ask — if you want:**
> - "**Why did he not erase the two strokes?**"
> - "**Page 3 and page 19 are the same angle. What changed?**"
> - "**Wasim forgot two of four Thursdays. Does this spoil the agreement?**"
>
> And all of them are **without a right answer**.
