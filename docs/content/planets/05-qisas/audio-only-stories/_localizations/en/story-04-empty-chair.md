## Characters and Performance

- **Narrator:** natural and warm, not turning the situation into a direct lesson.
- **Mariam:** fast-talking at first, then more attentive.
- **Noor:** calm, few sentences but decisive.
- **Ziad:** a little hesitant, then at ease.

## Full Recording Script

**[A short school bell, then a classroom murmur fading down]**

Mariam came into the classroom carrying her box of colors. Today was "City Project" day. Each group would build a small neighborhood out of paper and cardboard.

Mariam sat with Noor. They set between them the safe scissors, the glue, and the colors. In front of them stayed one empty chair.

Mariam said: "We can start. I will build the houses, and you make the park."

Noor said: "And who will sit here?"

Mariam shrugged: "A spare chair."

At the back of the room Ziad stood near the shelf, holding a rolled-up sheet. He had moved to the school this week, and did not yet know where he would sit at project time.

The groups began to work. The room filled with the sounds of paper, scissors, and laughter.

**[Light paper cutting and the rub of pencils]**

Mariam opened a blue box and said: "We need a river that passes near the houses."

Noor said: "And we need a bridge."

They heard a sheet unrolling behind them. Ziad had drawn a long bridge, with three arches beneath it. He lifted the drawing a little, then lowered it, as if he had wanted to speak but had not found the right moment.

Noor turned and said: "Did you draw this bridge?"

Ziad answered: "Yes. I used to build paper bridges with my dad."

Mariam looked at the empty chair. It was not spare. It had been waiting for a simple question she had not yet asked.

She pulled the chair over a little until there was room, and said: "Will you help us? We want a bridge, but we don't know how to make it stand."

Ziad sat down. He opened his sheet, and said: "If we fold the paper like this, it becomes stronger."

He folded a long strip, then set it between two little boxes. The strip held steady.

Mariam said: "It's standing!"

Ziad said: "The fold in the middle helps it."

The three worked together. Mariam built the houses, Noor made the trees, and Ziad added two bridges: one for the river and one for the people.

When the teacher passed by, she said: "Your city has many ways to cross."

Noor smiled: "Because we have a bridge expert."

Ziad laughed for the first time since he came into the room.

And before the class ended, a paper tree fell near the bridge. Mariam rushed to put it back, then stopped and said: "Ziad, what if we make it a folded base like the bridge?"

He said: "Excellent idea."

He was no longer only helping them; now they asked him, and he asked them.

The bell rang. They put the tools back in the box, and left the city on the table for the glue to dry.

Mariam said, looking at the three chairs: "Tomorrow we sit in the same place?"

Ziad said: "Yes. I have an idea for the bus stop."

They left together. And the three chairs stayed under the table: no spare chair, and no empty chair.

**[A light, distant bell and a warm ending]**

## Mixing Direction

- The classroom murmur is a faint layer that does not compete with the dialogue, and it fades during the quiet sentences.
- Ziad's invitation is not delivered in a pitying tone; it is a real request for a real skill.
- No question or explanation of the value after the ending; the action carries the meaning.

## Parent Guide

> **A ~260-second audio story for a 6–8 child**
>
> An audio-only story — no pictures. A new classmate is standing apart; two girls include him not out of pity, but by asking for a skill he really has.
>
> **What it gives your child:** a model of welcoming someone by **asking for their help**, not by feeling sorry for them. There is no lesson stated at the end — the empty chair filling itself is the meaning.
>
> **How to use it?** A daytime or car listen. Afterward you might ask, with no right answer: "Was there a chair like that in your class?"
