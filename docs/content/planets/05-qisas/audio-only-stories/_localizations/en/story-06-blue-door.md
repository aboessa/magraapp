## Characters and Performance

- **Narrator:** a gentle mystery with no scary whisper.
- **Hana:** curious and attentive to rules.
- **Aunt Dina:** the center's supervisor, clear and cheerful.

🔴 **Name adaptation:** "هنا" → **Hana**, "رامي" → **Rami**, "ميس" → **Mays**, "الخالة دينا" → **Aunt Dina**, substituted locally where needed.

## Full Recording Script

**[A cultural-center door opening, footsteps, and distant children's voices]**

Hana went to the cultural center every Saturday. She knew the art hall, the library, and the music room. But she did not know the blue door at the end of the hall.

The door was always closed. No sign on it, and no sound behind it. And every week Hana passed in front of it and asked herself: "What is in there?"

Her friend Rami said: "Maybe a treasure room."

Mays said: "Or a secret passage."

But Aunt Dina had told them before: "Any closed door in the center we do not open without the supervisor."

That Saturday, Hana was returning a book to the library when she heard a faint sound from the end of the hall.

**[Thump… then a light drag… then thump]**

She stopped. The sound came from behind the blue door.

She called Rami and Mays. The three listened.

**[Thump… thump]**

Rami said: "The treasure is moving."

Mays said: "The secret passage is opening!"

Hana put her hand near the handle, then pulled it back. She said: "The door is still closed. We will call Aunt Dina."

They found her in the art hall. She came with them, and took out a small key.

She said: "Well done for not opening it yourselves. This is the theater-props store, and some things are heavy."

She opened the door.

**[A key turning, then a short creak that is not scary]**

There was no treasure and no secret passage. There were boxes, folded curtains, and a big drum. And on the drum a small rubber ball rolled with each breeze that came from the window.

**[Thump… roll… thump]**

The children laughed.

Mays said: "The monster is a ball."

Hana said: "We never said it was a monster."

Rami said: "But I thought about it a little."

Aunt Dina took the ball and closed the window. Then she said: "Since you are here, will you help me choose the props for the moon play?"

They went in with her. They did not touch the heavy boxes. The aunt pointed to the safe things: a paper hat, a stick with a sponge star on it, and three small bells.

Rami tried on the hat. Mays moved the bells. And Hana held the star.

Hana said: "The door was hiding an ordinary store."

Aunt Dina answered: "The place is ordinary, but what we will make with its tools is not ordinary."

At the show a week later, the store became a palace on the moon, the hat became a crown, and the bells became the sound of the stars.

As for the blue door, it stayed an ordinary door, opened with a key and with a supervisor present. And Hana's curiosity did not lessen; it became a curiosity that knows how to ask before it goes in.

**[Three small chimes, distant applause, an ending]**

## Mixing Direction

- The knocking sound is low and spaced out, and its source is revealed quickly; no horror music and no tension pulse.
- The movement of the heavy boxes is not heard as the children's doing.
- The safety message is inside the event: asking the supervisor for help, not scaring the child of doors.

## Parent Guide

> **A ~250-second audio story for a 6–8 child**
>
> An audio-only story — no pictures. A mysterious closed door and a faint sound; the children resist opening it themselves and call a supervisor instead.
>
> **What it gives your child:** a safety habit wrapped in a small mystery — **ask a responsible adult before opening a closed door** — with no fear of doors and no lecture. Curiosity is kept; it just learns to ask first.
>
> **How to use it?** A daytime or car listen. The takeaway ("call a grown-up you know before opening a heavy or unknown door") is worth saying plainly once, separate from the story.
