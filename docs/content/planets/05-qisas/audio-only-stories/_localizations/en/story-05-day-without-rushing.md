## Full Recording Script

**[Quick footsteps, keys, then a door closing]**

Adam left the house in a hurry. He wanted to buy the bread and get back before the neighborhood match began.

He said to himself: "Two minutes to the bakery, one minute to buy, and two minutes back. Easy!"

He ran to the top of the street, then heard something drop behind him.

**[A jingle of keys on the ground]**

He stopped. The house keys had come out of his pocket. He went back and took them, put them in the same pocket, then ran again.

After a few steps, he heard the jingle again.

He stopped and said: "No time for this!"

He took the keys, and this time he closed the pocket zip. It took only three seconds.

He reached the bakery out of breath. There were two people ahead of him. He shifted his feet, and looked at the door, then at the shelf, then at the two people.

Uncle Sami said: "The bread is coming out right away."

Adam whispered: "'Right away' is a very long word."

The man ahead of him heard it and laughed. And a moment later the tray of bread came out.

**[The sound of a tray set down gently, and paper opening]**

Adam reached out his hand quickly, and his sleeve knocked an empty bag so it fell to the floor. He bent to pick it up, and his coin dropped. It rolled under the shelf.

Uncle Sami said calmly: "Stop a moment. Take a breath, then do one thing at a time."

Adam stopped. He took a breath. He picked up the bag first. Then he bent and took the coin. Then he asked for the bread. Then he put the change in his closed pocket.

He said: "Four things. Nothing dropped."

Uncle Sami answered: "Being slow is not stopping. Sometimes it is the shortest road."

Adam went out walking fast, but he did not run. He carried the bread with both hands. At the corner he saw a ball rolling into the road, so he stopped at the curb. A bicycle passed, then the ball's owner crossed and took it.

Adam said: "One thing at a time."

He reached home, put the bread on the table, and hung the keys in their place. When he went out to the field, the match had not begun. The two teams were still choosing players.

His friend asked: "Why are you late?"

Adam said: "I was going too fast, so I was late. Then I walked, so I arrived."

The match began. The ball reached him near the goal. He nearly kicked it at once, but he raised his head for a moment, and saw his teammate alone on the other side. He passed the ball to him, and he scored the goal.

The team cheered.

Adam said, laughing: "Even the ball likes one thing at a time!"

And at the end of the day, Adam did not decide to become slow. He decided something better: to be quick when the road is clear, and to pause a moment when a step needs attention.

**[A short match whistle and a cheerful ending]**

## Mixing Direction

- The opening is lively without raising the volume. The pace calms after Uncle Sami's line.
- The bicycle passes with a distant, un-startling sound; the moment teaches stopping at the curb and does not create a dramatic danger.
- No artificial teaching pause is left after the phrase "one thing at a time."

## Parent Guide

> **A ~270-second audio story for a 6–8 child**
>
> An audio-only story — no pictures. Adam rushes and keeps dropping things; a calm baker shows him that doing "one thing at a time" is often the fastest way.
>
> **What it gives your child:** a practical idea about attention over speed — with **no danger** made dramatic and **no lesson** lectured. Adam is never shown as careless or bad; he simply learns to pause when a step needs it.
>
> **How to use it?** A daytime or car listen. It pairs well with a moment your child was rushing — no need to point at the story.
