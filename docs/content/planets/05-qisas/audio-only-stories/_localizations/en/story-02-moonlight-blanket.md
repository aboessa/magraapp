## Full Recording Script

**[Two soft tones over a quiet nighttime-room ambience]**

Layla was in her bed. She fixed her pillow once, then again. She covered her feet, then said: "Mama, the blanket does not reach my dreams."

Her mother sat close by and asked: "And where are your dreams?"

Layla said: "Far away… behind the window."

A light breeze moved the curtain.

**[Soft fabric rustle]**

Moonlight came into the room and reached the edge of the bed.

The mother said: "Look… another blanket has come."

Layla whispered: "A blanket of moonlight?"

The mother said: "Yes. We do not carry it with our hands, but it touches things gently."

The light covered the foot of the bed. Then the pillow. Then Layla's small hand.

Layla said: "It is so light."

The mother said: "Light, so it does not weigh on you, and quiet, so it does not wake your toys."

The cloth bear near the pillow was still. And the little car under the chair was still. Even the clock seemed to speak in a slower voice.

**[A very slow tick for three beats]**

Layla said: "Does the moonlight cover the bird's home too?"

The mother said: "It covers the tree and the nest, then leaves them to sleep."

"And does it cover the cat in the street?"

"It covers her warm spot, then leaves her to curl up."

"And does it reach my dreams?"

The mother arranged the real blanket around Layla's shoulders and said: "Your blanket keeps you warm here, and the moonlight waits at the window, but your dreams know the way to you all by themselves."

Layla breathed slowly. Once… then again.

**[Two quiet breaths, natural and not exaggerated]**

She said: "My dreams found the way."

Her mother kissed her and said: "Goodnight."

The moonlight stayed on the edge of the bed. Nothing moved. There was nothing that had to be done, and no place that had to be reached.

Layla was warm. And the room was quiet. And she closed her eyes.

**[A 1.8s pause, then a faint tone that ends completely]**

## Mixing Direction

- No nighttime outside effects that could suggest danger; no owl, no strong wind, and no cars.
- The music stops before the last three sentences, and only the room layer remains.
- After the ending: a "Goodnight" screen with no recommendations and no autoplay.

## Parent Guide

> **A ~190-second audio story for a 3–5 child**
>
> An audio-only story — no pictures. Your child listens with eyes closed, and the whole story is built to lower the pace toward sleep.
>
> **What is in the story?** Layla feels her blanket does not reach her dreams. Her mother shows her the moonlight as "another blanket," and reassures her that her dreams know the way. There is **no loss, no danger, and no fear** — only warmth, presence, and stillness.
>
> **Why audio only?** Because at bedtime a screen alerts more than it soothes. Listening with eyes closed is itself part of the calm.
>
> **How to use it?** Play it with the screen dark or turned away, and stay near. And if your child asks to hear it again, repeat it — repetition at this age is comfort, not boredom.
