## Characters and Performance

- **Narrator:** cheerful and orderly, leaving short space for the sounds.
- **Yasmin:** loves rhythm and hears the details.
- **Uncle Hatim:** the building caretaker, a genial voice.
- **The residents:** short lines in natural, non-cartoonish voices.

🔴 **Name adaptation:** "ياسمين" → **Yasmin**, "عم حاتم" → **Uncle Hatim**, "كريم" → **Karim**, "السيدة منى" → **Mrs. Mona**, "أبو كريم" → **Karim's father**, substituted locally where needed.

## Full Recording Script

**[An elevator arriving, a single chime, then a door opening]**

Yasmin was waiting for her music lesson, but it was postponed because of the rain. She sat by the building's entrance and began to tap her finger on her bag.

**[Tap… tap tap… tap]**

Uncle Hatim said: "It seems the lesson came to you."

She asked: "Where?"

He said: "Listen to the building."

On the first floor, Mrs. Mona closed her window quietly.

**[A click… then a light latch]**

And on the second floor, a child was skipping with a rope.

**[Two light steps at a steady rhythm]**

And in the nearby kitchen, Karim's father beat the dough with a wooden spoon.

**[Dum tak… dum tak]**

Yasmin said: "Each sound by itself is ordinary."

Uncle Hatim said: "Try to arrange them."

Yasmin tapped the bag's rhythm. She waited for the elevator chime, then added two taps after it. And when the spoon's sound came, she made it the start of a new phrase.

**[The three rhythms enter one after another, not loudly together]**

Karim came down from the second floor and asked: "What are you doing?"

She said: "I'm gathering the building's sounds. But I'm missing a long sound."

Karim thought, then said: "I can hum a note."

**[A short child's hum on one comfortable note]**

Mrs. Mona opened her door and said: "And I have a box of grains. If you shake it gently it becomes like rain."

**[A soft rattle]**

Karim's father came with his spoon, but he did not strike anything. He said: "I'll use an empty box so I don't disturb anyone."

They gathered in the entrance. Uncle Hatim set one rule: "We play for a minute, at a sound heard by those with us and that does not wake those who are resting."

Yasmin began with four taps. The rattle entered. Then the hum. Then the box. And at the end the elevator chime came exactly in its place, as if the elevator had rehearsed with them.

Everyone laughed, but they kept the rhythm.

After a minute Yasmin raised her hand, and the sounds stopped together.

They heard the rain outside.

**[Clear, quiet rain for two seconds]**

Karim said: "The rain completes the piece."

Yasmin said: "No — it is what began it."

She wrote on a sheet: bag, chime, hum, grains, box, rain. Those were not expensive instruments, and the building was not a big hall. But each person listened to the other, entered on time, and left space for the sound that came after.

When a notice arrived from the music teacher setting a new time, Yasmin wrote to her: "Today I rehearsed with a full orchestra."

Then she looked at Uncle Hatim and said: "Next week we need a conductor."

He answered, raising his broom as if it were a conductor's baton: "I'm ready, on condition the music stays gentle."

**[A short reprise of the rhythm, then a laughing ending]**

## Mixing Direction

- This story is tested on a phone speaker: the layers must be distinguishable without loudness or crowding.
- No striking on metal pots or glass. Every household tool is safe and soft.
- The collective stop is an important part; do not extend the music after it except in a short, separate ending.

## Parent Guide

> **A ~250-second audio story for a 6–8 child**
>
> An audio-only story — no pictures. A postponed music lesson becomes a "building orchestra" of ordinary household sounds, made by neighbors listening to one another.
>
> **What it gives your child:** attentive listening and cooperation — each person enters on time and leaves space for the next. There is no danger and no lesson lectured at the end.
>
> **How to use it?** A daytime listen. Afterward you can make your own "home orchestra" from safe, quiet sounds — and practice starting and stopping together.
