## Characters and Performance

- **Narrator:** a warm, realistic adventure, giving directions with words.
- **Youssef:** 12 years old, loves recording but jumps to conclusions.
- **Nora:** 11 years old, precise and notes the clues.
- **Grandpa Mansour:** a retired clockmaker, present and light-hearted.

🔴 **Name adaptation:** "يوسف" → **Youssef**, "نورا" → **Nora**, "الجد منصور" → **Grandpa Mansour**, substituted locally where needed.

## Full Recording Script

**[An old recorder starting, tape hiss, then a distant market]**

Youssef found an audio tape in an old box at his grandfather's. On the case one sentence was written: "The neighborhood map — Thursday." There was no year, and no place names.

He played it with his cousin Nora. They heard a metal bell, then footsteps on a wooden floor, then a vendor calling from far away, then three chimes of a clock.

Youssef said: "This is a treasure map."

Nora said: "Or an ordinary recording of a Thursday."

They asked Grandpa Mansour, and he smiled and said: "I recorded it when I was your age. I was drawing a map from the sounds of places, but I forgot the route. If you find it, the new recorder is yours."

They loaded a digital copy of the recording onto the phone, and went out with him into the street in the daytime. Grandpa set a clear rule: they stay together, cross at the crossing place, and do not enter a shop or building except with his permission and in the company of its owner.

Nora stopped the recording at the first sound.

**[A metal bell: two uneven rings]**

Youssef said: "A bicycle bell."

Nora said: "A bicycle bell is a short ring. This one vibrates after the strike."

They heard it again. Between the two rings came the sound of a door moving.

Grandpa said: "A good observation. Where do we find a bell that a door moves with?"

At the corner of the street was an old shoe-repair shop. Its owner opened the door, and a small bell above it moved.

**[The same two rings, a little clearer]**

Nora put the first mark in her notebook.

The second sound was footsteps on wood, but all the pavements were stone. Youssef said: "Maybe an old house."

Nora said: "Behind it there is the sound of water."

They walked with Grandpa until they reached the public path near the small canal. Over it was a wooden footbridge. The three crossed, and the same sound came: two hollow steps, then a third a little higher at a raised plank.

Youssef said: "Even the plank itself is still here!"

Grandpa said: "Maybe, and maybe the bridge was repaired and a similar difference remained. The sound is a clue, but it does not tell us everything."

After the bridge, a vendor's call came in the recording. They did not understand the words because of the tape hiss, but behind the call they heard small dishes touching.

Youssef said: "A restaurant."

Nora said: "Or a café."

Grandpa remembered that the neighborhood square used to host vendors on Thursday. Today it had licensed food trucks and public benches. They asked one of the old vendors with Grandpa's permission. He said the sound of the dishes might be from a drinks cart that used to stand there, but he was not sure.

Nora wrote: "The place is possible, not confirmed."

Three clock chimes remained. They searched for a public clock, and found none. Youssef pointed to a distant tower, but Grandpa said its sound does not reach the square.

Nora played the last part again. Just before the chimes they heard the rub of a key, then the sound of a wooden drawer.

Youssef looked at his grandfather: "It's not a public clock. It's a clock in your old shop."

They went back to Grandpa's house. In his small room was a tall clock he had repaired years ago. Grandpa wound its key under his supervision, then they waited until the top of the hour.

**[Three warm chimes, with the same spacing between them]**

The chimes matched the recording.

Grandpa Mansour said: "I used to end my round here, at the clock I learned to repair."

Youssef said: "So we found the whole route."

Nora reviewed her notes: "We found the shop, the bridge, and the clock. The square is still a possibility."

Grandpa smiled: "And that is why the map needs two people: one who gets excited, and one who writes down the degree of certainty."

He gave them the new recorder. And the next Thursday they made their own map: the school gate, the pedestrian crossing signal, the square's fountain, and a train whistle from a safe distance.

And on the file they wrote: "The neighborhood map — Thursday — today's day and date." Then they added a note: "Sounds change, so do not let memory say more than what it heard."

**[The four sounds of the new map in a calm order, then the recorder closing]**

## Mixing Direction

- The differences between the bell, the footsteps, and the chimes must be audible on an ordinary phone speaker.
- No real brand or real vendor call is used. The distant speech is deliberately unintelligible.
- The journey is with the grandfather in the daytime, crossing at a designated place; no solo exploration and no unauthorized entry.

## Parent Guide

> **A ~380-second audio story for a 9–12 child**
>
> An audio-only story — no pictures. Two cousins reconstruct an old sound-recording's route through their neighborhood — one excited, one carefully noting how sure they really are.
>
> **What it gives your child:** investigative reasoning paired with intellectual honesty — the "excited one" and the "certainty-recorder" — plus a clear safety frame (with a grown-up, crossing at designated places, no unauthorized entry). No lesson is lectured.
>
> **How to use it?** A daytime listen. The closing note is worth keeping: "Don't let memory say more than what it heard."
