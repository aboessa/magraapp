## The Arc

Salma opens an old box with her grandmother, and finds a lantern that does not work. She thinks a thing that has lost its function is no longer useful. The grandmother does not fix the lantern and does not light it, but shows her a photo and tells of one family night it witnessed. Salma discovers that its value moved from lighting to memory, so she places it beside the photo: **"Not to shine, but to stay."**

---

## 🔴 The governing editorial decision: the lantern does not return to working

| Rejected form | Adopted form |
|---|---|
| They fix the lantern and it shines | 🔴 It stays unlit to the end |
| Its value returns when it does its job | 🔴 Its value is in the story it carries |
| The grandmother explains a lesson about heritage | 🔴 The grandmother tells one tangible night |
| Salma keeps everything old | 🔴 She chooses this thing because it has a documented story |
| Running oil or a flame | 🔴 No fuel · no flame · no attempt to operate it |

> 🔴 **If the lantern lit up at the end, the message would become: the old deserves to stay only if it becomes useful again.**
>
> So it stays as it is. The change happens in **Salma's view**, not in the lantern.

### The two pivotal sentences

1. **"It was not rusty metal. It was a story."** — the moment of understanding on page 9.
2. **"Not to shine, but to stay."** — the quiet decision on page 11.

Each sentence's function is preserved in every language; a literal translation is not required.

## 🔴 No sermon and no glorifying of everything old

The story does not say every old thing is precious, and does not call for hoarding things. The lantern earns its place because:

1. It has a specific family story.
2. There is a photo confirming the story's context.
3. The family chose to display it safely, not to operate it.
4. It became a memory token, not a broken tool in a box.

**And the phrases do not appear:** "We must preserve our heritage" · "The old is better" · "Never throw anything away."

## Parent Guide

> **A story about value moving from function to memory**
>
> The lantern does not work, and no one fixes it. The grandmother shows Salma a photo and tells of a family night it witnessed, so Salma understands that some things stay because they keep a story.

### Why did we not fix the lantern?

If the lantern returned to lighting, the child would tie its value to its old function. Leaving it unlit makes the change one of **understanding**: it is no longer a light tool, but it became a witness to a memory.

### Why does the grandmother not preach?

She does not say: "You must preserve the old." She tells one event: the family's first night in the house. The child sees the photo and hears the event, then reaches the meaning with Salma. **The story precedes the value and does not announce it.**

### This does not mean keeping everything

After reading, you might say:

- "Some things have a story, and some we can say goodbye to."
- "We can keep the story with a photo or a card, not always with the thing."
- "Old dangerous things are looked at by grown-ups only."

🔴 **Do not make the story a reason to pressure a child to give up their comforting object**, nor a reason to keep a whole mess. The choice is a family one and calm.

### The safety of old things

- Do not let the child operate an oil lantern or an old electric device.
- Do not use fire to imitate the story.
- Old metal may carry rust or sharp edges; a grown-up checks it first.
- If the piece is unsafe, a photo of it and its story is enough.

**No questions after the story in the bedtime path.** If the child wants to talk spontaneously, listen without turning the moment into a test.

## Family Activity — daytime

### An object's story card — 15 minutes

1. The grown-up chooses a **safe and clean** object with a family story.
2. Shows an old photo linked to it, if one exists.
3. Tells one event in three sentences: who? where? what happened?
4. The child writes a card: the object's name · the approximate year · the story's owner.
5. Draws or photographs the object with the grown-up.
6. Together they decide: display it, keep it, or is its photo enough?

**The goal:** documenting the memory, not collecting things.

### The alternative with no old object

Choose a family photo, and record under it a sentence in the story-owner's voice. This achieves the same goal with no old object.

🔴 The activity is daytime, and with no fire or batteries or dismantling or repair.
