## The Arc

Nour sees a point of light inside a puddle in the garden, so it looks **as if** a star has lost its way among the pebbles. The wind moves the water and the image vanishes. Her father points to the star in the sky, and shows that what she saw is **the reflection of its light**. When the water settles the image returns, so Nour understands before page ten that the star never left its place.

## 🔴 Fantasy–Science Rule — mandatory application

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| The literary claim | "a lost star" |
| What could be misunderstood | that a real star came down into the garden |
| The truth | stars are very far away; the point in the water is **a reflection of the light** |
| The protecting form | "**it looked as if** a star had lost its way" · "the **image** of the star" · "the **reflection** of its light" |
| The solution | 🔴 the reflection truth appears on pages 7–9, not in an external appendix |

### Forms used and forbidden

| ❌ Forbidden | ✅ Used |
|---|---|
| "A star fell into the puddle" | "It looked as if a star had lost its way among the pebbles" |
| "The star moved with Nour" | "The **image** moved with her" |
| "Her father returned it to the sky" | he points to the star already there in the sky |
| A small star on the ground | a reflected point of light on the water's surface |
| A star with a face or arms | 🔴 light only · no visual personification |

> 🔴 **The title is literary, but the body of the story corrects it inside the experience itself.** We do not leave the child with a metaphorical title and then ask the parent guide alone to fix it.

### The solution through understanding, not rescue

Nour does not pick up the star, and her father does not return it. The understanding comes through three observations:

1. The real star is still in the sky.
2. The image vanishes when the water ripples.
3. The image moves with Nour's viewing angle.

So the difficulty ends on page 9, then three pages of descent and reassurance begin.

## Parent Guide

> **A story fanciful in its title, accurate in its body**
>
> Nour sees a point of light in a puddle, so it looks like a lost star. Inside the story itself she learns that the star is still in the sky, and that what is in the water is a reflection of its light.

### The scientific fact

- Stars are very distant bodies, not small points that could fall into a puddle.
- A calm water surface can reflect the light of a body in the sky.
- A ripple changes or scatters the image; it does not break the star.
- Changing the viewing angle changes the position of the visible image.

That is why the story says: **"it looked as if"**, then uses "the star's image" and "the reflection of its light." It is linked to the [science](../../../03-oloom/README.md) planet, but we do not tie it to an unwritten astronomy episode.

### If your child asks: do stars fall?

An age-appropriate answer:

> "Stars are very far away. What Nour saw was the star's light in the water. And sometimes we see shooting stars in the sky, but they are not stars falling into the garden."

You do not need to explain orbits or sizes now. It is enough to separate the **star** from its **image**.

### Why did the father not go down to the garden?

Because observing from the room is enough, and because the story is before sleep. The solution comes through understanding, not through chasing or grabbing the light. It also keeps the scene from becoming an alerting night adventure.

### Do not correct the imagination harshly

If the child says "the star is visiting the water," you can reply: "A beautiful expression; what we see is its reflected light." We keep the imagination and name the truth in one sentence.

🔴 **No questions after the story in the bedtime path.** The information is there to prevent the wrong concept, not to turn the bed into a science class.

## Family Activity — daytime

### The light's image in the water — 10 minutes

**Materials:** a dark plastic bowl · a little water · a stable home lamp · a towel.

1. The grown-up places the bowl on a stable surface away from electricity.
2. It is filled with a little water, then the lamp's reflection is looked at.
3. The grown-up touches the bowl's edge lightly, so ripples appear.
4. The child waits until the water settles and the reflection returns clear.
5. The child slowly changes their head position, and notices the image's place change.
6. Any drop is wiped at once.

**The one required sentence:** "The lamp did not move; its image is what changed."

### Safety limits

- 🔴 No direct sun, and no mirror facing the sun.
- 🔴 No laser, and no pointing a lamp at the eye.
- The grown-up handles the water and the lamp.
- No glass bowl, and no doing it near an electrical outlet.
- The activity is daytime, then the place is fully dried.
