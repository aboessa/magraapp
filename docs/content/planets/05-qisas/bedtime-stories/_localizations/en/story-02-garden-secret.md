## The Arc

Bashir sees a small plant that grew in a stone wall's crack. He asks: how did a seed reach here? He watches for three days, sees the wind carrying seeds, and understands. Then he sleeps.

## 🔴 The two distinctive decisions

### 1. The grandfather **asks and does not answer**

| ❌ Rejected form | ✅ Form used |
|---|---|
| "His grandfather said: the wind carried it." | "His grandfather said: **watch for three days**." |
| An adult gives the answer | 🔴 **An adult gives a method** |
| The child listens | 🔴 **The child observes and discovers** |

> 🔴 **This is the heart of the story.** Bashir discovers by himself, and the grandfather **gives him a tool, not an answer**.
>
> The story thereby teaches **systematic observation** — the same skill as [A Seed Grows](../../../03-oloom/try-it-at-home/ep-04-seed-grows.md) on the science planet, without a table or an experiment.
>
> **A grandfather who gives the answer** produces a story of information. **A grandfather who gives a method** produces a story about how we know.

### 2. 🔴 Scientific accuracy without teaching

| Item | Ruling |
|---|---|
| The wind carries seeds | ✅ **scientifically true** — no simile needed |
| The plant grows in a crack | ✅ true — a little soil is enough |
| "The seed is asleep" | 🔴 **a simile** — phrased with "as if" |
| Explaining germination | ❌ **not explained** — the story is not a lesson |

**The story does not teach science, but it does not contradict it.** [The rule](../../00-story-page-model.md#10--قاعدة-الخيال-والعلم).

## Full Narration Script for Recording

```
P1.  This is Bashir. He sits in his grandfather's garden.
     He likes to look at things for a long time.
P2.  In the garden is an old stone wall.
     He has passed it a hundred times.
P3.  Today he saw something green in a crack.
     A small plant coming out of the stone.
P4.  Bashir said: there is no soil here.
     So how did a plant grow in stone?
P5.  He asked his grandfather, who did not answer.
     He said: watch it for three days.
P6.  On the first day he saw nothing.
     And on the second, the same.
P7.  And on the third a soft wind blew.
     So Bashir saw something small flying.
P8.  They were seeds. The wind carries them far.
     So some of them fall in the cracks.
P9.  He went back to the plant, and found a new bud.
     And he found another seed in a nearby crack.
P10. His grandfather said: now you know the secret.
     Bashir said: you did not tell me, so I knew by myself.
P11. They sat on the balcony as the evening calmed.
     And the stone wall is still.
P12. And now Bashir sleeps, and the seeds are in their places.
```

**12 pages · 25 sentences · each sentence ≤ 12 words.**

## Parent Guide

> **A ~4:30-minute story about how we know**
>
> Bashir sees a plant that grew in a stone wall's crack with no soil. He asks his grandfather, **who does not answer him**, but says: "Watch it for three days." So he watches, sees on the third day the wind carrying seeds, **and understands by himself**.
>
> **🔴 The grandfather does not give the answer**
> And this is the heart of the story. He gives a **method**, not information.
>
> A grandfather who says "the wind carried it" produces a story of information that is forgotten. A grandfather who says "watch" produces a story about **how we know** — and this one stays.
>
> **And Bashir's last sentence:** "You did not tell me, **so I knew by myself**." Quiet contentment, not pride.
>
> **🔴 And page six shows "nothing happened"**
> Two days with no result. And this is deliberate: **systematic observation includes empty days**, and they are not failure.
>
> The same lesson as the episode [A Seed Grows](../../../03-oloom/try-it-at-home/ep-04-seed-grows.md): "And maybe a seed does not grow, and that is a result too."
>
> **Accurate scientifically without teaching**
> The wind **really does carry seeds**, and the plant **grows in a little soil inside the cracks**. The story is true, but it **does not explain** germination — it is not a lesson.
>
> **Two new words:** **crack** and **bud**. In context, and the image explains them. **Do not explain them and do not quiz them.**
>
> **🔴 No questions and no game after the story** and no "next story."
>
> **Try this sentence with them**
> When they ask you about something observable, say: "**I don't know. Let's watch.**" — instead of answering at once. Once a week is enough, and the effect is large.
>
> **And look for a plant in a crack**
> In a wall, a pavement, or a pot. You will find one — and this turns the story into something they saw with their own eyes.

## Family Activity — daytime

> 🔴 **For the daytime, not after the story.**
>
> **Three-day observation — 5 minutes each day**
>
> 1. Choose **one thing** that changes slowly: a plant in a crack · a shadow patch on a wall · a tree branch.
> 2. Each day, **a minute of looking** then a small drawing or a sentence.
> 3. 🔴 **Explain nothing** on the first two days.
> 4. On the third day ask: "**What changed?**"
> 5. And if nothing changed: "**And that is a result too.**"
>
> **The fifth step is more important than the fourth.** A day with no change is **data**, not emptiness.
>
> **A hunt for stubborn plants — 15 minutes**
>
> Walk in the neighborhood and look for plants that grew in strange places: a pavement crack · the edge of a wall · an abandoned pot. Photograph or draw each one.
>
> Ask at each one: "**Where did its seed come from?**" — and the answer is always: something carried it. Wind, or a bird, or water.
>
> 🔴 **By looking only. No picking and no touching an unknown plant. And wash hands after the walk.**
