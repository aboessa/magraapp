## The Arc

A small bird flies a little farther each time; a soft wind blows, so he returns to his nest and sleeps.

🔴 **No loss and no fear.** The wind is a **small change**, not a danger. The nest is **visible on every page** — the child always knows home is near.

### Page Notes

**Page 3 — the governing visual rule.** The nest shrinks but never leaves. A three-year-old reads the **absence** of home as loss.

> 🔴 **Page 4 is the peak, and it is a soft wind.**
>
> **No storm, no fall, no shouting.** Fluff **is not afraid** — he tilts a little. The SFX is **≤300ms and soft**, not a gusting wind.
>
> A stronger peak than this breaks the 3–5 age rules.

**Page 5 — "He knew the way."** 🔴 Not "got lost," not "searched." **The bird always knows his way.**

**Page 7 — motion stops here**, one page before the last. 🔴 And **"a soft moon"** is a required visible light source on every night page.

**Page 8 — stillness with no event.** 🔴 **No event, no question, no goodbye from a character.** One stillness-description sentence, then a "Goodnight" screen. 🔴 **Music ends before this page**, and the only sound is the narrator.

## Parent Guide

> **A 2:40-minute story for a 3–5 child**
>
> Short on purpose. A three-year-old falls asleep in minutes, and a longer story ends while they are still awake.
>
> **What is in the story?**
> A small bird flies a little farther, a soft wind blows, and he returns to his nest and sleeps. **No loss, no danger, no fear.**
>
> **🔴 Three decisions you might notice**
>
> **First: the nest is visible on every page.** Even while Fluff is far, his home is in the background. A three-year-old reads the **absence** of home as loss, so we kept it in frame at all times.
>
> **Second: his mother is present from the first page** and never leaves. No character disappears in this series at all.
>
> **Third: Fluff "knew the way"** — he did not get lost and did not search. The bird knows his home, and the story never draws the child into the anxiety of getting back.
>
> **🔴 No question and no game after the story**
> Bedtime is not test time. No questions will appear, no game, and no "next story." The story ends with "Goodnight" **and everything ends**.
>
> The linked game is available **during the day** if they want it.
>
> **The screen dims gradually**
> The first page is brighter than the last. On the last two pages **the camera motion stops completely**. This is deliberate: motion alerts more than light does.
>
> **A sleep timer**
> Available at 10, 20, or 30 minutes — in case your child falls asleep with the device open.
>
> **How to read it with them?**
> Sit beside them and let them turn the pages themselves if they wish — **manual page-turning is allowed** even in "read to me" mode, and the audio follows along.
>
> And if they want a page again, repeat it. **Repetition at this age is learning, not boredom.**

## Family Activity — daytime, not at night

> 🔴 **This activity is for the daytime.** Do not do it after the bedtime story.
>
> **Animal homes — 10 minutes**
>
> 1. Ask: "Where does the bird sleep?" — the nest.
> 2. Then: "And where does the fish sleep? The cat? The ant?"
> 3. Draw together one home for an animal they love.
> 4. Ask: "And you? Where do you sleep?" — and finish with their own room.
>
> **Finishing with their room is the activity.** It links the story to their home, so their bed becomes **their nest**.
>
> **No scientific names and no details.** The goal is the connection, not the fact.
