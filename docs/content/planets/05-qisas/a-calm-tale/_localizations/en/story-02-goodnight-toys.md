## The Arc

Reem is playing; her mother reminds her it is time to sleep, so she tidies her toys one by one, says "goodnight" to each, and then sleeps.

## 🔴 The distinctive decision: the story is a **routine**, not an event

| ❌ Rejected form | ✅ Form used |
|---|---|
| Reem refuses to sleep and is persuaded | Reem **moves calmly** |
| "It's time for bed!" firmly | "The toys want to sleep" |
| A struggle then surrender | 🔴 **a familiar sequence of steps** |

> 🔴 **This story's job is to be imitable.** The child watches it every night, so tidying becomes a ritual.
>
> **That is why there is no struggle in it.** Showing a child who refuses to sleep — even if it ends with acceptance — gives the child a **script for refusal** to use. The story shows the **action directly**, with no resistance.
>
> **And the small peak is not a struggle** — it is **one toy that did not find its place** — a small practical problem, solved on the next page.

## Parent Guide

> **This story is a routine tool, not a tale**
> Its job is to be watched **every night** until tidying becomes a ritual. The repetition is the point.
>
> **🔴 Why does Reem not refuse to sleep?**
> On purpose. Showing a child who refuses to sleep — **even ending in acceptance** — hands your child a **script for refusal** to use with you. The story shows the action directly, with no resistance.
>
> **And the only difficulty is practical:** the ball did not find its place, so it went into the basket. A problem solved, not a struggle managed.
>
> **The phrase we hope you'll use**
> "**The toys want to sleep.**" — instead of "It's bedtime." Moving the request from a command to an invitation greatly reduces resistance at this age.
>
> **Tidy together and name each toy**
> Reem said "goodnight" to each toy. This makes tidying a **final game** rather than a chore, and it lengthens the ritual without excitement.
>
> **Her mother in the final shot**
> Deliberate. A three-year-old sleeps reassured when they see an adult in the last frame.
>
> **🔴 No questions and no game after the story**, and no "next story." It ends with "Goodnight" and everything ends.
>
> **If they don't tidy?** Do not turn the story into a means of compulsion. Watch it, and you tidy one toy and they tidy one. **Sharing builds the habit; compelling breaks the story.**

## Family Activity — daytime

> 🔴 **For the daytime, not after the story.**
>
> **Places for toys — 15 minutes**
>
> 1. Choose a **fixed place** for each type: a shelf for the bears · a basket for the balls · a box for the blocks.
> 2. Stick a **picture** on each place — the child does not read, and the picture is enough.
> 3. Try tidying once in the daytime with no rush.
> 4. Count: how many toys found their place?
>
> **The picture on the place is the activity.** A place the child knows without asking gets tidied; the unknown gets left.
>
> **This links to the episode** [The Plant's Responsibility](../../../04-qiyam/hekaya-wa-hikma/ep-04-plant-responsibility.md): **a system that reminds, not a memory that is strained** — and here the system is a picture on the basket.
