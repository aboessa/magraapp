## The Arc

A cold night. Noor feels cold. Her grandmother brings a blanket. The cat finds its place too. So Noor sleeps warm.

## 🔴 The distinctive decision: a story about **sensory safety**

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| The subject | 🔴 **physical warmth**, not abstract emotion |
| The reason | a 3–5 child understands the **sensation** before the concept |
| The tool | a blanket · a hug · a nearby cat — **things they can touch** |
| What it does not do | 🔴 **it does not explain love or name it** |

> 🔴 **The story does not say "I love you" or "the family protects you."** It shows **cold then warmth**, so the child understands safety **with their body**.
>
> Abstract concepts *(love, safety, protection)* do not land at this age. The sensation lands.
>
> **And the title "Warm Hugs" is for the grown-up**; the child hears a blanket, a hug, and a cat.

### 🔴 And the cold is not fear

| ❌ Forbidden | ✅ Used |
|---|---|
| Noor shivers and cries | "Noor said: I feel cold." |
| "The night was scary" | "The night was cold." |
| A wind whistling hard | 🔴 a wind SFX **≤300ms soft** |
| A window being knocked | 🔴 **does not exist** |

**Cold is a sensation solved by a blanket.** Had it become fear, it would need a psychological resolution, and that is above the series' age.

## Parent Guide

> **A 2:45-minute story about warmth, not about love**
>
> A cold night. Noor feels cold. She does not find her blanket, so her grandmother brings another. The cat sits close by. So she sleeps warm.
>
> **🔴 Why does the story not say "I love you"?**
> On purpose. Abstract concepts — love, safety, protection — **do not land** for a four-year-old. But **the sensation lands**: a blanket, a hug, and a nearby cat.
>
> The story shows **cold then warmth**, so your child understands safety **with their body**, not by a definition.
>
> **And the colors carry the meaning**
> From the second page to the sixth, the colors shift from cool to warm. Your child will feel the difference without noticing it, and without one word about love.
>
> **🔴 And the cold is not fear**
> Noor does not shiver and does not cry, and there is no strong wind sound and no knocking on the window. Cold is **a sensation solved by a blanket**. Had it become fear, it would need a psychological resolution, and that is above this series' age.
>
> **And the grandmother is on every page**
> She does not leave for a moment. The reassurance in this series is **presence**, not a clever solution.
>
> **🔴 No questions and no game after the story** and no "next story."
>
> **How to use it?**
> Read it **while you are under one blanket with them**. The story is about a sensation, and a sensation is passed by the body, not the screen. This is the most useful way for this story in particular.
>
> **If your child is afraid of the night?**
> This story suits them, because it **does not name fear** and does not open its door. It gives them warmth, presence, and stillness. And if the fear persists for a while, consult a specialist — the story is not a treatment.

## Family Activity — daytime

> 🔴 **For the daytime, not after the story.**
>
> **Warm things and cold things — 10 minutes**
>
> 1. Gather safe things to touch: a blanket · a towel · a stone · a metal spoon · wool.
> 2. The child touches each one and says: **warm or cold?**
> 3. Order them from warmest to coldest.
> 4. Ask: "Which one would you like to have with you in bed?"
>
> 🔴 **Nothing hot and nothing chilled in the fridge. Room temperature only.** And nothing smaller than their fist.
>
> **The fourth question is the activity.** Their choice becomes **their reassuring thing** — and that is useful every night.
>
> **This links to the episode** [The Five Senses](../../../03-oloom/discover-your-body/ep-02-five-senses.md): **the skin detects texture and temperature**.
