## 🔴 Fantasy–Science Rule — full application

This story is **the clearest case of conflict** on the planet, and its resolution is fully documented.

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| The fictional claim | "The moon sleeps" |
| The scientific fact | [Moon phases](../../../03-oloom/science-in-a-minute/ep-03-moon-phases.md): the moon **does not change**; what we see of it does |
| The conflict | "sleeps" could be understood as it actually vanishing or dimming |
| 🔴 **The adopted solution** | **an explicit simile form in every place** + a line in the parent guide |

### Forms used and forbidden

| ❌ Forbidden | ✅ Used |
|---|---|
| "The moon slept." | "**It is as if** the moon sleeps." |
| "The moon closed its eyes." | "The moon is **calm** tonight." |
| "The moon is tired." | "The moon **looks** still." |
| "The moon went to sleep." | "And a cloud **covers** it a little." |
| The moon with a face or eyes | 🔴 **a moon with no face** |

> 🔴 **"As if" is not a linguistic flourish but an epistemic guarantee.**
>
> A three-year-old accepts the simile with no trouble and understands that the moon does not actually sleep. But **the declarative statement** produces a wrong concept that a science episode will contradict years later, hard to correct.
>
> **And a moon with a face and eyes is entirely rejected** — because it produces the wrong concept **visually** no matter what the text says. The same rule that rejected drawing the moon as a crescent missing part of its body in [the phases episode](../../../03-oloom/science-in-a-minute/ep-03-moon-phases.md).

### 🔴 The intended benefit

The story is an **entryway** to a science planet, not an obstacle before it:

| Age | The experience |
|---|---|
| 3–5 | hears "as if the moon sleeps" and links the moon to stillness |
| 9–12 | watches [Moon phases](../../../03-oloom/science-in-a-minute/ep-03-moon-phases.md) and learns the real reason |

**And the story does not contradict the episode, because it declared nothing.**

## The Arc

Adam does not want to sleep. He looks out of his window and sees the moon. His father sits with him. A cloud covers the moon a little, then passes. So Adam grows calm and sleeps.

🔴 **The cloud is the small peak** — and it is not fear. It passes on the next page, and the moon **remains partly visible behind it**.

## Full Narration Script for Recording

```
1.  This is Adam. He does not want to sleep.
2.  His father sat with him by the window.
3.  Adam said: the moon is awake too.
4.  A cloud passed. It covered the moon a little.
5.  Then it went by. The moon was still there.
6.  His father said: it is as if the moon sleeps.
```

## Parent Guide

> **A 2:30-minute story about calm, not about the moon**
>
> Adam does not want to sleep. His father sits with him, they look at the moon, a cloud passes and then goes by, and Adam grows calm.
>
> **🔴 A scientific note — please read it**
>
> The story says: "**as if** the moon sleeps" — as a simile, not a statement.
>
> **And the scientific fact:** the moon **does not change and does not sleep**. What changes is **the lit part we see of it**, according to its position relative to the earth and the sun. This is the subject of the episode [Moon phases](../../../03-oloom/science-in-a-minute/ep-03-moon-phases.md) on the science planet for ages 9–12.
>
> **Why mention this now?**
> Because your child will hear this story at four and watch that episode at ten. We wanted the story to be an **entryway**, not an obstacle: it **declared** nothing, it made a comparison.
>
> **And notice that the moon in the pictures has no face and no eyes.** Had we drawn it with a sleeping face, we would have produced the wrong concept **visually** no matter what the text says.
>
> **If your child asks "Does the moon sleep?"**
> An age-appropriate answer: "**No. But it looks calm at night, like us.**" — deny the word and affirm the feeling. This keeps both accuracy and warmth.
>
> **🔴 The moon is absent from no page**
> Even behind the cloud, it is seen partly. A three-year-old reads absence as **loss**, and the moon's absence for a moment produces anxiety, not calm.
>
> **And his father sits with him**
> From the second page to the last. The reassurance in this series comes from an **adult's presence**, not from a clever solution.
>
> **🔴 No questions and no game after the story** and no "next story."
>
> **If your child often refuses to sleep?**
> Sit by the window for two minutes and actually look at the moon. **Looking at the moon is completely safe** — and a short, repeated ritual is more useful than persuasion.

## Family Activity — nighttime allowed as an exception

> ⚠️ **This is the only activity allowed at night on the planet**, because it is **screen-free and motion-free**.
>
> **Two minutes at the window**
>
> 1. Before sleep, open the curtain and look at the sky for **two minutes only**.
> 2. Ask: "Do you see the moon tonight?"
> 3. If it is absent: "It will come back. It is always there."
> 4. Close the curtain and say "Goodnight."
>
> 🔴 **Two minutes, no more. No screen. No questions. No information.**
>
> **The third sentence matters:** "It is always there" — it keeps the same story rule and prevents reading absence as loss.
>
> **A daytime activity — 10 minutes**
>
> Draw the moon on three different nights *(crescent · half · full)*, and notice the difference. **No explanation of why** — observation is enough at this age, and the reason comes later on the science planet.
