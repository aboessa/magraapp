## The Question It Answers

> **"I said yes a month ago, and I did not know. So do I still owe anything?"**

## Learning Objective

**One objective:** the child plans **how to keep a promise he made**, in steps with a time and a responsible party.

The criterion: facing a commitment he made earlier, he writes **at least three items**, each item with **an action and a time**, and among them at least one item in which **a third party is notified early**, in 3 of 4 real commitments, without help.

🔴 **The goal is not "he keeps his promise."** Keeping is a result, and the goal is **the work that makes keeping possible.** And a child who says "I will come" then sleeps **has promised twice and planned zero times.**

## 🔴 The Decision the Episode Is Built On: The Item That Closes Your Own Door

In Bashar's four-item plan, **the third item is the one that costs**: to tell Adel **tonight**, not Saturday morning.

| If he told him Saturday morning | If he told him tonight |
|---|---|
| The possibility stays open: the fair might be cancelled, someone might get sick | 🔴 **The door is closed** |
| And the team finds no substitute in an hour | 🔴 **The team finds a substitute in a night** |
| And he stays **able to change his mind** | 🔴 **He cannot** |

> 🔴 **A promise that stays retractable at the last moment is not a promise, but a deferred intention.**
>
> And the practical difference between an intention and a plan is exactly this item: **a plan notifies whoever needs to know, early, so it binds its owner.**
>
> Hence the episode has two explicit sentences: "**And the third item is the one that cost him.**" and "**Because it closes his own door.**"

## 🔴 And the Third Solution Was Computed and Rejected

In the third scene Bashar thinks of going for two hours and returning, **then computes it**: the road is forty minutes, and the match starts with a missing player. **So there is no third solution.**

| Why this is a mandatory segment |
|---|
| 🔴 The 9–12 audience **looks for the third solution immediately** — it is the first thing they think of |
| And if the episode did not compute it **the viewer would think the writer did not see it** |
| 🔴 And a dilemma whose exits are not closed **is read as artificial** |

**And the computation is by numbers, not by refusal:** forty minutes, and two hours, and a missing player. **Refusal by numbers convinces, refusal by saying does not.**

## 🔴 The Dilemma — the Four Conditions

| # | The condition | Its fulfillment in this episode |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | **Each side has a named cost** | **The table:** the team plays with four or does not play, and Adel let in the one who didn't come, and a once-a-year round passes · **The match:** Nour and Hasan face a numbering they don't know, a one-day fair not repeated, and a promise broken without its owner knowing |
| 2 | **The cost is actually paid** | 🔴 **The team played and won without him** · Adel cooled for three days · **and nothing happened at the table worth telling** |
| 3 | **No side is ugly** | 🔴 The tournament is **a legitimate right**, a once-a-year round, **and Adel let him in with trust** |
| 4 | **No adult solves it** | 🔴 No father says "the promise matters more" · Bashar computes and writes alone |

🔴 **The cost is not catastrophic:** no exclusion from the team, no punishment, no feud. **A match missed, and three days' coolness.**

### 🔴 And the harshest cost is that nothing happened

"**And nothing happened at the table worth telling. Four hours of arranging and exchanging and ordinary questions.**"

| If something had happened | What it produces |
|---|---|
| A sudden crowd, or an important visitor, or a crisis he saved | 🔴 The episode says: **keeping is rewarded with an event** |
| 🔴 **Four ordinary hours** | Keeping **needs no reward to be keeping** |

> **And this is the editorially hardest thing in this episode**, and the most honest in it: most promises we keep **have nothing happen in them.** And a child who learns that keeping brings an event **stops keeping the time nothing happens.**

## Parent Guide

> **What is your son or daughter learning?**
> To **plan** for a promise he made — not to remember it when its time comes.
>
> **🔴 And the difference between an intention and a plan is one item**
> Bashar wrote four items, and the third is the one that cost him: **to tell Adel tonight, not Saturday morning.**
>
> If he told him Saturday morning the possibility would stay open — the fair might be cancelled, someone might get sick — **and he would stay able to change his mind.** And telling him tonight **closed his own door.**
>
> And a promise that stays retractable at the last moment **is not a promise but a deferred intention.**
>
> **🔴 The question you ask at home**
> Don't ask "will you keep your promise?" — the answer is always yes.
>
> Ask: "**Which item closes your door?**" or simpler: "**Who needs to know now?**"
>
> **And the third solution was computed and rejected**
> Bashar thought of going for two hours and returning, **then computed it by numbers**: forty minutes road, two hours match, and a missing player.
>
> On purpose. Your child will look for the third solution immediately — and that is **intelligence, not evasion.** Teach him to **compute it by numbers** rather than reject it by principle: sometimes a third solution really exists, and the computation is what tells the difference.
>
> **🔴 And the most honest thing in the episode: nothing happened**
> "**And nothing happened at the table worth telling. Four hours of arranging and exchanging and ordinary questions.**"
>
> **And his team played with a new fifth and won.**
>
> On purpose. Most promises we keep **have nothing happen in them**, and the world **does not wait for us.** And a child who learns that keeping brings an event or proves he is indispensable **stops keeping the first time nothing happens.**
>
> **And no one thanked him**
> A plan is work that **is not seen.** Do not expect your child to be thanked for it, **and you thank him for the item, not the result**: "I saw that you told him early."
>
> **🔴 The red line**
> - 🔴 **Travel is always coordinated with an adult.** A child does not plan a route alone, nor compute travel between two places without an adult.
> - No early leaving from home without his family's knowledge.
> - The item "I arrive at eight-fifteen" **is arranged with an adult**, not with the clock alone.
>
> **How do you help him?**
> - Hang **the item sheet**, not a list of promises. The items have **an action and a time.**
> - If he has to break one: the rule is **"notify early and give an alternative"** — as Bashar said: "Look for a fifth from tonight."
> - If someone reproaches him: don't convince him he is right. "I know." is a sufficient sentence — **and nothing after it.**

## Family Activity

> **The Item Sheet — 20 minutes**
>
> 1. Choose **a real commitment** on one of the family this week.
> 2. Write it **at least three items**, each with **an action and a time**: "I do such-and-such, tonight / Tuesday / before seven."
> 3. 🔴 **Identify the item that closes the door**: who needs to know now? Circle it.
> 4. 🔴 **Do that item in the same session** — the call or message is sent now, not tomorrow.
> 5. Hang the sheet, and after a week review: **which item was done? And which was an intention in the form of an item?**
> 6. 🔴 **You start with a commitment on you**, in front of your children, and do the closing item yourself.
>
> **The third and fourth items are the activity.** The difference between a plan and an intention is learned **by actually closing one door**, not by explaining it.
>
> **The Classification Game — 5 minutes**
>
> One of you says a sentence, and the rest say **"plan" or "intention."** Examples: "I will try to remember" · "I send the message now" · "I set an alarm and see" · "I tell my grandmother tonight that I won't come."
>
> 🔴 **No planning travel without an adult · no early leaving without family's knowledge · and every item with a route is arranged with an adult.**
>
> **A harder variation (age 12):** he writes the items of a commitment with **a date a month away**, and gives each item **a date, not the word "before the appointment".**
>
> **A short conversation:** "When did I say 'I will do it' and not plan?" — 🔴 **you answer first with a real situation.**
