## The Question It Answers

> **"Everyone re-sent it, so what harm if I do like them?"**

## Learning Objective

**One objective:** the child applies **the same rules of respect on the screen** that he applies face to face.

The criterion: facing a message or picture or comment about a person, he checks it with the question "**Would I say this to his face?**" so he classifies correctly in 5 of 6 cases, **and writes — or refrains from writing — what matches his answer**, in 3 of 4 real situations.

🔴 **The goal is not "he doesn't bully."** More than half of what spreads among 9–12 children has no bullying intent: **re-sending without thinking**, and a laugh, and a line. And the goal is that the child has **one check that precedes sending.**

## 🔴 The Test the Episode Is Built On

| ❌ Rules that don't work | ✅ The test used |
|---|---|
| "Don't post what hurts" — who decides? | 🔴 "**Would I say this to his face?**" |
| "Think before you send" — think about what? | An answer of **yes or no** in a second |
| "The internet doesn't forget" — a threat, not a criterion | A criterion **he already has** from real life |
| A list of prohibitions to memorize | 🔴 **One question that moves him to a situation we didn't write** |

> 🔴 **The value of this test is that it teaches the child nothing new.** He **knows** what he says to a face and what he doesn't — he learned it in the playground and at home before he held a device.
>
> **And the problem is that the screen cancelled the test**: no face, no voice, no moment of silence after the word. And the episode **does not build new digital ethics, but reconnects the screen to what he knows.**

## 🔴 The Dilemma — the Four Conditions

| # | The condition | Its fulfillment in this episode |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | **Each side has a named cost** | **To write:** they say she is sensitive or she is teaching people, and Tala her friend is the one who sent it, and her place in the group changes · **To stay silent:** the picture continues its path, and Ziad reads thirty-one names none of which wrote anything, **and she stays knowing the frame is from her folder** |
| 2 | **The cost is actually paid** | 🔴 Two laughing marks on her line · "the group became a school" · Tala **does not reply for four days** · 🔴 **and the picture is not deleted from the devices it reached** |
| 3 | **No side is ugly** | 🔴 **Tala is not evil**, nor the fourteen. No one **intended** harm, and the episode says so by its build: a crop and a line and a laugh |
| 4 | **No adult solves it** | 🔴 No teacher and no father appears · and no scene where anyone is reported · Reem writes herself |

🔴 **The cost is not catastrophic:** no exclusion from the group, no losing a friend forever, no punishment, no investigation scene. **A place changes, and four days of silence.**

## 🔴 And Three Strict Editorial Decisions

### 1. The picture is not shown

| ❌ Forbidden | ✅ Used |
|---|---|
| The picture visible | 🔴 **The back of the screen** or light on a face |
| The picture blurred | 🔴 It does not appear at all — blurring **points to it** |
| The line written under it readable | 🔴 "**And she wrote a line under it**" — and we don't know what |

**Why:** showing it — even blurred — **makes the episode do the thing it discusses.** And it turns Ziad from a person into a picture.

### 2. Deletion does not fix everything, and the episode says so

"**And the picture is not deleted from the devices it reached. This is not fixed by deleting.**"

| If the episode said deletion is a solution | What it produces |
|---|---|
| 🔴 The child thinks sending is **retrievable** | so he sends first and deletes later |
| ✅ **Deletion reduces and does not erase** | so the **check before sending** becomes the only useful thing |

🔴 **And this is not a threat but a description.** We do not say "the internet doesn't forget so beware," but say what happened: it reached devices, and is not deleted from them.

### 3. No app interface and no safety settings

The episode **does not teach settings**, does not show a platform, and does not name an app. A double reason: **settings change so the episode becomes old**, and **a brand loses the episode its neutrality and its translation.**

## Parent Guide

> **What is your son or daughter learning?**
> That his rules on the screen are his rules to the face — **and that he has one question to check with.**
>
> **🔴 The question**
> "**Would I say this to his face?**"
>
> And its value is that it **teaches nothing new.** Your child **knows** what he says to a face and what he doesn't — he learned it in the playground before he held a device. And the problem is that the screen **cancelled the test**: no face, no voice, no silence after the word.
>
> **And the episode does not teach new digital ethics, but reconnects the screen to what he already knows.**
>
> **🔴 And it is not about bullying**
> **No one in the episode intended harm.** Tala cropped a picture and wrote a line. And fourteen re-sent it in twenty minutes. **Re-sending without thinking** is what spreads most among children of this age, and it is what the episode fixes.
>
> **So do not ask your child "do you bully?"** — the answer is always no, and it is honest. Ask him: "**Did you re-send anything today? And to whom?**"
>
> **🔴 And the most important line in the episode**
> "**And the picture is not deleted from the devices it reached. This is not fixed by deleting.**"
>
> Say it to your child in this form, **not in the form of a threat** *("the internet doesn't forget")*. The difference is that the first is **a description** that makes the check before sending the useful thing, and the second is **fear** that produces concealment.
>
> **And three writings in three places**
> 1. **In the group** — because silence in the place of the harm is read as agreement.
> 2. **To Tala alone** — because confronting a friend in front of thirty-one is **a punishment, not a repair.** And she began with "**You are my friend**" then asked for a specific action.
> 3. **To Ziad** — because he is the one whose matter it is and no one asked him.
>
> 🔴 **And the second is the one grown-ups also get wrong.** Objecting publicly to someone you love produces defense, not correction.
>
> **And Reem did not promise what she cannot do**
> "I erased the frame" and "locked the sharing" — **two actions in her hand.** And she did not tell Ziad "I'll make them delete it."
>
> **And the cost was paid**
> Two laughing marks on her line. "The group became a school." Tala did not reply for four days. **And seven wrote nothing.**
>
> **And nothing else in the group changed.** No reversal, no public apology, no admission she was right. **What changed: one folder.**
>
> **🔴 And this episode's boundary**
> The episode **does not teach safety settings** and does not name an app. Settings change, the question does not.
>
> **And another boundary:** this is general human ethics. If you want your child a basis from a particular religious or cultural authority, that is **outside this series**, and is sought from its source.
>
> **How do you help him?**
> - Make the question **a home sentence**, and say it yourself about yourself before you send something.
> - 🔴 **If he tells you something that reached him, do not punish him for telling you.** The first time you punish, the telling ends.
> - Do not ask him for the names of those who sent. **Ask him what he himself did.**
> - If someone mocks him for objecting: "They said you are sensitive. And you wrote what you say to the face. Both are true."

## Family Activity

> **One Question Before Sending — 20 minutes**
>
> 1. Write the question on a sheet and hang it: "**Would I say it to his face?**"
> 2. Gather **six real cases** from your week — messages and posts and comments — **without names.**
> 3. Classify each case: **"I say it" or "I don't say it".**
> 4. 🔴 **Look for a case whose answer is "I say it"** that involves **a disagreement or critique** — because the goal is not silence but consistency.
> 5. 🔴 **You start with a case from your own messages**, and you are the parent.
> 6. Add one home rule: **"What reaches me I don't re-send before I ask the question."**
>
> **The fourth and fifth items are the activity.** The fourth prevents your child from understanding digital ethics as silence. And the fifth makes the rule apply to everyone.
>
> **The Three Places Game — 10 minutes**
>
> Take one situation, and ask: **what is said in the group? and what is said to the person alone? and what is said to the one whose matter it is?** And notice the three are **different, and all honest.**
>
> 🔴 **No mentioning real names · no showing any embarrassing picture of anyone · and no opening the children's devices and reading them in front of the family.**
>
> **A harder variation (age 12):** he writes **three forms** of the same objection: to the group, to the sender alone, and to the one whose matter it is. And he reads them, and asks himself: which would harm with no benefit?
>
> **A short conversation:** "When did I send something and regret it?" — 🔴 **you answer first with a real situation.**
