## The Question It Answers

> **"If I give everyone the same as everyone else, I have been fair — haven't I?"**

## Learning Objective

**One objective:** the child distinguishes between **people being treated the same** and **people being treated fairly.**

The criterion: in 4 distribution cases, in 3 of them he identifies which distribution is **equal** and which is **fair**, **and names the criterion** he built his distribution on *(need, or effort, or reaching the same result)*, without help.

## 🔴 The Dilemma — the Four Conditions

| # | The condition | Its fulfillment in this episode |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | **Each side has a named cost** | **Equality:** Hala and Yusuf hand in incomplete pages · **Need:** nine students lose minutes, Mazen among them, and some think he favored those he knows |
| 2 | **The cost is actually paid** | 🔴 Karim keeps his distance for a week · Mazen's page is shorter than he wanted · **and no one apologizes or applauds** |
| 3 | **No side is ugly** | Distributing equally is a **respected position**, and it is what most schools do, and it looks fair on paper |
| 4 | **No adult solves it** | 🔴 No teacher appears · Mazen was asked to distribute, then left to it |

🔴 **The cost is not catastrophic:** no one is expelled, no one loses a friend forever, no punishment. Karim **keeps his distance** and does not cut off.

## Parent Guide

> **What is your son or daughter learning?**
> The difference between **everyone taking the same as others** and **each one taking what he needs to reach.**
>
> **A dilemma, not a situation**
> There is no free answer in this episode. Mazen distributed by need, **so he paid a cost**: his friend kept his distance for a week, his page came shorter than he wanted, and two said he favored those he knows. **And no one apologized to him in the end, and no one applauded.**
>
> On purpose. Content that ends with everyone admitting the child was right **teaches that being right is costless** — which is a lie he discovers in the first real situation, so he concludes that what he learned was for the screen.
>
> **🔴 And the sentence we want as a tool, not a slogan**
> "**I accept that they be angry with me, and I do not accept that we call equality fairness while they do not finish.**"
>
> The structure is what matters: "**I accept that... and I do not accept that...**" — and its first half **before** the second. Acknowledging the cost before the stand is what makes the stand a decision.
>
> **Try it at home** in a small matter: "I accept that we be ten minutes late, and I do not accept that we go out without finishing this."
>
> **🔴 And half the episode is one line**
> "**He wrote at the bottom of the sheet the reason for each number.**"
>
> Fairness whose criterion is not explained **is read as arbitrariness.** And this applies at home literally: when you give one of your children something you do not give the other, **the difference between a decision and a whim is that you say the criterion out loud.**
>
> **And Karim is right**
> "**This does not give me back the minutes.**" And Mazen **did not answer** — because there is no answer.
>
> If your child says such a sentence to you, **do not convince him and do not silence him.** Say: "True. It does not." — acknowledging his right is more useful than convincing him he has none.
>
> **🔴 And what the episode deliberately avoided**
> **No pity for Hala and Yusuf.** Their circumstance is mentioned as a fact: a device broke, a device shared. **And no reference to money nor parents' profession nor a home.**
>
> Because pity **stereotypes**: it turns a classmate into a case. And the goal is for your child to understand **a criterion**, not to pity anyone.
>
> **How do you help him?**
> - Ask him: "**What is the criterion?**" in every home distribution — not "is this fair?".
> - Give him a case where **equality** is the right one, so "fairness" does not turn into always favoring.
> - If he says "this is unfair," ask: "unfair by what criterion?" — the question trains, the defense silences.

## Family Activity

> **The Criterion Sheet — 20 minutes**
>
> 1. Choose something real to be distributed at home this week: **screen time, or seat arrangement, or chores.**
> 2. Ask the child to propose a distribution, **and write at the bottom of the sheet the reason for each number.**
> 3. 🔴 **The first condition: whoever distributes cuts from his own share.**
> 4. Hang the sheet where everyone sees it, **with the reasons, not the numbers alone.**
> 5. Allow objection — **to the criterion, not the number**: "your reason is not correct because..."
> 6. After a week, sit and review: **which criterion worked? And which did not?**
>
> **The fifth item is the whole activity.** Objecting to the criterion trains thinking, objecting to the number trains bargaining.
>
> **A harder variation (age 12):** give him a case where **equality** is the fairness, and ask him to justify **why it is not distributed by need here.**
>
> **A short conversation:** "When did I treat you the same as your sibling and it was wrong?" — 🔴 **you answer first with a real situation.** Admitting a wrong criterion from the grown-up is the strongest part of the activity.
