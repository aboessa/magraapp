## The Question It Answers

> **"I want it now, so why wait?"**

## Learning Objective

**One objective:** the child waits his turn, **and says with words that he is waiting.**

The criterion: he says the sentence "I am waiting for my turn" — or its equivalent — in 3 of 4 real waiting situations, with one hint at most, and stays in place until his turn comes.

🔴 **"And says with words" is half the goal, not its decoration.** A three-year-old who waits silently bursts after half a minute, because waiting for him is **emptiness**. And the one who names his waiting has a name for it, and the name makes it **something he does**, not something happening to him.

## Voice-over Script for Recording

```
1.  This is Rima. In the yard is one swing.
    [pause 2s]
2.  Rima loves the swing very much.
3.  She came today, and found Nour on it.
4.  Nour swings and laughs.
5.  Rima stood nearby. And looked.
6.  She wanted the swing now.
7.  She thought: I'll tell Nour get off.
    [pause 3s]
8.  And she thought: I'll wait until she gets off.
    [pause 3s]
9.  And she stayed standing.
10. What would you do?
    [total silence 5 seconds]
11. Think.
    [total silence 3 seconds]
12. Rima sat on the nearby stone.
13. Then she said to Nour:
14. [Rima] I am waiting for my turn.
    [pause 2s]
15. So Nour looked, and finished her swinging.
16. Nour swung a little, then got off.
17. So Rima got on, and swung.
18. And Nour was pushing her from behind.
19. What Rima did is called: waiting for a turn.
```

**19 sentences · every sentence ≤ 7 words · narrator ~90 words/minute.**

## Parent Guide

> **What is your child learning?**
> To wait his turn, **and to say that he is waiting.**
>
> **🔴 And the second half is the harder and more important one**
> Silent waiting for a three-year-old is **emptiness**, and emptiness bursts after half a minute. But the one who says "**I am waiting for my turn**" has made waiting **something he does**, not something happening to him.
>
> **The five-word sentence**
> "**I am waiting for my turn.**" — five words, in the first person, and no command to anyone in it. Teach it to your child **by saying it yourself first** in a line and at home.
>
> **And notice what the episode did not do**
> **Nour did not get off when Rima said her sentence.** She finished her swinging then got off.
>
> On purpose. If she got off at once the sentence would become **a spell that grants the wish** — so your child learns that waiting is a fast way to get, and gets angry the first time it doesn't work.
>
> **And there is no adult in the episode**
> No one says "wait your turn." Rima chooses on her own. The presence of authority would turn the choice into **obedience**, and obedience disappears when the authority disappears.
>
> **🔴 The red line**
> - **Do not stand in front of or behind a moving swing.** The waiting is **to the side.**
> - Do not push a swing with a child on it unless a parent is near.
> - Do not pull a child off the swing nor hold the rope while it moves.
>
> **How do you help him?**
> - Name the waiting in time: "**Your turn after Nour**" — "after" is clearer than "be patient."
> - Give him an **action** to do while waiting: he sits on the stone, counts the swings, holds his turn in his hand.
> - 🔴 **Do not reward him for waiting** with a sweet or a star. Reward moves the motive outside, so waiting stops when the reward stops.
> - If he bursts: no scolding. Repeat the sentence alone: "I am waiting for my turn."

## Family Activity

> **One Turn for Each — 10 minutes**
>
> 1. Choose **one thing** everyone loves: a ball, or a seat, or the mixing spoon in the dough.
> 2. Put **a stone or a cushion** beside it: this is the **waiting place.**
> 3. Whoever waits sits there, **and says the sentence out loud**: "I am waiting for my turn."
> 4. 🔴 **The first rule: the grown-ups wait too** and say the same sentence.
> 5. Make the turn **short** — twenty seconds, not two minutes.
>
> **The father sitting on the waiting cushion and saying the sentence is the whole activity.** A child who sees the grown-up wait understands that waiting is not a punishment for the little ones.
>
> 🔴 **Nothing heavy · nothing hot · and no swing inside the house.**
>
> **A variation for the older child (age 5):** he holds **a small hourglass** and turns it for his turn. And seeing the time run down makes the waiting felt.
>
> **A variation for the three-year-old:** the turn is **ten seconds** only, and the grown-up counts out loud.
