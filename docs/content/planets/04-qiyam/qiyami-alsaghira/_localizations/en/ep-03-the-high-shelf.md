## The Question It Answers

> **"My hand doesn't reach, so what do I do?"**

## Learning Objective

**One objective:** the child asks for help **with words**, and offers it **when he sees a need.**

The criterion: he says an explicit asking sentence — "I want help, please" or its equivalent — in 3 of 4 situations where he is unable, **and offers help with a sentence** in 3 of 4 situations where he sees another in need, with one hint at most.

🔴 **The goal has two halves because it is one skill in two directions.** A child who asks but does not offer learns that people are **a source of services.** And one who offers but does not ask learns that asking is **shameful.** And the two halves together build **an exchange**, and that is what is required.

## 🔴 Two Decisions That Define This Episode

### 1. The solution is **asking**, not waiting or crying

| ❌ The rejected form | ✅ The form used |
|---|---|
| Tariq cries, so someone comes | 🔴 **Tariq walks and asks** |
| Tariq waits for someone to notice him | 🔴 **Tariq begins** |
| "I can't" | 🔴 "**I want help, please**" |
| An adult notices and intervenes | 🔴 **No adult in the scene** |

> 🔴 **The same decision on which [The Ant's Journey](../../05-qisas/bedtime-stories/story-01-ant-journey.md) is built in the Stories planet.**
>
> "It got tired so they rescued it" teaches that **inability brings help** → passive waiting and crying.
>
> "It saw the problem so it asked" teaches that **asking is a decision, not weakness** → a practical skill.
>
> And the consistency between the two planets is intentional: **the child asks, and is not waited to be noticed.**

### 2. 🔴 The second half is in the same episode, not another

Offering help comes **after** asking in the same 160 seconds. This is intentional:

| If they were separated | What it produces |
|---|---|
| An episode for asking and an episode for offering | The child learns them as **two separate skills** |
| ✅ **Together in one episode** | He learns them as **two faces of one thing** |

**And Tariq is the one who does both.** If Hind offered help to Tariq alone, he would become **the always-needy party** — and that builds a position, not a skill.

### 🔴 A documented exception: two sentences, not one

[The series bible](../series-bible-qiyami-alsaghira.md) limits the child's dialogue to one sentence. **This episode is an explicit exception** because its goal has two halves:

| # | The sentence | The half |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | "I want help, please." | Asking |
| 2 | "Can I help?" | Offering |

**And the second is one phrase**, so the total is five words — shorter than a single sentence in another episode.

## Parent Guide

> **What is your child learning?**
> Two things that are two faces of one thing: to **ask** for help with words, and to **offer** it when he sees a need.
>
> **The two sentences**
> - "**I want help, please.**" — the ask
> - "**Can I help?**" — the offer
>
> Five words in total. Teach them to your child **by saying them yourself first** at home.
>
> **🔴 And the difference we built the episode on**
> Tariq **did not cry and did not wait.** He walked and asked.
>
> "It got tired so they rescued it" teaches that **inability brings help** — so the child waits for someone to notice his inability, and cries if not noticed.
>
> "It saw the problem so it asked" teaches that **asking is a decision, not weakness** — and this is a skill he uses at daycare and at home.
>
> **And trying is not shameful**
> Tariq reached out his hand **twice** before asking, and the episode does not blame him for that. The intended order: **I try, then I ask** — not "ask at once" nor "try forever."
>
> **🔴 "Can I help?" is a question, not an action**
> Tariq **did not gather Hind's blocks without permission.** He asked first.
>
> And an offer that bypasses the one in need is not help — and it is the commonest mistake in helping, and correcting it begins at this age.
>
> **And the offer begins with seeing**
> "**So Tariq looked**" before "Can I help?". Noticing is the skill, and the sentence comes after it.
>
> **🔴 The red line**
> - 🔴 **No climbing on a chair or table or shelf** to bring something down. Never.
> - No pulling on a shelf nor hanging from it.
> - No bringing down anything heavy or breakable.
> - **A high thing is brought down by a grown-up** — and this is a sentence said plainly at home.
>
> **How do you help him?**
> - **Do not precede him.** When you see him unable, wait a few seconds for him to ask. Preceding prevents the ask.
> - If he cries or shouts, no scolding. Repeat the sentence alone: "I want help, please."
> - 🔴 **Do not reward him for asking nor for offering.** Reward moves the motive outside.
> - **You ask him** for small real things: "I want help, please — will you hold this end?" — seeing the grown-up ask ends the idea that asking is weakness.

## Family Activity

> **Two Sentences at Home — 15 minutes**
>
> 1. Hang in the kitchen a sheet with **two drawings**: a reaching hand, and an offering hand.
> 2. Agree that whoever needs something says: "**I want help, please.**"
> 3. And whoever sees another in need says: "**Can I help?**" — **and asks before he acts.**
> 4. 🔴 **The first rule: the grown-ups use the two sentences too** — out loud.
> 5. At the end of the day count together: how many times was each sentence said?
>
> **Hearing the father say "I want help, please" is the whole activity.**
>
> **The Two Ends Game — 5 minutes**
>
> Fold clothes together. An end for each. Whoever is unable says the first sentence, and whoever sees says the second.
>
> 🔴 **No climbing on anything · no drawers pulled out · and the requested things are light and unbreakable · and everything high is brought down by a grown-up.**
>
> **A variation for the older child (age 5):** he says the ask **with its reason**: "I want help, please — my hand doesn't reach."
>
> **A variation for the three-year-old:** the first sentence alone this week, and the second next week.
