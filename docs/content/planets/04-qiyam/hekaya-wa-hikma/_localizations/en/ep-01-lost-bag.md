## The Situation

A child finds a bag on a bench in the schoolyard. Inside it is a complete set of colored pencils — and he owns only three broken ones.

## The Value

**Honesty** — named only in the final segment.

## Learning Objective

**One objective:** the child learns that what is not his is returned, **and that the right path is to hand it to a responsible person.**

The criterion: he orders the steps of returning a found item correctly, in 3 of 4 attempts.

## 🔴 A Safety Decision Inside the Value

The value is honesty, but **the execution must be safe.**

| ❌ Forbidden | Why |
|---|---|
| "Look for its owner in the street" | Pushes the child to talk to strangers |
| "Wait for it by the bench alone" | Keeps him isolated |
| Opening the bag and searching it | Privacy · it may contain something harmful |
| Handing it to any passing adult | A direct danger |

| ✅ The taught path | Form |
|---|---|
| 1 | Don't take it home |
| 2 | Don't open it or take anything from it |
| 3 | **Hand it to your teacher or an adult you know** |
| 4 | Tell your mom or dad what you did |

> 🔴 **A value taught through an unsafe path becomes a danger.** Honesty here ends at a **known responsible person**, not directly at "its owner."

## Parent Guide

> **What is your child learning?**
> That what is not his is returned — **even if no one saw him**. And that the right path is: he does not take it, does not open it, and hands it to **an adult he knows**, then tells you.
>
> **🔴 A safety clause inside the value**
> The episode **does not say** "look for its owner." Teaching a child to search for the owner of a lost item pushes him to talk to strangers. Honesty ends at a **known responsible person** — a teacher, a staff member at a known place, or you.
>
> Nor do we teach him to open the bag to find its owner. Privacy is part of honesty, and a lost item may contain something harmful.
>
> **🔴 Why was Samer not rewarded?**
> On purpose. No gift, no star, no applause — an ordinary word of thanks, and it ends.
>
> **An external reward for moral behavior weakens the internal motive.** A child who learns that honesty brings a gift will ask "and what is in it for me?" — and will lie when there is nothing in it.
>
> The effect in the episode is a **feeling**: "Nothing more was in his hand, but he walked at ease."
>
> **And this means something for you too**
> If your child returns something that is not his, praise the **act** and do not give him a reward: "You returned it. That is about you." instead of "Take a sweet because you are honest."
>
> **The episode's moment**
> After Samer reaches out his hand and stops, a frame appears and your child is asked: "**What would you do?**" and the sound falls silent for **eight seconds**.
>
> **Sit with him during these seconds and do not answer for him.** The silence is intentional, and it is the most useful part of the episode.
>
> **And why do we not ask "Did Samer do wrong?"**
> Because that trains the child to judge others, which is easier than reviewing himself. The question is always: "What do **you** do?"
>
> **What if your child admits he took something before today?**
> This is a moment of trust, not a moment of punishment. Thank him for his honesty, and help him return it. **Punishment here teaches him that honesty is costly.**

## Family Activity

> **The Lost-and-Found Box — 15 minutes**
>
> 1. Gather small things you find left around the house: a pen, a sock, a toy, a clip.
> 2. Make a real **lost-and-found box** at home, and put in it whatever you find.
> 3. Agree on a rule: **what you find that is not yours goes to the box** — not to your pocket.
> 4. Once a week, open the box and ask: "Whose is this?"
>
> **The box turns the value into a daily habit** — more useful than a single talk.
>
> **A question to discuss:** "What is the hardest thing to return?" — let him answer honestly. **Admitting a value is hard makes it real, not a far-off ideal.**
>
> **A situation to talk about:** "If you found money in the street, what would you do?" — the answer: he tells you first. **He does not act alone, and does not talk to a stranger.**
>
> 🔴 **What we don't do:** we don't reward him materially for returning something. And we don't make the box a contest between siblings.
