## The Situation

Layla has a new box of colors. Beside her is Faris, with no colors, looking at his blank paper.

## The Value

**Sharing — and its limits.**

## Learning Objective

**One objective:** the child distinguishes what is shared from what is **personal**, and learns that sharing **does not mean giving up.**

The criterion: he sorts 6 items into "shared / mine," in 3 of 4 attempts.

## 🔴 Two Editorial Decisions That Define This Episode

### 1. Sharing **does not mean** giving the thing away

| ❌ The rejected form | ✅ The form used |
|---|---|
| Layla gives the box to Faris | Layla places the box **between them** and they draw together |
| "She gave up her colors" | "They used them together, and they stayed with her" |
| Sharing = loss | Sharing = **joint use** |

**Why:** a child who understands sharing as **loss** will refuse it. Real sharing at this age is **joint use**, not a transfer of ownership. Correcting this concept makes the value actually applicable.

### 2. 🔴 The right to say no is preserved

| What is not shared | Why |
|---|---|
| A water bottle · a spoon · food eaten from | 🔴 **Hygiene** — germ transfer |
| A comb · a towel · a toothbrush | 🔴 **Personal cleanliness** |
| Something very precious to him | **A legitimate personal boundary** |
| Something not his *(his brother's or his mom's)* | It is not his to dispose of |

> 🔴 **Forced sharing is a pedagogical danger.** A child compelled to share everything learns that **his boundaries have no value** — and that weakens his ability to say "no" in far more dangerous situations.
>
> The episode teaches **respectful refusal with an alternative:** "This is mine, but I'll ask for one for you."

## Parent Guide

> **What is your child learning?**
> That sharing is **joint use**, not giving away. And that **some things are personal, and that is okay.**
>
> **🔴 The first inversion: sharing is not loss**
> Layla **did not give** her box to Faris. She placed it **between them** and they drew together, and it stayed with her in the end.
>
> A child who understands sharing as "losing the thing" resists it fiercely — and he is right. But "we use it together" is applicable at once.
>
> **Try this phrasing:** instead of "give your brother the toy," say "**play with it together**." The difference in outcome is noticeable.
>
> **🔴 The second inversion: the right to refuse is preserved**
> At the end of the episode Faris asks for Layla's water bottle, and she says: "**This one is mine, but I'll ask for one for you.**" And the narrator does not blame her; he says: "**And some are personal, and that is okay.**"
>
> **And why does this matter?**
> Compelling a child to share **everything** teaches him that **his boundaries have no value**. A child who learns that finds it hard to say "no" in situations far more dangerous than a box of colors.
>
> **What is never shared:** what touches the mouth *(a bottle, a spoon, food eaten from)* · personal hygiene tools *(a comb, a towel, a toothbrush)*. **These are hygiene reasons, not stinginess.**
>
> **And something very precious?** He may keep it. Offer an alternative: "Your new toy is yours. Shall we play with another?"
>
> **How do you help him?**
> Sort his room's things together into two groups: **"we use together"** and **"mine."** Let him decide the second group — **on condition it includes everything that touches the mouth.**
>
> **The episode's moment**
> After Layla draws the box near, your child is asked: "**What would you do?**" and **eight seconds of silence.** Sit with him and do not answer for him.
>
> **If he says "I won't share"?**
> Ask: "**What are you afraid of?**" Usually the answer: it will break, or run out. These are real worries with solutions: "we put it in the middle and use it carefully" or "I'll watch with you both." **The fear is treated, not scolded.**

## Family Activity

> **Two Shelves in the Room — 20 minutes**
>
> 1. Set aside two spots in the child's room: **"we use together"** and **"mine."**
> 2. The child distributes his things himself into the two spots.
> 3. 🔴 **The one non-negotiable rule:** everything that touches the mouth or belongs to hygiene goes to "mine" — for hygiene reasons.
> 4. Beyond that, **the decision is his.**
> 5. Agree: what is on the "together" shelf is asked for without awkwardness, and what is on the "mine" shelf is respected without argument.
>
> **Giving him the authority to distribute is the activity.** A child who decides his boundaries respects others' boundaries faster.
>
> **A question to discuss:** "Why don't we share a water bottle?" — the answer: germs, not stinginess. And this links to the episode [My Hygiene and My Protection](../../03-oloom/discover-your-body/ep-06-hygiene.md).
>
> **A situation to talk about:** "Your friend asked for your favorite toy. What do you say?" — practice the phrasing of **respectful refusal with an alternative:** "This one is precious to me, but let's play with this."
>
> 🔴 **What we don't do:** we don't force him to share everything, we don't call him stingy, and we don't take something from his personal shelf to give to another.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] Duration 240 seconds ±10.
- [ ] Every sentence ≤ 12 words · **31 sentences** within the 30–36 range.
- [ ] 🔴 **The value's name in sentence 30 only** — not in the title.
- [ ] 🔴 **No preaching**: no "you must share" nor "the generous child."
- [ ] 🔴 **The pause question** with a unified frame and **total silence 8 seconds**.
- [ ] 🔴 **Layla does not give the box away** — she puts it in the **middle**.
- [ ] 🔴 The sentence **"But she did not give it to him"** is present.
- [ ] 🔴 "And the box stayed with Layla in the end" is present.
- [ ] 🔴 **The privacy-boundary segment** is present, with a **clear refusal + respectful alternative**.
- [ ] 🔴 The sentence **"And some are personal, and that is okay"** is present.
- [ ] 🔴 The narrator **passes no judgment on the refusal**.
- [ ] The three worries (10–12) **in the same tone** — no blame.
- [ ] 🔴 **Faris does not ask** — the situation is about noticing and initiative.
- [ ] 🔴 Faris **without exaggerated sadness or pity**.
- [ ] 🔴 Layla **without a scowl** at the refusal.
- [ ] The reason for the refusal is **hygiene**, not stinginess.
- [ ] 🔴 **No reward**.
- [ ] The "mine" bin is all **hygiene** items.
- [ ] The `give-away` distractor = the targeted misconception.
- [ ] 🔴 **No religious text** · the setting is neutral.
- [ ] The family activity **gives the child the authority to distribute**, with one non-negotiable hygiene rule.
- [ ] The parent guide forbids **forced sharing** and calling the child stingy.
- [ ] 🔴 **Pedagogical review** documented — **and the right to refuse especially**.
- [ ] ⚠️ **Cultural-neutrality review** and names and the refusal phrasing.
- [ ] ⚠️ English language review: "might break" · "the two papers" · "you two use."
- [ ] No flashing > 3Hz · no sudden sound.
