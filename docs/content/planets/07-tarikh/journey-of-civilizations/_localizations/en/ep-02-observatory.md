## The Question

How would you know the time if the electricity and the network were cut off together?

## The Concept

Measuring the **angles** of sky bodies, recording the measurement **repeatedly over years**, and building a **table** that can be predicted from.

## Learning Objective

**One goal:** the child knows that observation is **organized, recorded repetition**, not a single clever glance.

Criterion: distinguishes between "looked at the sky" and "observed and recorded then compared," and explains why the table needs years, in 3 of 4 tries.

| 10 | 🔴 **The ancients knew why the bodies move** | 🔴 **explicitly denied** — "they measured and did not know the cause" | **confirmed** | 🔴 contradiction check with [Moon Phases](../../03-oloom/science-in-a-minute/ep-03-moon-phases.md) |

> 🔴 **Claim 10 is the most important item in the episode, and it is a prevention item, not a proof item.**
>
> The episode **does not say** the ancients knew the cause of the phases or the cause of the planets' motion. It says they **measured the positions precisely**. The difference between **measurement** and **explanation** is what protects the episode from contradicting the [Moon Phases episode](../../03-oloom/science-in-a-minute/ep-03-moon-phases.md).
>
> 🔴 **And claim 7 is completely omitted:** precision numbers differ between observatories and sources, and stating one number makes the others wrong.

## 🔴 The Signature Decision: measurement is not explanation

| ❌ rejected form | ✅ adopted form |
|---|---|
| "they knew how the moon orbits" | 🔴 "they **measured** its positions, and recorded them" |
| "they understood the universe" | "they made a table they predict with" |
| "they knew the earth turns" | 🔴 **not stated** — a claim that bears dispute |
| "they discovered the cause of the phases" | 🔴 "**they measured and did not know the cause**" |

> 🔴 **You can measure something precisely and predict it precisely, while you do not know its cause.** This is a deep idea, and a ten-year-old can understand it with one example.
>
> And the example in the episode: a **tide table** or a **sunrise table** — people use it without knowing its cause.
>
> This protects two things at once: **it prevents the episode from contradicting Planet Science**, and it teaches the child that **description and explanation are two different layers of knowledge.**
>
> 🔗 **And the link to the Moon Phases episode is stated explicitly** in the trace part — it is not left to chance.

## 🔴 The Second Decision: the hero is repetition

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| one scientist looks one night and discovers | 🔴 **a group observes for years and records** |
| a moment of inspiration | 🔴 **boring, repetitive, organized work** |
| "looked and saw" | "observed, recorded, compared, and repeated" |

🔴 **The episode says explicitly that the work was long and repetitive.** Because the child imagines science as moments of discovery, while the truth is that it is **organized, boring recording** extending over years. And this honest picture is the more useful one for them if they want to work in science.

### Visual Rules

| # | Rule |
|---|---|
| 1 | **do not show a disk to be looked at** · time is measured **by the shadow** |

| 3 | **no face and no eyes** — the same rule as [Moon Phases](../../03-oloom/science-in-a-minute/ep-03-moon-phases.md) |
| 4 | **no faces** · and no constellations drawn as animal figures |
| 5 | 🔴 **simplified** · and it is stated that the shape is estimated |
| 6 | **unreadable or symbolic text** — no real readable Arabic text |
| 7 | 🔴 no borders · 🔴 **not mirrored in RTL** |
| 8 | 🔴 **mirrored in RTL** |
| 9 | 🔴 no individual features · diverse |
| 10 | −24 dB · 🔴 no instrument stereotyping |

> 🔴 **The manuscript clause matters:** showing real readable Arabic text in a manuscript **creates a claim** about its content we cannot support, and it might be read as a religious text. **So the text is symbolic or blurred**, and the numbers and columns are what is visible.

## Parent Guide

> **What is your child learning?**
> That people measured the positions of sky bodies **with tools that measure angles**, and recorded the measurements **for years**, then compared them and found a regularity that allows prediction.
>
> **🔴 And the hero in the episode is repetition, not genius**
> The child imagines science as moments of discovery: looked, saw, discovered. The truth is that it is **organized, boring recording** extending over years.
>
> This is why the episode says: "**years, not nights**." And the honest picture is the more useful one for your child if they want to work in science one day.
>
> **🔴 And the most important sentence in the episode: measurement is one thing and explanation is another**
> The ancients measured the positions precisely, **and did not know their causes.** And you can measure something and predict it precisely while you do not know its cause.
>
> This is not a belittling of them, but **a description of two layers in knowledge**: description and explanation. It is a deep idea a ten-year-old understands with one example.
>
> **And we have two reasons for this:**
>
> 1. **Honesty.** We do not attribute to the ancients what we found no evidence for.
> 2. 🔴 **Non-contradiction.** The cause of the moon's phases is the subject of [an episode in Planet Science](../../03-oloom/science-in-a-minute/ep-03-moon-phases.md). And had this episode said they "knew the cause," two planets would contradict in one app. And the episode **references that episode explicitly.**
>
> **🔴 And the "how do we know?" part**
> The evidence here: remains of buildings with circular foundations, and written tables that reached us with numbers and dates.
>
> And what we do not know: **the names of most observers**, and **the exact shape of some tools.** This is why the drawings in the series are **simplified, not realistic** — the picture is simplified because our knowledge is simplified.
>
> **⚠️ Safety note — important**
> 🔴 **Never look at the sun directly**, nor with sunglasses, nor with smoked glass, nor with binoculars or a telescope. The harm may happen **without any sensation of pain.**
>
> And the episode shows measuring time **by the shadow**, not by looking at the sun, and states the warning explicitly. And the activity below uses **the shadow only.**
>
> **A question to talk about after the episode**
> "Do you know something we predict precisely and do not know all its causes?" — the weather is a good example. **And the conversation alone is enough.**

## Family Activity — optional

> **The pole's shadow — 15 minutes over the course of a day · with adult supervision**
>
> **Materials:** a straight stick or a long pen · sticky tack · a large sheet · a pen.
>
> 1. Fix the stick vertically on the sheet, in a **sunny place that does not move.**
> 2. Mark the tip of the shadow with a dot, **and write the time beside it.**
> 3. Repeat the marking **every hour** — three or four times in the day.
> 4. Notice: the dots draw **a regular path.**
> 5. 🔴 **Repeat the experiment on another day**, and compare the two sheets.
> 6. Ask: "**Can you predict the shadow's position tomorrow at the same hour?**"
>
> 🔴 **Steps 5 and 6 are the activity.** One day gives dots, **and two days give a regularity** — and this is exactly the difference between "a night" and "years" in the episode.
>
> 🔴 **And do not look at the sun in any step.** We look at **the shadow** only.
>
> ⚠️ A safe place with no car traffic · a short time in the sun · water · a hat · and the adult chooses the place and the time.
>
> **And if they ask about the cause of the shadow's motion**, the answer is in [Planet Science](../../03-oloom/README.md). And this activity is about **measurement**, not about the cause — exactly like the episode.
