## The Question

If you wanted people a thousand years from now to read what you wrote today, what would you do?

## The Concept

Carrying knowledge between languages **with a method**, and preserving it **by repeated copying**.

## Learning Objective

**One goal:** the child knows that a text does not survive by itself, but **what was copied survives**, and that translation is **methodical, collective work.**

Criterion: explains why a text that was not copied is lost, and distinguishes methodical translation from literal translation, in 3 of 4 tries.

> 🔴 **Claim 1 is the key to the episode, and it is a dispute claim, not a proof claim.**
>
> "House of Wisdom" is a famous name, and its size and system are **disputed among historians.** And the episode **says this explicitly** instead of choosing one account and presenting it as fact.
>
> This is the hardest editorial decision in the planet: the title is famous and attractive, **and honesty requires us to say we do not know its details.** The solution is that the episode is **about translation and copying**, not about a building.
>
> 🔴 **And claim 12 is an absolute prevention item:** no religious text and no religious ruling in this planet, however Islamic the historical context. Its place is [Planet 9](../../09-islamic/README.md) after the Sharia-approval gate.

## 🔴 The Signature Decision: what we know is what was copied

| ❌ rejected form | ✅ adopted form |
|---|---|
| "all knowledge was preserved" | 🔴 "**what was copied survived**" |
| "their books reached us" | "what was copied of them repeatedly reached us" |
| "little was lost" | 🔴 "**we do not know how much was lost**" |
| a whole intact library | 🔴 **scattered copies in far-apart places** |

> 🔴 **This is the deepest idea in the whole planet, and the most useful for the child.**
>
> **We do not know the past. We know what remains of it.** The text that was copied ten times reached us, and the one copied once was probably lost — **even if it was more useful and more correct.**
>
> So what reached us is **not the best of what was written, but the most of what was copied.** These are two different things.
>
> And a ten-year-old understands this with one example: **if you wrote a page and did not copy it, and it was lost, no one knows it existed.** Then they ask the right question: "so what was lost that we do not know was lost?"
>
> 🔗 **And this idea is completed in** [Paper and Writing](./ep-05-paper-and-writing.md): the cheaper the medium, the more copying, so the more survives.

## 🔴 The Second Decision: translation is a method, not a talent

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| a brilliant translator alone | 🔴 **a group comparing copies** |
| a word-for-word translation | 🔴 **carrying the meaning and setting a term** |
| "they knew two languages" | "read the text, compared its copies, named the meanings" |
| translation is transfer | 🔴 **translation is building a language** |

🔴 **Scientific translation makes words that did not exist.** If a meaning with no name in the language was transferred, **a name was set for it.** This is a linguistic and scientific work at once, and it has a root in [Planet Abjad](../../01-abjad/README.md): the precise word spares a sentence.

## Parent Guide

> **What is your child learning?**
> That knowledge before printing was **one copy written by hand**, and that its survival depended on one thing: **that it be copied repeatedly and distributed in far-apart places.**
>
> **🔴 And the most important idea in the episode: what reached us is not the best of what was written, but the most of what was copied**
>
> This is a deep idea, and we ask you to pay attention to it. **We do not know the past; we know what remains of it.** The book copied ten times reached us, and the one copied once was probably lost — even if it was more useful and more correct.
>
> And the intuitive assumption is that what remained remained **because it was the best.** It is a logical error that accompanies people into adulthood, and appears in every judgment of the past.
>
> And the episode links it to something your child knows: **preserving files.** One copy is lost, and three far-apart copies remain. The rule is one after a thousand years.
>
> **🔴 And the episode says explicitly that historians differ**
> "House of Wisdom" is a famous name, **and its size and system are disputed among historians.** It would have been easiest to choose one account and present it as fact. We chose to state the dispute.
>
> So the episode in its essence is **about translation and copying**, not about a building. And what we have evidence for is **the manuscripts and their margins**, not a description of halls.
>
> **🔴 And a difference between two kinds of not-knowing**
> "We do not know how many books were lost, **and we cannot know**." Some not-knowing is removed by new research, and this not-knowing is **structural**: what was lost without a trace cannot be counted, because counting it needs a trace of it.
>
> **And translation in the episode is a method, not a talent**
> It is not word for word, but **reading, comparing copies, naming meanings, and agreeing on them.** And many Arabic scientific words your child uses today **were set in that work** — since they had no name before it.
>
> **And we do not attribute the work to one group**
> **People of different languages and religions** took part in it, and this is historically precise. And you will not hear in the series "we invented" nor "the golden age."
>
> **⚠️ And this series has no religious content**
> The historical context here is Islamic, and the episode **does not mention a religious text nor a Sharia ruling** — neither in the sound nor the image. That belongs to the Planet of Faith and Manners alone, after documented Sharia approval.
>
> **A question to talk about after the episode**
> "What is the thing you wrote and would like to survive? And how do you preserve it?" — and the practical answer *(a second copy in another place)* is the lesson itself.

## Family Activity — optional

> **Copy and compare — 15 minutes · no screen**
>
> 1. Choose a short text **of three lines** — from your child's book or from what they wrote.
> 2. Your child copies it **by hand**, then you copy it **by hand** from their copy, not from the original.
> 3. Then they copy **from your copy** once more.
> 4. 🔴 Compare **the last copy with the original**, and look for every difference.
> 5. You will find small differences: a letter · a mark · a word.
> 6. Ask: "**How do we know which copy is the correct one?**"
> 7. The answer: **by comparing more than one copy** — and this is what the copyists did.
>
> 🔴 **Steps 4 and 6 are the activity.** Seeing the error enter by itself is what explains why they needed the comparison.
>
> 🔴 **And do not correct during the copying** even if you see an error. The error is **part of the experience.**
>
> **Optional addition:** write in the margin of each copy **the copyist's name and the date** — as the copyists did. Then understand how we knew that about them.
>
> ⚠️ 🔴 **No religious text is copied in this activity.** Choose an ordinary text: a description, or a story, or what your child wrote.
