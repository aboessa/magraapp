## The Question

Where did the thing in your hand right now come from?

## The Concept

A route is **not a straight line**, but **a chain of stations** imposed by water, mountains, and distance.

## Learning Objective

**One goal:** the child knows that historical routes **follow water and passes**, and that they are **successive stations**, not one journey.

Criterion: draws a route between two cities accounting for water and mountains, and explains why it was not straight, in 3 of 4 tries.

> 🔴 **Claim 7 is delicate and important:** names like "the Silk Road" **were given by later historians**, and the owners of the route did not name it that.
>
> Mentioning this in the episode teaches the child that **some of the names we use for the past we made ourselves** — a useful critical idea, and it is said in one sentence.

## 🔴 The Signature Decision: the route is a network of stations, not one journey

| ❌ rejected image | ✅ adopted image |
|---|---|
| one trader crosses the whole route | 🔴 **goods move between many traders** |
| one straight line on the map | 🔴 **a winding route with stations** |
| "traveled from here to there" | "the goods arrived, and the trader did not arrive" |
| a heroic journey | 🔴 **a chain of short exchanges** |

> 🔴 **The common image is wrong: a trader carrying their goods thousands of kilometers.**
>
> The closer picture is that the goods **move from hand to hand** in successive stations. Each trader knows their leg and who is before them and who is after them, **and no one crosses the whole route.**
>
> And this explains two things at once: **why the stations were large cities**, and why **ideas and words moved** with the goods — for every station is **a meeting of two languages**, not a passing-through.
>
> 🔗 **And it is the same idea as dividing into stages in** [My First Project](../../06-maharat/robo-codes/ep-06-first-project.md) in Planet Skills: a large work is divided into stages that are done and tested.

## 🔴 The Second Decision: geography imposes the route

The episode teaches that the route **is not a free choice**:

| The constraint | Its effect |
|---|---|
| **water** | no route without water · so the stations are at the water |
| **mountains** | crossed only through a pass |
| **desert** | crossed by the shortest line between two water sources |
| **the coast** | guides ships with no tools |
| **the wind** | sets the **time** of departure, not its route |

🔴 **"Water first" is the key to reading any historical map.** And the child who understands it can **predict** the position of an old city before being told — and this is what the episode's challenge measures.

### Visual Rules

| # | Rule |
|---|---|
| 1 | **no political borders, flags, or country names** — cities, rivers, coasts, and mountains |
| 2 | **the map is never mirrored in RTL** |
| 3 | **drawn broken between stations** — not one confident continuous line |
| 4 | dots **prominent and clear** · they are what is visible, not the line |
| 5 | 🔴 **moving between different people** — not one person |
| 6 | 🔴 diverse · no individual features · 🔴 **no dress stereotyped by a people** |
| 7 | no overloading · no whips · no harm |
| 8 | 🔴 **a shape marker** not a red color |
| 9 | simplified not realistic · no exaggerated yellow filter |
| 10 | −24 dB · 🔴 no instrument stereotyping |

> 🔴 **Drawing the route broken is a precise and important decision.**
>
> The continuous line **claims knowledge** of a route we do not know *("we see the stations, and we do not see the route between them")*. And the broken line **says what we know honestly**, and agrees with the "how do we know?" part in the same episode.

```
22. And here is the most important point: no one crossed the whole route.
23. Each trader knows their leg, and who is before them, and who is after them.
24. So the goods arrive, and the trader returns.
    [pause 3s]
25. And at each station two languages meet.
26. So words, tools, and ways of working move with the goods.
27. And this is an effect no one intended.
28. And how do we know this?
29. The first piece of evidence: things found far from where they were made.
30. A coin, or a bead, or a piece of pottery.
31. And the second: words shared between far-apart languages.
    [pause]
32. So what do we not know?
33. We do not know each caravan's route, nor each journey's duration.
34. And we see the stations, and we do not see the route between them.
35. And what remains of this?
36. The highways pass through the same passes.
37. For the mountain did not change.
38. And many cities today were a station on a route.
    [pause]
39. And names like the Silk Road were given by later historians.
40. For the owners of the route did not name it that.
41. So every new innovation stands on a prior one.
42. Today's challenge is on the map, not on the timeline.
43. Place five stations in their positions.
    [pause]
44. And begin with the water. The water shows you the rest.
```

## Parent Guide

> **What is your child learning?**
> That historical routes **were not straight**, and that they **follow water and mountain passes.** And the real distance is not what is between the two cities, but **what is between two water sources.**
>
> **🔴 And a common image the episode corrects**
> We imagine a trader carrying their goods thousands of kilometers. The closer picture is that the goods **move from hand to hand** in successive stations: each trader knows their leg and who is before them and who is after them.
>
> **So the goods arrive, and the trader returns.**
>
> And this explains something important: **why the words and ideas moved with the goods.** Each station was **a meeting of two languages**, not a passing-through. And the most important results of trade **no one intended.**
>
> **🔴 And the route on the map is drawn broken on purpose**
> Because the archaeological evidence is **dots not lines**: we find things in places, and we do not find the route between them. And the continuous lines in books' maps are **a drawn inference.**
>
> This is why the "how do we know?" part says: "**we see the stations, and we do not see the route between them.**"
>
> **🔴 And a fact that may surprise you**
> Names like "the Silk Road" **were given by later historians.** And the owners of the route did not name it that. Some of the names we use for the past **we made ourselves** — a useful critical idea for your child.
>
> **And the series does not prefer one people over another**
> You will not hear "our traders were the most skilled." Trade is **exchange**, and the episode is about **geography and organization**, not precedence.
>
> And the episode does not show **battles or conflict**, and it is a rule in the whole planet.
>
> **🔴 And the most useful thing your child takes: "begin with the water"**
> The last sentence in the episode. It is **a map-reading strategy**, not a fact: whoever begins with the water can predict the position of an old city before being told.
>
> **A question to talk about after the episode**
> Open a map of your city and look for a river, a coast, or a spring. Then ask: "**Why did the city arise here?**"

## Family Activity — optional

> **Draw the route — 15 minutes · paper and pen**
>
> 1. Your child draws an **invented map**: two cities · a mountain range · a desert · three water points · one pass.
> 2. 🔴 **They first draw the straight line** between the two cities, and notice what it passes over.
> 3. Then they draw the **real route** that passes by the water and the pass.
> 4. They measure the two routes with a string or a ruler: the real one is **longer.**
> 5. Ask: "**Why is the longer route better?**"
> 6. The answer: because the shorter one **cannot be traveled.**
>
> 🔴 **Step 4 is the activity.** Seeing that the longer is better breaks the "shorter is best" intuition — the same intuition as "the shortest solution is the most correct" that [Planet Skills' packs](../../06-maharat/game-packs.md) break.
>
> **Optional addition:** your child places **a station at each water point**, and names it. Then asks themselves: "which station would have become a large city?" — and the answer: **the crossroads.**
>
> ⚠️ An activity with paper and pen · no scissors · and no need for continuous supervision.
