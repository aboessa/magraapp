## The Question

How many times did you write something today and then erase it or throw it away?

## The Concept

**The price of the medium determines what is written.** For when the medium became cheap, more was written, so more survived.

## Learning Objective

**One goal:** the child knows that the cheapness of the writing medium **changed the kind of what is written**, not its quantity alone.

Criterion: explains why daily notes are not written on an expensive medium, and links the cheapness of the medium to the survival of knowledge, in 3 of 4 tries.

> 🔴 **Claim 3 is stated explicitly, and it is an important neutrality item.**
>
> Paper-making **arose in East Asia**, and moved westward. Stating it explicitly **prevents** the child from understanding that it arose in the Arab region — a common error in national-pride content.
>
> **And the episode attributes the origin to its place, and describes the contribution to developing and spreading it without exaggeration.** This is more honest, and more useful educationally: the child learns that **a contribution in a chain** is no less in worth than the beginning.

## 🔴 The Signature Decision: the price of the medium determines the kind of what is written

| The medium | Its price | What is written on it |
|---|---|---|
| stone | months of work | 🔴 **what one wants to survive for centuries** |
| treated hide | expensive | an important book · a document |
| cheap paper | little | 🔴 **a note · an account · a draft · a letter** |

> 🔴 **This is the most precise idea in the episode, the hardest, and the most useful.**
>
> When the medium is expensive, **only what is worthy is written on it.** And who decides what is worthy? Whoever has the price.
>
> So the cheapness of paper did not only increase the **quantity** of what was written, it **changed its kind**: the daily account, the ordinary letter, the draft, and the student's note came to be written.
>
> **And most of what we know about the lives of ordinary people came from this cheap kind** — not from the luxurious books.
>
> 🔗 **And here the episode meets** [The House of Wisdom](./ep-03-house-of-wisdom.md): "what reached us is not the best of what was written, but the most of what was copied." **And the cheap medium makes copying more** — so the series closes on itself in the last episode.

## 🔴 The Second Decision: the origin in one place, and the development in many places

| ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|
| "we made paper" | 🔴 "**the craft arose in East Asia**" |
| "we carried it to the world" | "it moved west, and developed in many cities" |
| "they stole it from us" | 🔴 **never a conflict form** |
| "the first to make paper" | "among the oldest we know" |

🔴 **The episode teaches the child to rejoice in contribution, not in precedence.** And this message is more important than any fact in it, and it is the true closing of the series.

## Parent Guide

> **What is your child learning?**
> That **the price of the surface we write on determines what we write.** And when paper became cheap, the kind of what was written changed, not its quantity alone.
>
> **🔴 And this is the most precise idea in the planet**
> When the medium is expensive, **only what is seen as important is written on it.** For stone is carved for months, and treated hide is expensive.
>
> And when paper became cheap, it came to be written on: **the daily account, the ordinary letter, the draft, and the shopping list.**
>
> **And most of what we know about the lives of ordinary people came from this cheap kind** — not from the luxurious books.
>
> **🔴 And the deepest sentence in the series**
> "**And we know about people's lives as much as their paper was cheap.**"
>
> Our knowledge of the past is **biased by the price of the medium.** An age whose medium was expensive, we know its great news and do not know its households. It is not that the ancient people had no daily life, but that their life **was not written.**
>
> And your child may need to hear this sentence twice. **And it deserves it.**
>
> **🔴 And the episode says explicitly that paper-making arose in East Asia**
> And this is a deliberate item. The common error in national-pride content is to assume the craft arose where it flourished. And Baghdad and others were **a place of development and spread**, not a place of origin.
>
> And we teach your child to **rejoice in contribution, not in precedence.** A contribution in a chain is no less in worth than the beginning, and honesty is better in both cases.
>
> And you will not hear in the episode "we carried it to the world" nor "they stole it from us." The craft **moved and developed**, with no conflict form.
>
> **🔴 And an open question we leave to your child**
> "When everything becomes written, **what is worthy of keeping?**" And the episode says: "**And the question is yours, not ours.**"
>
> It is a question of their age, not the ancients' age, and we have no answer to give. And if they want to talk about it, **listen more than you speak.**
>
> **⚠️ Safety note**
> 🔴 **Historical paper-making used boiling and caustic materials, and we have omitted them all from the display.** And the episode shows soaking, beating, and drying only.
>
> **And no paper is made at home.** And the activity below uses ready-made paper, with no hot water and no chemicals.
>
> **🔗 And this is the last episode in the planet**, and it completes [The House of Wisdom](./ep-03-house-of-wisdom.md): that one is about copying, and this one is about the price of what it is copied on. **And it is preferable to be watched after it.**

## Family Activity — optional

> **The expensive page — 15 minutes · paper only**
>
> 1. Give your child **one small sheet** *(a quarter page)*, and say: this is all your paper today.
> 2. Ask them to write on it **what is worthy of surviving a year.**
> 3. 🔴 **Do not give them a second sheet** even if they ask. The constraint is the activity.
> 4. Then give them **ten sheets**, and say: write whatever you want.
> 5. 🔴 Compare: **what was written on the one sheet, and what was written on the ten.**
> 6. Ask: "**Which sheet tells whoever reads it more about your day?**"
> 7. The expected answer: **the ten** — because they carry the daily and the passing.
>
> 🔴 **Steps 5 and 6 are the activity.** The child sees **themselves** that the cheap medium preserves ordinary life, and the expensive one preserves occasions.
>
> **Optional addition:** keep the two sheets in one place, and open them **after a year.** And then ask the sixth question again.
>
> ⚠️ 🔴 **No paper-making at home** · no hot water · no blender · no chemicals · and no scissors if the child is alone.
