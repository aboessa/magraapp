## The Question

Where does the water in your home come from?

## The Concept

Moving water long distances with a **measured slope** and no power but gravity.

## Learning Objective

**One goal:** the child knows that moving water without pumps needs **a precise measurement of a very small slope** over a long distance.

Criterion: explains why a water channel needs a slope, and why the slope is small rather than large, in 3 of 4 tries.

> 🔴 **Claim 3 is stated qualitatively with no number on purpose.** The slope ratio differs between channels, and stating one number makes the variety **an error**. The concept is understood from "very small" and from the image, not from a ratio.
>
> 🔴 **And claims 7, 8, and 9 are stated in the plain form of not-knowing** — which is what fulfills [the series rule](../series-bible-journey-of-civilizations.md#5--مقطع-كيف-نعرف--المواصفة): at least one sentence says what we do not know.

## 🔴 The Signature Decision: the achievement is measurement, not an idea

| ❌ rejected form | ✅ adopted form |
|---|---|
| "they invented the channel" | 🔴 "they dug a course **with a measured slope**" |
| the idea is the achievement | 🔴 **the measurement** is the achievement |
| "they were clever" | "they measured the land before they dug" |
| "the first channel in history" | "among the oldest we know" |

> 🔴 **The idea "a course that carries water" is simple, and a nine-year-old thinks of it in a second.**
>
> **The hard part is digging kilometers underground with a very small, uniform slope.** More than that and it scours the course and destroys it; less than that and the water stops and does not move.
>
> This is why the episode is **about measurement, not the idea**. The child who understands this knows that engineering is **precision**, not inspiration — the same message as controlling the variable in [Planet Science](../../03-oloom/README.md).

## Parent Guide

> **What is your child learning?**
> That water reached cities before electricity through **courses dug underground with a very small slope**, in which the water moves by gravity alone.
>
> **🔴 And the achievement in the episode is not the idea but the measurement**
> The idea "a course that carries water" is simple, and your child thinks of it in a second. **The hard part is digging kilometers with a uniform, very small slope.**
>
> More than that and the water scours the course and destroys it. Less than that and the water stands and does not move. This is why the episode shows **the two wrong cases** before the correct one — "small" is understood only by comparison.
>
> And our message is that engineering is **precision, not inspiration.**
>
> **🔴 And the "how do we know?" part**
> In every episode of this series there is a one-minute part that explains **where the information came from.** Here there are two pieces of evidence: channels still working, and rows of shafts seen from above.
>
> Then — and this is the most important — the part says **what we do not know**: "we do not know who dug the oldest of them, nor how long it took to build."
>
> We want your child to learn that **the evidence supports a specific claim**, not everything we would like to say. It is a skill that serves them in everything they read, not history alone.
>
> **🔴 And we do not attribute the achievement to a people**
> You will not hear in this series "we invented" nor "the golden age." The form is always **a time, a place, and a described work.** And the channels were found in many far-apart regions, and we do not know who came before whom.
>
> And the last sentence in the episode: "**every new innovation stands on a prior one**" — and it repeats in the five episodes.
>
> **⚠️ Safety note — please read it**
> 🔴 **Do not allow any digging**, nor approaching a well, an opening, or a channel, nor descending into any underground void. The old openings are **deep and unsecured**, and the air in them may be unfit to breathe.
>
> The activity below uses **a tray and a little water on a table**, and has nothing to do with digging.
>
> **A question to talk about after the episode**
> "How do you think they knew the land was sloped before they dug?" — you do not need an exact answer. **The question itself is the benefit.**
>
> The episode is `independent` and does not need your supervision, and the activity is optional.

## Family Activity — optional

> **The tray's slope — 10 minutes · with adult supervision**
>
> **Materials:** a flat baking tray · a thin book or a piece of cardboard · a little water · a towel.
>
> 1. Place the tray on a **level** table, and pour a little water in it.
> 2. Notice: the water **stands and does not move.**
> 3. Raise one edge with a thin piece of cardboard — **a very small slope.**
> 4. Notice: the water **moves slowly and uniformly.**
> 5. Raise the edge with a thick book — **a large slope.**
> 6. Notice: the water **rushes and splashes.**
> 7. Ask: "**Which slope moves the water without destroying the course?**"
>
> 🔴 **Step seven is the activity.** The three cases together are what make "very small" understood.
>
> ⚠️ **Very little water** · on a table **away from any electrical outlet** · the adult handles the water · and any drop is wiped at once · **no hot water** · and no glass tray.
>
> 🔴 **And the activity has nothing to do with digging.** Whoever wants more, let them draw a side cross-section on paper.
