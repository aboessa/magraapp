## The Question It Answers

> **"Can something in the house be older than everyone I know?"**

## Learning Objective

**One goal:** the child knows that **an ordinary thing can be older than every person they know**, and expresses this **with the ladder of grandparents**, not a number of years.

Criterion: orders four rungs of the grandparent ladder, and sorts 5 of 6 things into "was here before my grandfather was born" or "made in my time," in 3 tries of 4.

## 🔴 The Signature Decision: the ladder of grandparents instead of a timeline

| ❌ rejected | ✅ used |
|---|---|
| "a thousand years ago" | 🔴 "**before your grandfather was born**" |
| a timeline with numbers | 🔴 **four rungs: me · my father · my grandfather · my grandfather's grandfather** |
| "a hundred grandfathers ago" | 🔴 **never a number** |
| "this jar is ... old" | 🔴 "**we do not know how old it is**" |

> 🔴 **The grandparent ladder is not a simplification of a timeline — it is a different tool.**
>
> A timeline measures **distance**; the grandparent ladder measures **a number of people**. The child knows their grandfather, and knows their grandfather had a grandfather, **so they climb the fourth rung without representing a number.**
>
> ⚠️ **And the rungs are not converted into years, nor into a count of grandfathers.** A number brings back the very problem the ladder was built to solve.

## Parent Guide

> **What is your child learning?**
> That **an ordinary thing in the house can be older than every person they know**. And that the thing itself tells us things — and stays silent about others.
>
> **🔴 And no dates in this whole series**
> You will not hear "a thousand years ago," nor a civilization's name. The reason is that a seven-year-old **does not represent "a thousand years"**: they hear it as a sound, not a distance, and come away with confusion, not knowledge.
>
> **Our alternative is the grandparent ladder:** you, your father, your grandfather, your grandfather's grandfather. 🔴 **With not a single number.** Your child knows their grandfather, and knows their grandfather had a grandfather — so they climb the ladder without representing a count.
>
> ⚠️ **So do not convert the ladder into years.** "That means about two hundred years ago" cancels its whole benefit.
>
> **🔴 And the most important part of the episode is "And what does it not tell us?"**
> It has three lines: we do not know who made it, nor how old it is, nor who drank from it. Then: **"We see the thing, and we do not see its owner."**
>
> We want your child to learn that **a thing supports a specific thing**, not everything we would like to say. It is a skill that serves them in everything they hear, not history alone.
>
> **⚠️ Safety note — please read it**
> 🔴 **Do not give your child a clay jar to carry, fill, or lift.** Pottery is heavy, it breaks, **and its shards are sharp like glass**. The jar in the episode is **an image, not an activity tool**.
>
> The activity below uses **a plastic cup, a wet cloth, and cool water**, on a table away from any outlet. 🔴 **No hot water, no glass cup, and nothing goes in the mouth.**
>
> **A question to talk about after the episode**
> "What is the oldest thing in our house? And who gave it to us?" — 🔴 **and the answer comes from a person, not a guess.** And if no one knows, then "we do not know" is a completely correct answer, and it is the very thing the episode teaches.

## Family Activity

> **The wet cup — 12 minutes · 🔴 with adult supervision**
>
> **Materials:** two sturdy **plastic** cups · a clean cotton cloth · cool tap water · a towel · a plate.
>
> 1. Fill the two cups with the same amount of **cool** water. 🔴 **The adult pours the water.**
> 2. Wrap the cloth around one cup, and wet it with the cool water.
> 3. Place the two cups on the plate on a table, 🔴 **away from any electrical outlet**.
> 4. Leave them for half an hour, and do something else.
> 5. Touch the two cups on the outside with the back of your hand. **Which is colder?**
> 6. Ask: **"The cloth is wet. So where did its water go?"**
> 7. Connect: 🔴 **the jar's outer face does this by itself, because pottery is rough.**
> 8. 🔴 **And do not drink from the two cups** — empty them into the sink after the activity.
>
> 🔴 **Steps 5 and 6 are the activity.** Touching with the back of the hand is enough; no thermometer is needed.
>
> **Variation for the older child (8 years):** a third cup with a wet cloth **in a place with a breeze**, and compare the three.
>
> ⚠️ **Conditions:** 🔴 no clay jar in the activity · no glass cup · no hot water · no drinking from the activity cups · nothing goes in the mouth · away from outlets · the adult handles the water and wipes any drop at once.

## Production Notes

- 🔴 **No year, century, civilization name, or person name** · this is the **governing constraint of the series**.
- 🔴 **No map and no timeline** · consequently the series is **not gated on `timeline_map` nor on the neutralized base map**, unlike [Journey of Civilizations](../series-bible-journey-of-civilizations.md).
- 🔴 **The jar is seen still, with no label and no inventory number** · it represents a **type**, not a specific artifact.
- 🔴 **A 3-second pause at second 42** with the shot still · it is intentional and is not shortened in the edit.
- 🔴 **The grandparent ladder is shown with no number** · and "and he was a child like you" is a mandatory line.
- 🔴 **The "and what does it not tell us?" part is mandatory** · with three "we do not know" lines in the same rhythm.
- 🔴 **"We see the thing, and we do not see its owner" is repeated across the five episodes in the same wording** · its 9–12 counterpart is "we see the work, and we do not know its makers."
- 🔴 **No clay jar in any activity** · pottery is heavy, breaks, and its shards are sharp.
- ⚠️ **Material-culture review required on** lines 7, 8, 18, 19, and 29 — all in the [fact-check table](#-fact-check-table).
- ⚠️ **Non-contradiction check with [Planet Science](../../03-oloom/README.md)**: what is said about evaporation here **does not contradict** the water-cycle episode.
- ⚠️ **Language review:** "pottery" · "evaporates" · "dried" · and the grandparent ladder.
