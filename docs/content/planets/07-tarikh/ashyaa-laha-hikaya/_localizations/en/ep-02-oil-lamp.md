## The Question It Answers

> **"How do I know that a human hand made this thing, not a machine?"**

## Learning Objective

**One goal:** the child identifies **at least two signs** that a human hand shaped a thing rather than a machine: **the thumbprint**, and **two pieces differing from each other**.

Criterion: distinguishes the hand-made thing from the machine-made thing in 5 of 6 cases, and names at least one sign, in 3 tries of 4.

> 🔴 **Claim 9 is in the table on purpose.** It is easy for the episode to slide into a seemingly clever inference: "the thumbprint is here, so its owner was right-handed." **It is an inference we do not have support for**, and letting it in ruins the whole part.
>
> **The adopted limit: the thumbprint tells us a hand shaped it. And it tells us nothing about the owner of the hand.**

## 🔴 The Signature Decision: two signs, not one

| Sign | What it shows | Why it does not suffice alone |
|---|---|---|
| **the fingerprint** | a hand shaped the thing | 🔴 it could be a mold that was made by hand then copied |
| **the difference between two pieces** | each piece was shaped on its own | 🔴 it could be an old, imprecise machine |
| 🔴 **the two signs together** | **a hand shaped each piece on its own** | — |

> 🔴 **One piece of evidence does not distinguish — and this is the same rule as [Planet The World Around Us](../../08-alam/README.md)** in the goal "gathering evidence, one piece of which does not distinguish."
>
> The consistency is intentional: the child meets the **same principle** across two planets, and learns it is not a history rule or an observation rule, but a **way of thinking**.

## Parent Guide

> **What is your child learning?**
> To know **by a sign** that a human hand shaped a thing rather than a machine: the fingerprint, and the difference between two alike pieces.
>
> **🔴 And one sign does not suffice**
> The fingerprint alone could be in a mold copied many times. The difference between two pieces alone could be from an old, imprecise machine. **The two signs together** are what tell us a hand shaped each piece on its own.
>
> This is the same thing they learn in [Planet The World Around Us](../../08-alam/README.md): **one piece of evidence does not distinguish.** The consistency is intentional.
>
> **🔴 And the most important limit in the episode**
> The thumbprint **tells us nothing about its owner** — not the size of their hand, nor which hand, nor their age. The mark supports one claim: **that a hand shaped it.**
>
> And you will find your child wants to infer more, and that is good. The question that gently stops them: **"And what tells you that?"**
>
> **⚠️ Safety note — please read it**
> 🔴 **The lamp in the episode is unlit in every shot, and there is no fire in the whole episode.** This is a deliberate decision: the subject of the episode is lighting, **and there is no flame in it.**
>
> 🔴 **And do not try to light any lamp, wick, or oil.** No match or lighter within your child's reach. The activity below uses **flour-and-water dough**, with no fire and no oil.
>
> 🔴 **And do not give them a clay lamp to carry** — pottery breaks and its shards are sharp like glass.
>
> **A question to talk about after the episode**
> "Do we have something in the house that a hand shaped?" — and if you find nothing, that is a correct answer too. 🔴 **The search itself is the benefit.**

## Family Activity

> **Two thumbs that do not match — 12 minutes · 🔴 with adult supervision · no fire**
>
> **Materials:** a cup of flour · water · two plates · a towel. 🔴 **And no oil, no wick, no fire.**
>
> 1. 🔴 **The adult kneads** the flour with water until it becomes a firm dough.
> 2. Your child takes a piece the size of their palm, and you take one like it.
> 3. Each of you shapes a **small vessel** by pressing with the thumb in the center of the piece.
> 4. Place the two vessels on the plate side by side.
> 5. Ask: **"Are they exactly identical?"** Then be silent.
> 6. Look together for 🔴 **the thumbprint** in each, and for **one difference** between them.
> 7. Ask: **"And what does the vessel not tell us about its maker?"**
> 8. 🔴 **The dough is not eaten or brought near the mouth**, and it is discarded after the activity and hands washed.
>
> 🔴 **Steps 5 and 6 are the activity** — and the two signs together, not one.
>
> **Variation for the older child (8 years):** make **four** vessels, and ask them to order them from closest to furthest from identical.
>
> ⚠️ **Conditions:** 🔴 no fire, no match, no lighter, no oil, no wick · no clay lamp in the child's hand · no oven or heat-drying · the dough is not eaten or brought near the mouth · no much salt in the dough · hands washed after the activity.
