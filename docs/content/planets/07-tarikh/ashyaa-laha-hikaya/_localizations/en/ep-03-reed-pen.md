## The Question It Answers

> **"What can I know about people's lives from a single tool?"**

## Learning Objective

**One goal:** the child infers **one thing about people's lives** from a single tool, **and supports their inference with what they see in the tool.**

Criterion: states one inference supported by what is seen in the tool, and rejects two inferences the tool does not support, in 3 tries of 4.

## Parent Guide

> **What is your child learning?**
> To infer **one thing about people's lives from a single tool**, and to **support their inference with what they see in the tool.**
>
> **🔴 And the inference in the episode is simple and solid**
> "A tool is made only for the one who uses it." So the existence of a pen tells us someone was **writing**. Your child walks through this inference themselves, and is not handed its conclusion.
>
> **🔴 And "because it is old" is not support**
> It is the most common answer at this age. The required support is that they point to **something in the tool**: the inner hollow, or the slanted tip. And the question that teaches this: **"And what in the pen tells you that?"**
>
> **🔴 And the empty sheet**
> At the end of the episode an **empty** sheet appears beside the pen, and not a letter is written on it. It shows your child the limit of evidence without words: **we have the tool, and we do not have what was written.**
>
> And you will not hear in the episode what was written, nor in which language, nor who wrote. **The answer to all three: "we do not know."**
>
> **⚠️ Safety note — please read it**
> 🔴 **A reed pen is made by cutting its tip with a sharp tool, and there is no place for this in a child's activity.** Do not cut reeds in front of them, and do not give them a knife, blade, or scissors, **and no cutting tool is seen in the whole episode.**
>
> The activity below uses 🔴 **a wide-tipped wooden stick** — a drink stirrer or a cotton swab stick — **and water paint on paper.** No permanent ink, nothing goes in the mouth, and no pointed stick brought near the face or eye.
>
> **A question to talk about after the episode**
> "What is the oldest written thing in our house? And who wrote it?" — 🔴 **and this is a question answered by a person, not by a guess.**

## Family Activity

> **One angle, and two lines — 12 minutes · 🔴 with adult supervision · no cutting**
>
> **Materials:** a **wide-tipped** wooden stick *(a drink stirrer)* or a cotton swab stick · water paint or food-colored water · thick paper · a small plate · a towel. 🔴 **And no knife, no scissors, no blade.**
>
> 1. 🔴 **The adult prepares the paint** in the plate, and prepares the stick.
> 2. Your child dips the tip of the stick a little, and wipes the excess on the plate's edge.
> 3. They draw a line **with the wide tip flat on the paper**. They notice: a wide line.
> 4. They draw another line **with the tip on its edge**. They notice: a thinner line.
> 5. Ask: **"What changed? The paint or the hand?"**
> 6. They try third and fourth angles, and see a series of different lines.
> 7. Ask: **"And what does this paper not tell us about tomorrow?"** — and let them think about the limit of evidence.
> 8. 🔴 **The stick is not brought near the face or the eye**, and everything is washed after the activity.
>
> 🔴 **Step 5 is the activity.** Discovering that **the hand** is what changes the line is the lesson.
>
> **Variation for the older child (8 years):** they write one large letter with two different angles, and compare.
>
> ⚠️ **Conditions:** 🔴 no cutting, no knife, no scissors, no blade · no pointed stick · no permanent ink · nothing goes in the mouth · no real reed in the child's hand *(its edges may be sharp)* · an adult prepares the paint · and hands washed after the activity.
