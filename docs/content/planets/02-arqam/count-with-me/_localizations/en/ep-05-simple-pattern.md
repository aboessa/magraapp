## Learning Objective

**One objective:** the child discovers a repeating pattern and predicts the next element.

> **Why patterns on the numbers planet?**
> A pattern is the **root of algebraic thinking**. A child who sees "red blue red blue" and expects "red" is doing the same mental operation they will later use with sequences and functions. It is also the basis of **skip counting** (2, 4, 6).

Criterion: completes 3 of 4 patterns correctly.

## Parent Guide

> **What is your child learning?**
> Discovering a pattern and predicting the next. This is the **root of algebraic thinking**: a child who predicts the next element is doing the same mental operation they will later use with sequences and equations.
>
> **Why start with sound?**
> Because rhythm is closer to a young child's understanding than shapes. When they hear the repeat before they see it, the concept transfers without a visual load.
>
> **How to help?**
> Patterns are everywhere: floor tiles · shirt stripes · days of the week · the bedtime routine. Point at them and say: "Look, it repeats!"
>
> **Try the clapping game:** clap a simple pattern (one clap — two claps) and let them complete it. Then swap roles.
>
> **When to worry?**
> A five-year-old should complete a simple two-element pattern. A three-element pattern is harder and **may need until age six** — this is normal.
>
> **What not to do?**
> Do not explain the pattern in long words. **Repeat it in front of them** several times until they see it themselves. Discovery is stronger than explanation.

---

## Family Activity

> **Patterns from Home — 15 minutes**
>
> 1. Gather two different, plentiful items: buttons and beads · spoons and forks.
> 2. Start a simple pattern on the table: button — bead — button — bead.
> 3. Stop and ask: "What comes next?"
> 4. **Swap roles:** they make the pattern and you complete it. **And get it wrong on purpose once** so they correct you.
> 5. **Raise the difficulty gradually:** button button bead · button bead bead.
>
> **A movement pattern too:** jump — spin — jump — spin. The body learns the pattern as the eye does.
