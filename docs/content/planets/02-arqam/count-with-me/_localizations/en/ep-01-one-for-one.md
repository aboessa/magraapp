## Learning Objective

**One objective:** one-to-one correspondence — one place for each item, and one item for each place.

> **Why is this the very first episode of the whole planet?**
> One-to-one correspondence is the **foundation of counting**. A child who recites "one, two, three" without understanding it is repeating sounds, not counting. Counting cannot be taught before this idea.

Criterion: matches 4 of 5 pairs with no item skipped or repeated.

## Parent Guide

> **What is your child learning?**
> **One-to-one correspondence** — that each thing has exactly one place. This is **the foundation of all counting**. A child who chants "one two three" without grasping this principle is not counting, they are singing.
>
> **How do you know they understood?**
> A simple test: put 5 objects in front of them and ask them to count. If they **touch each object once** with each number, they understood. If they count faster than they touch, or touch one object twice, they are reciting numbers, not counting.
>
> **How to help?**
> **Mealtime is the best lesson.** "Put one spoon for each of us." "How many cups do we need?" Real distribution helps more than any screen.
>
> **When to worry?**
> A five-year-old should distribute 5 things across 5 places correctly. If they keep skipping or repeating after repeated practice, talk to their teacher.
>
> **What not to do?**
> Do not push them to count to 20 before they master matching up to 5. **High counting without understanding hides the problem rather than solving it.**

## Family Activity

> **The Family Table — 10 minutes a day**
>
> 1. Before eating, ask your child to hand out the spoons: "One spoon for each person."
> 2. Do not tell them the number. Let them **count the people** first.
> 3. If they make a mistake, do not correct at once. Ask: "Does everyone have a spoon?"
> 4. **The reverse game:** give them extra spoons and let them discover the surplus.
>
> **Try also:** socks and feet · lids and boxes · beads and cups. Daily life is full of one-to-one matching.
