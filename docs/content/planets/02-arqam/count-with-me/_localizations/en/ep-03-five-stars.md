## Learning Objective

**One objective:** the child counts to 5, and links **the spoken number to its written shape**.

What is new: this is the first episode to introduce the **shape of the numeral** as a symbol, after counting was mastered in the two prior episodes. The order is intentional: **meaning before symbol**.

Criterion: counts 5 items and picks the correct numeral in 4 of 5 attempts.

### Visual rules

- The stars are **identical in size**.
- At 5 stars, Suha **shrinks to the edge of the frame** to keep the 6-element limit.
- Every number card comes **with its dots**, mandatory.
- The dots are arranged in a fixed pattern (like the pips on a die) — this helps instant recognition later.

## Parent Guide

> **What is your child learning?**
> Counting to 5, **and linking the written numeral to the quantity**. Notice the order: we taught counting for two episodes before showing the numeral's shape. **Meaning before symbol**, always.
>
> **Why the dots under each numeral?**
> Because "5" on its own means nothing to a four-year-old. The five dots beside it are the **bridge** between the quantity they see and the symbol they will read.
>
> **How to help?**
> Post numerals at home **with dots or pictures** matching their count. A numeral alone on the wall does not teach.
>
> **When to worry?**
> A five-year-old should count to 5 confidently and recognize the shapes of numerals 1–5. If they count but do not recognize the shapes, **that is normal** and needs more time.
>
> **What not to do?**
> Do not teach them to write numerals now. **Recognition first, writing later.** And do not move on to 10 before mastering 5 fully.

## Family Activity

> **Match Number to Quantity — 10 minutes**
>
> 1. Write the numerals 1 to 5 on 5 small pieces of paper.
> 2. Gather small objects: buttons, beads, spoons.
> 3. Your child picks a paper and puts the correct number of objects on it.
> 4. **Reverse:** put 4 buttons out and let them find the paper with numeral 4.
> 5. **No timer, no winner.** The goal is the link, not speed.
>
> **Try also:** elevator numbers · page numbers · birthday candles. Numbers are all around them, more than you think.
