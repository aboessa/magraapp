## Learning Objective

**One objective:** the child discovers the rule of a numeric pattern (**skip counting**) and completes the missing one.

> **Why skip counting?**
> 2, 4, 6, 8 is not just a pattern. It is the **root of multiplication**: skip counting by two five times = 2 × 5. A child who masters it enters multiplication as an understanding, not a memorized table.

Criterion: completes 4 of 5 numeric patterns, and justifies the rule in two.

## Parent Guide

> **What is your child learning?**
> **Skip counting** — to count 2, 4, 6 or 5, 10, 15 by a fixed amount. And this is **not just a pattern**:
>
> **Skip counting is the root of multiplication.** "Skip by two five times" is exactly 2 × 5. A child who masters it enters multiplication understanding, and one who memorizes the table without understanding stumbles at the first indirect problem.
>
> **Looking for the amount:** they do not guess the missing number, they ask: "How much do we add each time?" and then apply it.
>
> **Counter's mistake is intentional:** it chose 9 because it is before 10. It chose **the visually closest**, not the one the rule requires. Real children do exactly this.
>
> **How to help?**
> **Skip count in real situations:** things in pairs, stairs two by two, money in fives, the fingers of both hands in fives.
>
> **When to worry?**
> An eight-year-old skip counts by twos, fives, and tens. Skipping by threes and fours is harder and **may need until age nine** — normal.
>
> **What not to do?**
> Do not mention the word "multiplication" or a table now. **Let them skip count until it becomes automatic**, and they will receive multiplication later as a shortcut for something they know.

## Family Activity

> **The Number Staircase — 20 minutes**
>
> 1. Write numbers on papers and arrange them like a staircase on the floor.
> 2. Start a pattern: 2, 4, 6 and leave a **gap**, then 10.
> 3. Your child jumps on the steps and says the numbers — **the body learns the pattern**.
> 4. At the gap they stop and say the missing number, **then explain why**.
> 5. **Swap:** they make the pattern and you complete it, **and err on purpose** as Counter does.
>
> **A fun escalation:** try skip by 3 · skip by 5 · skip by 10. Then **descending** from 20 downward.
>
> **A smart question:** "If we skip by two ten times, where do we reach?" — this is multiplication without mentioning multiplication.
