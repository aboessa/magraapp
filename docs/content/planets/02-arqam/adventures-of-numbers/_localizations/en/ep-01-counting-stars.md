## Learning Objective

**One objective:** the child estimates a quantity by sight, then **counts to check**.

> **Why estimate before counting?**
> Estimation is an independent mathematical skill that builds a **number sense** which catches errors later. A child who estimates "about ten" and then computes 47 knows their calculation is wrong. One who does not estimate accepts any result.

Criterion: makes a reasonable estimate (±3) then counts correctly, in 3 of 4 attempts.

## Voice-Over Recording Notes

- A tone that is **friendly and confident, not artificially childish** · ~120 words per minute.
- Light machine processing on a human voice · a regular, lightly-inflected rhythm.
- The most pedagogically important line ("An estimate is not wrong or right") is spoken slowly and clearly.
- Counter is corrected in an **explaining, not scolding** tone — Counter is a friend, not an accused.
- The count is from separate segments `asset-vo-num-1` … `-7`, one second per numeral.

## Parent Guide

> **What is your child learning?**
> Two skills together:
> **First — estimating:** to look and say a close number without counting. This is not a random guess, but a **number sense** that lets them catch their own errors later. A child who estimates "about ten" and then computes 47 knows their calculation is wrong.
> **Second — arranging before counting:** a strategy that prevents skipping or repeating an item.
>
> **Why does Counter make a mistake?**
> On purpose. When your child corrects a robot, they shift from a receiver to an **evaluator** — the highest level of thinking. And they see that a mistake is normal and fixable, so their fear of it lessens.
>
> **How to help?**
> At the market or at home: "Guess, how many apples in the bag?" then count them together. **Never correct their estimate** — say: "Your estimate was close" or "Now you know, so your next estimate will be better."
>
> **When to worry?**
> A seven-year-old should count to 10 correctly and arrange to count. If they keep skipping items, train them to **touch** each item while counting.
>
> **What not to do?**
> Do not call their estimate "far off" or "wrong". Estimation is a skill built by repetition, and judging it negatively kills it.

## Family Activity

> **Guess Then Count — 10 minutes**
>
> 1. Fill a clear bowl with small things: beads, buttons, cubes.
> 2. Each person says their estimate and writes it on paper.
> 3. **Count together** by arranging the things in rows.
> 4. **The winner is not the closest** — it is whoever improved their estimate the next time.
> 5. Repeat with different amounts over several days.
>
> **An important tip:** keep the old estimates. Seeing their estimates improve is stronger encouragement than any prize.
