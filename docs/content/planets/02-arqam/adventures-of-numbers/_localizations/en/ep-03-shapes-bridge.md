## Learning Objective

**One objective:** the child infers the pattern's rule **and justifies it**, rather than completing it by guessing.

The difference from the sprouts episode: there they completed the pattern by intuition. Here they are asked **"why?"** — and justification is the difference between understanding and guessing.

Criterion: completes 3 of 4 patterns **and justifies** two of them correctly.

## Parent Guide

> **What is your child learning?**
> Two things deeper than completing the pattern:
> **First — the pattern unit:** to see "triangle and square" as **one** repeating part, not two separate shapes. This is what separates understanding from guessing.
> **Second — justification:** to say **why** they chose their answer. A right answer with no reason may be luck, and the reason is what transfers to new problems.
>
> **Why is this important later?**
> A pattern is the root of algebraic thinking. A child who infers the rule "a square after a triangle" is doing the same operation they will use with sequences, functions, and equations.
>
> **Counter's mistake is intentional and useful:** it chose the new shape because it was new. Real children do this — they look for variety, not the rule. Seeing Counter err then correct teaches your child that a mistake is fixed by thinking.
>
> **How to help?**
> Make patterns and always ask: **"why?"** Do not accept "because it is". Ask: "What is the part that repeats?"
>
> **When to worry?**
> A seven-year-old completes a two-element pattern confidently. A **three-element** pattern is harder and may need until age eight — this is normal.
>
> **What not to do?**
> Do not settle for "correct" at the right answer. Always ask "why?". **Justification is the learning.**

## Family Activity

> **The Home Bridge — 20 minutes**
>
> 1. Make a "bridge" on the floor from repeating things: spoon, fork, spoon, fork...
> 2. Leave a **gap** in the middle.
> 3. Your child picks the missing piece, then says **why**.
> 4. **Swap roles** — they make the pattern and you complete it, **and err on purpose** as Counter does.
> 5. **Raise the difficulty gradually:** spoon-spoon-fork · then four pieces in the unit.
>
> **A smart question:** "How many pieces are in the repeating unit?" — this measures understanding, not intuition.
>
> **Patterns in the surroundings:** floor tiles · pavement stones · shirt stripes · days of the week.
