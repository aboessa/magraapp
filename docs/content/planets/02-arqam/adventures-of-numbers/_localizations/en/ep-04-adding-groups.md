## Learning Objective

**One objective:** the child understands addition as **merging two groups**, and links it to the `+` symbol.

Criterion: adds two groups whose sum is ≤ 10 in 4 of 5 attempts, and reads the symbolic sentence correctly.

## Parent Guide

> **What is your child learning?**
> **Addition as a meaning:** joining two groups into one. Not memorizing "3 + 4 = 7" but seeing three apples and four become seven in one basket.
>
> **Notice the order:** they saw the merging, then counted the sum, **then** saw the `+` symbol. Introducing the symbol before the meaning produces a child who memorizes a table and does not understand an operation.
>
> **The reasonableness check:** when Counter said 5 + 2 = 5, Noma asked: "Can the sum be the same as one of the groups?" This rule protects your child from accepting any result.
>
> **How to help?**
> In the kitchen and market: "You have three spoons, and I gave you four. How many do you have now?" then **join them for real** and count.
>
> **When to worry?**
> A seven-year-old adds up to 10 by counting. Relying on **their fingers is completely normal** and a healthy stage — do not stop them.
>
> **What not to do?**
> Do not make them memorize the addition table now. **Understanding first, speed later.** And do not stop them counting on their fingers; fingers are a thinking tool, not a flaw.

## Family Activity

> **The Addition Basket — 15 minutes**
>
> 1. Gather two bowls and small things.
> 2. Put a number in each bowl, and let your child **estimate** the sum first.
> 3. **Pour them together** into a third bowl and count.
> 4. Write the sentence on paper: 3 + 4 = 7.
> 5. **The reverse game:** say a clearly wrong sum ("3 + 4 = 2"), and let them find that it does not make sense **and explain why**.
>
> **A tip:** always write the symbolic sentence **after** the real merging, not before. The symbol records what happened, it does not precede it.

- All authored values (3+4=7, 5+2=7, 6+3=9) were preserved exactly. Characters Noma and Counter are kept.
