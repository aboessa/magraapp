## The Question It Answers

> **"Why not measure everything in centimeters and be done with it?"**

## Learning Objective

**One objective:** the child chooses a measuring unit suited to what they are measuring, and justifies their choice by the resulting number being **easy to say and use**.

Criterion: chooses the suitable unit in 5 of 6 situations, and justifies the choice in at least 3.

## New Vocabulary

| Word | How it is introduced |
|---|---|
| unit of measure | what we count with: centimeter, meter, gram, kilogram, milliliter, liter, minute, hour |
| the suitable unit | the one that gives a number neither too small nor too large |
| conversion | expressing the same amount in another unit |
| an amount alone is not enough | a number without a unit has no meaning |

The six bins are **paired within each field** (a large and a small unit for length, capacity, and time), so knowing the field is not enough — you must choose the right size within it.

## Mastery Criterion

```
Chooses the suitable unit in 5 of 6 situations,
justifies the choice in at least 3 by the resulting number being easier to say and use,
and refuses any numeric answer without a unit.
```

---

## Parent Guide

> **What is your child learning?**
> **Choosing the unit, and that a number without a unit is not an answer.** This skill appears in every measurement problem and every exam answer. A child who writes "25" without a unit loses a mark on something they actually know.
>
> **The red line**
> **Measuring in this activity is with a flexible tape measure, a ruler, and a measuring cup only.** No sharp tools, no hot water, no climbing on a chair or table to measure a height, and no measuring anything outside the home. If the measurement needs a height, the parent measures it.
>
> **How to help?**
> Make measuring a daily habit: "How far is your room door from the kitchen door, in meters or centimeters?" And most important: always ask for the unit with any number they say.
>
> **When to review with them?**
> If they choose a correct unit but cannot justify their choice, the knowledge is superficial. Replay scene three on its own and discuss the idea of a "comfortable number".

> **The Measuring Notebook — 20 minutes**
>
> 1. Make a three-column table: the thing · the chosen unit · the amount.
> 2. Choose five things **inside the home and within reach**: a book, a table, a cup, a hallway, an eraser.
> 3. **Your child chooses the unit before measuring**, and writes a reason in a word or two.
> 4. They measure with the flexible tape, the ruler, or the measuring cup.
> 5. Compare: is the number they got "comfortable"? If not, try another unit **by conversion, not by re-measuring**.
> 6. **The condition:** every number in the table is written with its unit. A number without a unit is crossed out.
>
> **Safety:** no scissors, no knife, nothing hot. No climbing on a chair or table; high things **the parent measures**. No measuring on the street or in the bathroom.
>
> **Variation for an older child:** estimate the amount before measuring, then measure, then compute the difference between your estimate and the measurement.

## Production Notes

- All conversions are by tens only (10 · 100 · 1000) so the child can verify them.
- **The field, the room, and the pencil are authored examples**, not attributed to a real place or an official measurement.
- It is **forbidden** to mention any price or currency in this episode; measurement only.
- The "comfortable number" rule is presented **as a practical habit, not a law**, with the engineer case stated explicitly.
- Numerals follow `numeral_system: auto`.
- `safety_notes` is **mandatory** in this episode because the activity asks for real measurement. The text names what is forbidden.
- No timer · no flashing > 3Hz · no UI text inside the images.
- ⏳ A pedagogical review is required for the wording of the "comfortable number" rule so it is not understood as a law.
