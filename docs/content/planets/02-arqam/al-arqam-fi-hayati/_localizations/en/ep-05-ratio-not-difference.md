## The Question It Answers

> **"Two mixtures with the same taste… and their amounts are different. How?"**

## Learning Objective

**One objective:** the child compares two quantities **by ratio**, not by difference alone, and decides which of the two comparisons answers the question asked.

Criterion: judges the equivalence of two ratios in 4 of 5 cases, and decides in at least 3 cases whether the question asks for a difference or a ratio.

## New Vocabulary

| Word | How it is introduced |
|---|---|
| the difference | how much one exceeds the other by subtraction |
| the ratio | how much of this corresponds to how much of that |
| two equivalent ratios | two ratios that give the same result even if the numbers differ |
| per one | a phrasing that reveals the ratio: how much of this per one of that |

In the first question **there are two correct answers**, and that is intentional: a ratio is a family, not a single case. And `mix.4_and_10` is a strong distractor because its difference equals the difference of `mix.3_and_9`.

## Mastery Criterion

```
Judges the equivalence of two ratios in 4 of 5 cases using "per one" or by multiplying by a number,
decides in at least 3 cases whether the question asks for a difference or a ratio,
and refuses to keep a ratio by adding to both sides.
A correct judgment without justifying it by the ratio does not count as mastery.
```

## Parent Guide

> **What is your child learning?**
> **Comparison by ratio, not by subtraction alone.** This is one of the most important hurdles in mathematics at this age, and it is the gateway to equivalent fractions, scale, speed, and percentage later. A child who compares only by subtraction stumbles on every one of them.
>
> **The red line**
> Do not accept "I add one to each side and the ratio stays". This additive thinking is the most famous error on the topic, and it must be corrected with a concrete example, not a rule.
>
> **How to help?**
> In the kitchen, the best place for this: "The recipe is one cup of rice per two cups of water. If we used three cups of rice, how much water?" And let them answer by multiplying.
>
> **When to review with them?**
> If they say the mixture of 4 and 10 is like the mixture of 3 and 9 because the difference is the same, replay scene four on its own. This is exactly the error the episode was built for.

## Family Activity

> **Two Mixtures With the Same Taste — 15 minutes**
>
> 1. Use **cold water** and something safe to mix: a spoon of concentrated juice or a drink powder made for children.
> 2. The first mixture: **one spoon per three cups of water**.
> 3. The second mixture: **two spoons per six cups**.
> 4. Taste both. **The taste is the same** even though the amount is double.
> 5. Now a third mixture: **two spoons per four cups**, and taste it. Stronger — because there is less water per spoon.
> 6. Your child writes the three ratios on paper in the "per one" form.
>
> **Safety:** cold tap water only. **No hot water, no boiling, no non-food substances, and no mixing anything not meant for drinking.** The spoon and the cup are the only tools of the activity.
>
> **Variation for an older child:** a ratio of 2 to 5. Make a mixture equivalent to it with larger numbers, and prove the equivalence by multiplying.

## Production Notes

- **The cups and cubes are drawn counted and visible** in each jug, so the child counts them rather than trusts them.
- The second and third jugs **have the same difference (6)** intentionally; this is the pivot of scene 4 and the numbers must not be changed.
- Every calculation is verifiable: 6÷2=3 · 9÷3=3 · 4×3=12 · 10÷4=2.5 · 8÷2=4 · 12÷3=4.
- "Juice powder" is **an authored substance with no brand and no product name**, and mentioning a price or currency is forbidden.
- In the taste activity, any substance not meant for drinking is forbidden; the safety text in the Parent Guide is mandatory in the display.
- Decimals (2.5) follow the locale's format, and numerals follow `numeral_system: auto`.
- No timer · no flashing > 3Hz · no UI text inside the images.
- ⏳ A pedagogical-mathematics review is required: is 2.5 introduced in this episode, or is the third jug replaced with numbers that give a whole result? *(see `open_questions`)*

## Localization Notes

- All authored numeric values (2/6, 3/9, 4/10, ratios 1:3, 2.5, 2/8, 3/12) were preserved exactly.
