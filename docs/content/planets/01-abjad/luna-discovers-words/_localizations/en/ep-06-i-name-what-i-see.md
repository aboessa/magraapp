## Learning Objective

**One objective:** the child names what they see in a scene, from memory, not from shown options.

The core difference: every earlier episode was a **choice between two or three**. Here the child **recalls the word themselves** — much harder, and a truer sign of real learning.

Criterion: names 4 of 6 things in a scene with no options shown.

### `self_check` — a design decision

Question 1 is **open naming**, and the app **does not use speech recognition**. The child speaks, then the correct word is spoken, and they confirm themselves ("I said it!" / "Let's hear it again").

**Why:** recognition of young children's speech is not reliable enough, and its errors unfairly discourage the child. Self-check is more honest pedagogically and safer technically. `score` is computed from question 2 only.

`multi_answer: true` — the answer is **two items**.

## Parent Guide

> **What is your child learning?**
> **Recall**, not recognition. The difference is big: recognition is picking from three; recall is bringing the word out of memory alone. The second is real learning.
>
> **Why doesn't the app correct their speech?**
> Because automatic recognition of young children's speech is not accurate enough, and its errors unfairly discourage your child. **You** are the best one to hear and encourage them.
>
> **How to help?**
> Sit with them for this episode in particular. At each silence, listen to what they say and encourage them — even if the word is not perfect. **The attempt is the win.**
>
> **If they say a wrong word?**
> Don't say "wrong". Say: "I heard you! And this one is called a tree. Say it with me." Then move on without pressure.
>
> **What comes after this series?**
> Your child is ready for the Planet Arqam (numbers) and Planet Alam (the world around us) series. And if they have turned five, begin preparing for the Explorers 6–8 track.

## Family Activity

> **My Window — 15 minutes**
>
> 1. Make a frame from cardboard like Luna's.
> 2. Sit at a real window or in the garden.
> 3. Your child looks through the frame and names **everything they see**.
> 4. Count together how many words they said — **no target number, just for fun**.
> 5. The next day, try a different window and notice the new words.
>
> **The frame is an attention trick:** it narrows the field of view so the child focuses on a few things instead of being scattered across many.
