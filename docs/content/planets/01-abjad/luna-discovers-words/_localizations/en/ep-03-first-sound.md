## Learning Objective

**One objective:** the child hears the **first sound** in a spoken word.

This is the most important episode in the series pedagogically: **phonemic awareness** is the single strongest early predictor of later reading success — stronger than knowing letter names.

Criterion: identifies the first sound in 3 of 4 words.

## The Sounds and Words

| Sound | Said as | Word | Reference asset |
|---|---|---|---|
| /b/ | **"buh"** (not "bee") | ball | `asset-ball` |
| /m/ | **"mmm"** (not "em") | mouse | `asset-mouse` |
| /s/ | **"sss"** (not "ess") | sun | `asset-sun` |
| /k/ | **"kuh"** (not "kay/see") | cat | `asset-cat` |

### A critical rule of speech

> The sound is said **as its sound, not its letter name**.

| ✅ Right | ❌ Wrong |
|---|---|
| "buh" | "bee" |
| "mmm" | "em" |
| "sss" | "ess" |
| "kuh" | "see" / "kay" |

**Why:** the child builds a word from sounds, not names. "bee-ay-el-el" does not make "ball" in their mind, but "b-a-ll" does. Teaching names first **hinders** reading, it does not help. Letter names are introduced in the `kids` (6–8) track, not here.

### Choice of sounds

The four are chosen because they are:
- **Different places of articulation**: bilabial stop · bilabial nasal · alveolar fricative · velar stop.
- Their words are **very familiar** to a 4-year-old.
- They contain **no confusable pairs** (b/d, m/n, s/f) in the same episode.

## Parent Guide

> **What is your child learning?**
> **Phonemic awareness** — that a word is made of sounds, and has a first sound. This skill is the strongest early predictor of your child's later reading success, stronger than memorising letter names.
>
> **Why do we say "buh", not "bee"?**
> Because the child builds a word from sounds. "b-a-ll" makes "ball" in their mind; "bee-ay-el-el" makes nothing. Letter names are taught later, at ages 6–8.
>
> **How to help?**
> Anytime: "Look, apple starts with aaa. And your name — what sound does it start with?" Make it a game, not a lesson.
>
> **When to worry?**
> If your child turns five and still cannot pick out the first sound after repeated practice, see a speech specialist. **Early detection saves years.** The app **does not diagnose**.
>
> **What not to do?**
> Don't force them to speak. If they go quiet, you say the sound and move on. Forcing creates a dislike of language.

## Family Activity

> **First Sound Game — 5 minutes a day**
>
> 1. Pick one sound for the day, like "mmm".
> 2. Race to find things at home that start with it: milk · mug · map · mat.
> 3. **No winner** — count together, not against each other.
> 4. The next day, a new sound.
>
> **Tip:** start with the first sound of **your child's name**. Nothing holds their attention like their own name.
