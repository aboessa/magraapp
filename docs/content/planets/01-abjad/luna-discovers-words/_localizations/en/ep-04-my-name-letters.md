## Learning Objective

**One objective:** the child traces a letter's shape with a correct stroke order.

Criterion: completes tracing 3 of 4 letters at ≥ 80% coverage.

## The Letters — Luna's name: L · U · N · A

| Letter | Form taught | Stroke order | Word |
|---|---|---|---|
| L | uppercase | one stroke down, then a short turn right | Luna |
| U | uppercase | down, curve, up | Umbrella |
| N | uppercase | down, up-diagonal, down | Nest |
| A | uppercase | up-slope, down-slope, then a crossbar | Apple |

> Luna's name preserved: the four teaching letters are the letters of **Luna** — the same identity as the Arabic edition, where the child traces "لونا". Native English identity uses the Latin letters L, U, N, A.

### A critical stroke rule

> **Body first, then the crossbar.**

Letter "A" draws its two slopes **first**, then the **crossbar** is added as a separate stroke. Doing the crossbar first teaches a bad habit that is hard to fix later.

### Why these letters

- **They are the letters of Luna's name** (L · U · N · A) — a child learns best what is tied to someone they know.
- All are **simple to draw**: one or two strokes plus one crossbar.
- Three single-motion letters + one with a crossbar — introduces the crossbar idea gradually.
- **No confusable letters** in the same episode (b/d, p/q, M/N kept apart).

### Visual rules for tracing

| Rule | Value |
|---|---|
| letter size | 40dp visually |
| tolerance band | 24dp · widens to 36dp after two tries |
| start dot | pulses `#FFD34D` twice before start |
| stroke speed | 2 seconds per stroke — **deliberately slow** |
| crossbar | different colour `#FF6FAE` + drawn as a separate stroke |
| off-path | the line pauses at the last correct point · **does not restart from the beginning** |

## Parent Guide

> **What is your child learning?**
> Fine motor control and the order a letter is drawn in. Order matters: **body first, then the crossbar**. The bad habit built now is hard to fix at school.
>
> **Why the letters of Luna's name?**
> Because a child learns best what is tied to someone they know. And "L U N A" are among the simplest letters to draw.
>
> **How to help?**
> Write your child's name in big letters on paper and let them trace it with a finger, then a pencil. **Their name is the strongest reason to write**, more than any other letter.
>
> **When to worry?**
> Don't worry if they go outside the lines a lot at this age — the small muscles are still growing. Worry only if they **refuse** to hold a pencil at all after five.
>
> **What not to do?**
> Don't hold their hand and draw for them. Let them get it wrong. The wrong stroke they tried themselves is worth more than the right one you drew.

## Family Activity

> **My Name Everywhere — 15 minutes**
>
> 1. Write your child's name in big letters on 3 sheets.
> 2. Sheet one: they trace with a finger.
> 3. Sheet two: they trace with a coloured pencil.
> 4. Sheet three: they draw it alone — **as best they can**.
> 5. Hang up sheet three, **with no corrections**.
>
> **Try also:** draw the name in sand, or with dough, or with dried beans. Touch fixes the shape more than a pencil does.

## Production Notes / Acceptance

- Duration 180 seconds ±10.
- Every line ≤ 6 words — **35 matching lines**.
- **Body before the crossbar** on "A" — taught explicitly and checked in the game.
- The crossbar is a **separate stroke**.
- Off-path **does not restart from the beginning**.
- Colouring has **no success condition**.
- The four letters have **no confusable pair**.
- **Certified handwriting / literacy review** of the path coordinates and their order.
- A simplified motor mode works (40dp margin · 60% coverage).
- The game is hidden on TV (`supports_dpad: false`).
