## The Question It Answers

> **"I read all the words… so why don't I know what happened?"**

## Learning Objective

**One objective:** the child reads a three-to-five-word sentence and shows understanding by answering a question about its **meaning**, not about its words.

Criterion: reads 4 of 5 sentences and answers one meaning question per sentence correctly, unaided.

**Note on re-authoring:** this Abjad episode is re-authored natively for English. The child arrives having blended sounds and letters into words in Episodes 1–3 (English phonics, not Arabic syllables); here those known words are read together as a sentence. Every displayed word uses only the letters and letter-sounds already taught.

## New Vocabulary

| Word | How it is introduced |
|---|---|
| sentence | words in order that tell us one whole thing |
| Who? What? Where? | three questions we can ask any sentence |
| read it again | I read the sentence a second time if I did not understand |


```
the goal is understanding, not saying the words.
```

## Parent Guide

> **What is your child learning?**
> **Reading a sentence with understanding** — saying the words **and knowing what happened**. Many children read a whole sentence and cannot say who did what; that is incomplete reading, not slow reading.
>
> **The red line**
> Do not let them answer from the picture. Cover it, let them read, then reveal it so they can check. Guessing from pictures works for two years and then collapses at the first book with no pictures.
>
> **How to help?**
> After each sentence they read, ask **one** question only: "Who?" or "What did they do?" or "Where?" — one question is enough; three questions turn reading into a test.
>
> **When to worry?**
> If they read a sentence fluently but cannot answer "who did it?" after two re-reads, repeatedly, on every sentence, that is worth a conversation with their teacher. **The app does not diagnose.**

## Family Activity

> **The Covered Sentence — 8 minutes**
>
> 1. Write a short sentence on one card, and a picture on another.
> 2. Cover the picture. Your child reads the sentence **once**.
> 3. They answer one question: Who? What? or Where?
> 4. **Then** reveal the picture so they can check for themselves.
> 5. **Swap:** they write the sentence, you read — and **get the doer wrong on purpose** so they correct you.
>
> **Variation for an older child:** two similar sentences with a different doer — "The paper is under the rain" and "The rain is on the paper" — and ask: what is the difference?
