## The Question It Answers

> **"I know the sounds… so how do sounds turn into a word?"**

## Learning Objective

**One objective:** the child blends two spoken syllables into one correct word, and says what it means.

Criterion: blends two syllables into a correct word for 4 of 5 words, unaided, and points to the thing the word means.

**Teaching order:** meaning, then sound, then symbol. In this episode **we do not write the word until the child has said it**, and we do not write it in full until the final scene.

## New Vocabulary

| Word | How it is introduced |
|---|---|
| syllable | a part of a word we say in one breath |
| blend | we put the two parts together with no gap |
| word rail | a wooden board in the workshop the syllables slide onto |

## Parent Guide

> **What is your child learning?**
> **Blending syllables** — hearing "pen… cil" and saying "pencil". Your child already knows letter sounds, but knowing sounds does not produce reading on its own; blending is the bridge between them.
>
> **The red line**
> Do not show them the word written before they say it. If they see the print first, they will memorise the shape of the word and skip blending — and that stops working at the very first new word.
>
> **How to help?**
> Any time, anywhere, say a two-syllable word and wait: "pen… cil?" "ba… nana?" Clap with each syllable. Their own name is the best start: "Ry… an."
>
> **When to worry?**
> A seven-year-old who hears the two syllables and cannot blend them after repeated practice needs a speech and hearing assessment. **The app does not diagnose.**

---

## Family Activity

> **Syllable Walk — 7 minutes**
>
> 1. Stand at the start of the hallway.
> 2. You say a two-syllable word: "cam… el."
> 3. Your child takes **one step per syllable**, then says the whole word when they stop.
> 4. **Swap roles:** they say the syllables and you step — and get it wrong on purpose once so they correct you.
> 5. No winner and no timer.
>
> **Variation for an older child:** three-syllable words — "el… e… phant."
