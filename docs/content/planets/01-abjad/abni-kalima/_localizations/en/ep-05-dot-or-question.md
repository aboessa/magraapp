## The Question It Answers

> **"What is this little dot at the end of the line?"**

## Learning Objective

**One objective:** the child reads a sentence with the tone that matches its mark — **the voice drops and stops at a full stop**, and **the voice rises at the end of a question** — and says what kind of sentence it is.

Criterion: reads 4 of 5 sentences with the correct tone, and identifies the kind (statement or question) in 5 of 5.

## New Vocabulary

| Word | How it is introduced |
|---|---|
| full stop | a mark that says: the sentence is finished — stop and take a breath |
| question mark | a mark that says: this sentence is asking |
| tone | the voice going up or down at the end of a sentence |
| question word | a word a question begins with: Do · What · Where · Who |

## Mastery Criterion

```
Identifies the kind of sentence (statement or question) in 5 of 5,
and reads 4 of 5 sentences with the correct tone: falling and stopping at a full stop,
rising at the end of a question.
Identifying the kind correctly while reading everything in one flat tone does not reach mastery.
```

## Acceptance Criteria

- Every question in the episode uses an **explicit question word**. No statement is turned into a question by only adding a "?".
- The tone arrows appear **after** reading, not before, so they do not become a visual cue instead of listening.
- Ryan makes his mistake once and corrects it himself after the Narrator's question.
- No flashing > 3Hz · no timer · no interface text inside the pictures.

## Parent Guide

> **What is your child learning?**
> **That punctuation is read, not just seen.** A full stop is a stop and a breath, a question mark is a lift in the voice. A child who reads with no punctuation reads one long flat line and understands nothing.
>
> **The red line**
> Do not accept one flat tone for every sentence. And do not teach that any sentence becomes a question just by adding "?"; a written question carries a question word: Do · What · Where · Who.
>
> **How to help?**
> **You read first, clearly**, then they read after you. Tone is passed by imitation, not explanation. And remind them of Ryan's trick: "Look at the end of the line before you start."
>
> **When to worry?**
> Tone grows with fluency up to about age nine. If they are still sounding out letters, tone is not today's battle — go back to Episodes 1 and 2.

## Family Activity

> **The Two Mark Cards — 8 minutes**
>
> 1. Make two cards: one with a **full stop**, one with a **?**.
> 2. Write a short sentence on a strip **with no mark at the end**.
> 3. Your child picks a card, puts it at the end, **then reads with the matching tone**.
> 4. Swap the card on **the same sentence** — if it doesn't work as a question, they explain why *(it has no question word)*.
> 5. **Swap roles:** they write, you read, **and read with the wrong tone on purpose** so they correct you.
>
> **Variation for an older child:** add the exclamation mark **!** and discuss: how is it different from a full stop?
