## The Question It Answers

> **"I read the word… so why do I read so slowly?"**

## Learning Objective

**One objective:** the child reads a three-sentence passage of words they already know, **without sounding out letter by letter**, and pauses at every full stop.

Criterion: reads the whole three-sentence passage without sounding out any word, with a pause at all three full stops, and at most one self-correction.

## New Vocabulary

| Word | How it is introduced |
|---|---|
| reading passage | three sentences that tell one thing |
| smoothly | with no choppiness and no sounding out |

## Mastery Criterion

```
Reads the whole three-sentence passage without sounding out any word letter by letter,
with an audible pause at all three full stops,
and at most one self-correction, unaided.
Reading time is not measured and is never shown to the child.
```

**Explicit decision:** **no timer and no words-per-minute.** Measuring reading speed creates anxiety and turns fluency into a race. The criterion is **the absence of sounding out** and **correct pausing**, not time.

## Parent Guide

> **What is your child learning?**
> **Fluency** — reading a known word in one go without sounding out, so their attention is freed for meaning. A child who sounds out every word reaches the end of a sentence and cannot recall its start; that is not slowness of mind but a full working memory.
>
> **The red line**
> **Do not time their reading with a clock, and do not interrupt them while reading.** If they get a word wrong, wait until they finish the sentence; many children self-correct from meaning. Immediate correction breaks the meaning and teaches fear of reading.
>
> **How to help?**
> **The second reading is the secret:** any passage is read twice, the first for meaning and the second for smoothness. And you read a sentence, then they read it after you — imitation carries the rhythm.
>
> **When to worry?**
> Fluency grows slowly to about age ten, and the spread between children here is very wide and normal. But an eight-year-old who sounds out **every** word, even daily repeated ones, is worth an assessment with their teacher or a learning specialist. **Early detection saves years, and the app does not diagnose.**

## Family Activity

> **Two Readings Per Passage — 10 minutes a day**
>
> 1. Choose a three-sentence passage from any book they know.
> 2. **First reading:** for meaning. Afterwards, one question only: "What happened?"
> 3. **Second reading:** for smoothness. Listen and never interrupt.
> 4. At each full stop they take a breath — make that an agreement between you.
> 5. **Swap:** you read and sound out a word on purpose, and let them tell you: "Read it in one go."
>
> **No clock and no counting.** The measure: did the second reading become smoother than the first?
>
> **Variation for an older child:** four sentences, with **one question** in them so they apply the tone from Episode 5.
