## The Question It Answers

> **"I hear the word… so how do I write it?"**

## Learning Objective

**One objective:** the child builds a word from three letters they know, in the correct order **and choosing the right letter for the sound in its place**.

Criterion: builds 4 of 5 three-letter words in the right order and spelling, unaided.

## New Vocabulary

| Word | How it is introduced |
|---|---|
| letter's place | where it sits: the start, the middle, or the end of the word |
| joins | sits right next to the letter after it, left to right |
| stands alone | a single sound written with one letter, no partner needed |

## Parent Guide

> **What is your child learning?**
> **Building the written word** — turning the sounds they hear into letters in order, and choosing the letter that fits the word. English spells the same sound with different letters depending on the word, and this is the first time your child meets that on purpose.
>
> **The red line**
> Do not teach the letter's name instead of its sound while building. "cee + ay + tee" does not produce "cat" in their head, but "c + a + t" does. Names are for talking about letters; sounds are for building.
>
> **How to help?**
> Write short words together on paper: pen · cat · sun · mat. Point to a letter and ask: "This is at the start of the word — what sound does it make?" Do not correct by erasing; correct by rebuilding.
>
> **When to worry?**
> Reversing the order of letters once or twice is completely normal at six and seven. **Constant reversal in every word** after long practice is worth a specialist's opinion. **The app does not diagnose.**

## Family Activity

> **The Letter Box — 10 minutes**
>
> 1. Write letters on small cards, **each letter on its own card**.
> 2. Your child draws three cards and tries to build a word.
> 3. At each letter they ask themselves: "Which place is it in?"
> 4. **Swap:** you build and put a wrong letter in on purpose, and they correct it and explain why.
>
> **Variation for an older child:** add letters that make the same sound (c/k, s/c) and call them "the tricky twins".

## Production Notes

- Each letter block is **one sound in a specific place**. Showing the wrong letter for a word's spelling is a teaching error and fails acceptance — do not depict it except in the deliberate mistake scene.
- The natural space beside a single-sound letter is drawn as a **normal space**, never as an error mark and never in a warning colour.
- Ryan makes one mistake in the episode, and he — not the narrator — fixes it.
- The letter is said by its sound in every place except at 4:12, where the name "pee" is mentioned once.
- 🔴 Certified English literacy review of grapheme choice and letter order — critical.
- No flashing > 3Hz · no timer · no interface text inside the images *(letter blocks are play data packaged per language)*.
