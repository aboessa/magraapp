## The Question It Answers

> **"What did I make? And how did I make it?"**

## Learning Objective

**One goal:** the child builds a tower of three blocks, then **names what they made and mentions one step of how they made it** — in two or three words.

Criterion: says what they made *("a tower")* and mentions one step *("I put a block on a block")* in 3 tries of 4, without the sentence being said to them first.

## 🔴 The Signature Decision: describing after making, not before

| ❌ rejected | ✅ the one used |
|---|---|
| "What will you make?" before building | 🔴 "**What did you make?**" after it |
| Nabil names the tower for the child | 🔴 **the child names it** after silence |
| "Say: I made a tower" | 🔴 **an open question** then silence |
| correcting the naming | 🔴 **any naming the child says is accepted** |

> 🔴 **Planning before doing is not this age's objective.**
>
> "What will you make?" is a question that needs imagining something that does not exist, and it is an **age 6–8 objective** and belongs in [I Think Step by Step](../series-bible-ufakkir-khutwa-khutwa.md) in "The Plan Comes First." Here: they build first, **then they tell**.
>
> 🔴 **And any naming the child says is accepted.** If they say "a house" not "a tower," they have named what they made — and the objective is **naming and describing**, not the correct word. And correcting the naming ends the talking entirely.
>
> ⚠️ **And one step is enough.** "I put a block on a block" is a complete description at this age, and describing all three steps in full is **an overload**.

## Parent Guide

> **What is your child learning?**
> To **make something with their hands, then tell about it**. The telling is half the skill: a child who says "I put a block on a block" owns **a language for their action**, and becomes able to explain, ask for help, and repeat what they did.
>
> **🔴 Ask after building, not before**
> "What will you make?" is a question that needs imagining something that does not exist, and it is an age 6–8 skill, not 3–5. So ask **"What did you make?"** after they finish.
>
> **🔴 And any naming they say is accepted**
> If they say "a house" not "a tower," they have named what they made — and this is the objective. **And correcting the naming ends the talking entirely**, and a four-year-old who is corrected twice stops describing.
>
> **🔴 And the silence is the most important thing you do**
> After the question, wait **at least four seconds**. Your child needs a second to understand the question, a second to look at what they built, and two seconds to find the words. And an adult who answers for them in those seconds prevents the answer.
>
> And if they say nothing, say your own sentence: "I see a block on a block." **Then be silent again** — the model after the chance, not before.
>
> **The red line**
> 🔴 **A tower of three blocks, no more, and on the floor or a low table.** No tower higher than their chest, no building above their head, no standing on a chair — 🔴 **and the tall tower falls on the child's face**.
>
> 🔴 **And no glue, no scissors, no small pieces, no string.**
>
> **How do you help them?**
> If the tower tilts, **do not comment and do not support it**. The tilt is part of building, and Nabil himself tilts it in the episode on purpose.
>
> **And name what they did at the end:** "You made it with your hands, and you told about it."

## Family Activity

> **A tower and a story — 10 minutes · 🔴 with an adult present**
>
> **Materials:** 3 wooden blocks or 3 closed cardboard boxes, each edge ≥ 6 cm · a space on the floor or a low table.
>
> 1. Sit on the floor with them, and put the three blocks scattered.
> 2. Say: "Build a tower of three." Then 🔴 **be silent for four seconds**.
> 3. If the tower tilts, 🔴 **do not support it and do not comment**. Wait until they do it again.
> 4. After they finish, ask: **"What did you make?"** Then 🔴 **be silent for four seconds**.
> 5. Then ask: **"And how did you make it?"** Then 🔴 **be silent for four seconds**.
> 6. 🔴 **Accept any answer**, even one word, even if they call it a house.
> 7. If they say nothing, say your own sentence: "I see a block on a block." **Then be silent again**.
> 8. Gather the blocks together: "Everything to its home."
>
> 🔴 **Steps 4 and 5 are the activity.** The building is a prelude to them.
>
> **Variation for the older child (5 years):** ask for **two steps** in the description, not one, or ask them to describe their build **to someone who did not see it**.
>
> ⚠️ **The conditions:** 🔴 three blocks, no more · on the floor or a low table · no chair · no tower higher than their chest · no glue or scissors · no small pieces or string · and the blocks are gathered after the activity.
