## The Question It Answers

> **"Where is this thing's home?"**

## Learning Objective

**One goal:** the child returns **four tools to their named homes** after hearing each home's name **once at the start of the episode**, without everything being named for them when they return it.

Criterion: returns 3 tools of 4 to their correct home after one naming at the start, without a reminder for each tool, in 3 tries of 4.

## 🔴 The Signature Decision: naming once at the start, not a reminder for each thing

The goal here is **not that they tidy up**, but that they **tidy without everything being named for them**.

| ❌ rejected phrasing | ✅ the adopted phrasing |
|---|---|
| "put the block in the basket. Now put the clip in the box." | 🔴 **"everything to its home"** after one naming |
| a reminder with each tool | 🔴 **one naming in scene 2** then silence |
| "tidy your room" | 🔴 **four things and four named homes** |
| new homes every episode | 🔴 **the same homes** in the episode and the activity |

> 🔴 **The difference between "put this here" and "everything to its home" is the whole episode.**
>
> The first measures **obedience**, and the second measures **memory and self-organization**. A child who has everything named for them does not build a habit, but waits for the naming.
>
> ⚠️ **And "tidy your room" is a rejected request at this age.** The room is not one task but dozens of tasks, and a four-year-old stands in the middle of it with no way in. **And four things and four homes** is one achievable task.
>
> 🔴 **And the homes are within reach** — a safety item and an educational item together: a high shelf needs a chair, and a chair needs an adult, and a habit that needs an adult does not become a habit.

## Parent Guide

> **What is your child learning?**
> To return things to their places **without everything being named for them**. This is the difference between a child who carries out an order and a child who builds a habit.
>
> **🔴 And the naming is once at the start**
> The most common error here is a reminder with each piece: "put this ... now this ... and this too." This measures **obedience** not organization, and the child learns to wait for the naming.
>
> So name the homes **once** at the start of the activity, then say "everything to its home" **and be silent**.
>
> **🔴 "Tidy your room" is a rejected request at this age**
> The room is dozens of tasks, not one task, and a four-year-old stands in the middle of it with no way in, so they freeze. **And four things and four homes** is a task they can finish and see the end of.
>
> **The red line**
> 🔴 **All the homes are within reach and at a low level.** No high shelf, no climbing on a chair, no box with a heavy lid or a hinge that snaps on their fingers. And nothing heavy to carry.
>
> 🔴 **And no small pieces, no string, no bag.**
>
> **How do you help them?**
> 🔴 **Fix the homes and do not change them.** A habit is built on a fixed place, and a changing place prevents it.
>
> And if they put a thing in another's home, **name the home and do not move it with your hands**: "This is the block's home." Then wait.
>
> **And start with four things only**, and add one each week.

## Family Activity

> **Four homes — 10 minutes · with an adult present**
>
> **Materials:** 4 of your child's large tools · and 4 "homes" **within their reach**: a basket on the floor · an open box · a low shelf · a bottom drawer.
>
> 1. Choose the homes **with your child**, all within their reach.
> 2. 🔴 **Name each home once**: "The block's home is the basket." Then the rest at the same rhythm.
> 3. Spread the four tools on the floor or a low table.
> 4. Say one sentence: "Everything to its home." Then 🔴 **be silent for four seconds**.
> 5. If they stop, repeat **the same sentence** — 🔴 **and do not name the thing or its home**.
> 6. At the end, name what they did: "You returned everything to its home."
> 7. 🔴 **Fix these four homes for a whole week** before you add anything.
>
> 🔴 **Step 7 is what turns the activity into a habit.** And changing homes prevents the habit.
>
> **Variation for the older child (5 years):** ask them to **name** the home of a fifth thing themselves, then keep to it.
>
> ⚠️ **The conditions:** 🔴 all homes within reach · no chair · no high shelf · no heavy lid or hinge · nothing heavy to carry · no small pieces · no string or bag.

## Production Notes

- 🔴 **The naming once in scene 2** · and it is not repeated for each tool · and this is **the core of the objective** not a shortening in the text.
- 🔴 **The request does not mention the home**: "return the tongs to their home" not "to the drawer."
- 🔴 **The four sentences in scene 2 at the same rhythm** · and any emphasis on one makes it the one memorized.
- 🔴 **Nabil leaves one tool** so the child has a real task, not an imitation.
- 🔴 **The homes are within reach** — a safety item **and a habit-building item**: what needs an adult does not become a habit.
- ⚠️ **Translation:** "its home" is chosen in each language with a **concrete word familiar to the child** · and it is not translated with an organizational term.
- ⚠️ **The game's homes = the episode's homes** · and changing them separates the measure from the habit.
