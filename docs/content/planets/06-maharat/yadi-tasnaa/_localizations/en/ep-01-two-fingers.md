## The Question It Answers

> **"How do I lift the thing with two fingers, not with my whole palm?"**

## Learning Objective

**One goal:** the child lifts a wooden clip **with the thumb and the index finger**, and places it on the basket's edge, without using the whole palm.

Criterion: lifts 4 clips of 5 with the thumb and index finger and places them on the edge, with verbal help not hand help, in two tries of three.

## 🔴 The Governing Safety Decision in This Episode

The pincer grip is trained in most content with **beads, legumes, and small wool balls**. They are the best trainers for it, **and the most dangerous thing to give a three-year-old**.

| ❌ rejected tool | The reason | ✅ the substitute used |
|---|---|---|
| beads · buttons | 🔴 swallowed | 🔴 **a wooden clip ≥ 7 cm** |
| legumes · pasta | 🔴 swallowed and inhaled | a wooden clip |
| small wool balls | 🔴 swallowed | a piece of sponge ≥ 8 cm |
| a button battery · a magnet | 🔴 grave danger | 🔴 **no substitute — absolutely forbidden** |

> 🔴 **We changed the tool and did not change the age.** The wooden clip forces **the same two fingers** and the same pressure, and its length prevents swallowing.
>
> **And it is better educationally too:** the clip needs pressure to open, so it trains strength with precision, and a bead is lifted with no pressure.

## Parent Guide

> **What is your child learning?**
> To lift the thing **with the thumb and the index finger**, not with the whole palm. This grip is the one they will need in two years to hold a pencil, button their shirt, and open their food bag.
>
> **The red line**
> 🔴 **No beads, no buttons, no legumes, no small wool balls, and nothing smaller than your child's palm.** These are common tools for training this skill, **and they are a real choking hazard at this age.**
>
> 🔴 **And never a button battery or a magnet**, no metal clip with a protruding spring, no scissors, no needle, nothing hot, no wire.
>
> **And the wooden clip is a better trainer educationally too:** it needs pressure to open, so it trains strength with precision.
>
> **How do you help them?**
> With words, not with your hand. Say "with two fingers" and point to your thumb, **and do not place their fingers yourself**. A child whose hand is moved does not learn the movement.
>
> And if the clip drops, **do not comment**. The drop is part of the training, and Nabil himself drops it in the episode on purpose.
>
> **And name what they did** rather than praise them: "You lifted it with two fingers" is more useful than "well done." The first gives them language for their action.
>
> **⚠️ After the activity**
> 🔴 **Gather all the clips and put them away out of reach.** And the episode is `required` supervision: 🔴 **do not show it to a child working alone.**

## Family Activity

> **The clip basket — 8 minutes · 🔴 with an adult present**
>
> **Materials:** 6 **wooden** clothes-clips ≥ 7 cm long · a basket or an open box · a towel.
>
> 1. Put the clips on a towel spread on the table — not on the floor.
> 2. 🔴 **Place your child's palm beside a clip** and say: "The clip is longer than your palm."
> 3. Give one request: "Take a clip with two fingers." Then **be silent for four seconds**.
> 4. Give the second: "Put it on the basket's edge." Then **be silent**.
> 5. Name what they did: "You lifted it with two fingers."
> 6. Repeat the round three times, then stop — 🔴 **even if they want to continue**.
>
> 🔴 **Step 3 is the activity.** The silence after the request is what makes them do it.
>
> **Variation for the older child (5 years):** ask for **two clips** with one hand, or a clip on the edge of a cup higher than the basket.
>
> ⚠️ **The conditions:** 🔴 wooden clips only · no small pieces on the table · away from any outlet · no water · no scissors · and the clips are gathered and put away after the activity.
