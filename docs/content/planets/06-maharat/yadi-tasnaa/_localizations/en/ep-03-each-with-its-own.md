## The Question It Answers

> **"Where do I put this? With which things does it look alike?"**

## Learning Objective

**One goal:** the child gathers six pieces into two baskets by **one attribute Nabil names** — the wood in a basket, and the cloth in a basket.

Criterion: sorts 5 pieces of 6 into the correct basket after hearing the attribute, in 3 tries of 4, without hand help.

## 🔴 The Signature Decision: one attribute named explicitly

| ❌ rejected | ✅ the one used |
|---|---|
| "tidy these" | 🔴 "**put the wood here, and the cloth here**" |
| two attributes together *(color and size)* | 🔴 **one attribute** |
| an attribute the child discovers | 🔴 **an attribute named before starting** |
| different colors that distract | 🔴 **the pieces are the same color as much as possible** |

> 🔴 **The attribute is named, not discovered, at this age.**
>
> "Tidy these" is an open request, and a three-year-old **sorts them by another correct attribute** — by size or by what they like — so they appear mistaken when they are not. And naming the attribute makes the task **measurable**, and makes the error a real error.
>
> 🔴 **And the color is kept uniform as much as possible on purpose**: if the wood were brown and the cloth blue, the child would sort **by color** and succeed without understanding the attribute. And pieces of a close color **force** attention to the named attribute.
>
> ⚠️ **And free discovery of the attribute** is the topic of [the World Around Us planet](../../08-alam/README.md) on an upper track, not this episode.

## Parent Guide

> **What is your child learning?**
> To gather things by **one attribute that is named for them**: the wood with the wood, and the cloth with the cloth. This is the first step in ordering, and it is a prelude to counting and to comparing.
>
> **🔴 Why do we name the attribute and not let them discover it?**
> Because "tidy these" is an open request, and your child will sort them by **another correct attribute** — by size, or by what they like — so they appear mistaken when they are not. And naming the attribute makes the task clear, and the error a real error that can be fixed.
>
> **🔴 And notice that the pieces are a close color**
> This is on purpose. If the wood were brown and the cloth blue, your child would sort **by color** and succeed without understanding the attribute.
>
> **The red line**
> 🔴 **No pieces smaller than your child's palm, no string, no ribbon, no bag.** And six pieces are enough — more produces chaos, not learning.
>
> **How do you help them?**
> Name the attribute **twice in the same wording**, then be silent. And if they put a piece in the wrong basket, **name it and do not move it with your hands**: "This is cloth." Then wait.
>
> **And one attribute per session.** Size, color, and texture together are an overload at this age.

## Family Activity

> **Two baskets and six pieces — 8 minutes · with an adult present**
>
> **Materials:** two baskets or open boxes · 3 wood pieces *(blocks · clips ≥ 7 cm)* · 3 cloth pieces ≥ 30 cm.
>
> 1. Spread a towel on the table, and put the six pieces on it **scattered**.
> 2. 🔴 **Name the attribute:** "The wood in this one, and the cloth in this one." And point to the two baskets.
> 3. Touch a wood piece and say "wood," then a cloth piece and say "cloth" — **the sound and the texture together**.
> 4. Give one request: "Put the wood with the wood." Then **be silent for four seconds**.
> 5. If they make a mistake, name only the piece: "This is cloth." 🔴 **And do not move it with your hands**.
> 6. At the end, name what they did: "You gathered the wood with the wood."
>
> 🔴 **Step 3 is what makes the attribute concrete.** The child distinguishes by texture and sound before they distinguish by the word.
>
> **Variation for the older child (5 years):** add a third basket **for a third named attribute** *(such as thick paper)*, or repeat the same pieces by another attribute: "The big here, and the smaller here."
>
> ⚠️ **The conditions:** six pieces, no more · all longer than the child's palm · no string or ribbon or bag · open baskets with no locking lids · and the pieces are gathered after the activity.

## Production Notes

- 🔴 **The pieces are a close color** — a measurement decision, not aesthetics · and any color palette that separates the two categories **is rejected**.
- 🔴 **The attribute is spoken twice in the same wording** · and it is not inferred at this age.
- 🔴 **The wood sound and the cloth sound** carry the attribute audibly · and they are the condition of "understood by sound alone."
- 🔴 **Two baskets only** · and the third is a variation in the activity, not in the episode.
- ⚠️ **Translation:** "wood" and "cloth" are replaced by two materials **locally familiar and of a close color** if needed · and the condition is that they are distinguished **by texture and sound**, not by color.
- ⚠️ **Linkage:** the same sorting skill is in [Colors Around Us](../../08-alam/README.md) by the color attribute · 🔴 **and the two episodes are not shown in one session** to avoid an attribute conflict.
