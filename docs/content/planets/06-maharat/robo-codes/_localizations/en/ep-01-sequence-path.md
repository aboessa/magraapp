## The Mission

Robo sits on a 4×4 grid. The goal is two tiles ahead and one tile to its left. Only one block is available: `move`.

## The Concept

**Sequence:** the order of commands changes the result.

## Learning Objective

**One goal:** the child understands that Robo runs the **written command, in order** — not the intention.

Criterion: reaches the goal on Level 1, and explains why Robo stopped on a wrong attempt, in 3 of 4 tries.

## Terms Introduced

| Term | Definition in the episode |
|---|---|
| **command** | one single thing Robo does |
| **sequence** | the order of commands from first to last |

---

## 🔴 The Signature Decision: failure from a "correct intention"

Failure in this episode is **not a wrong command** — it is correct commands in the **wrong order**.

| ❌ pedagogically rejected failure | ✅ the failure used |
|---|---|
| The child writes a meaningless command | 🔴 **all commands are correct** |
| Robo crashes because the program is empty | 🔴 Robo overshoots because the **turn came too late** |
| A spelling or technical error | 🔴 an error **of order alone** |

> 🔴 **If the failure came from a wrong command, the child would learn "check the commands."** If it comes from order, they learn **"check the sequence"** — which is the intended concept.
>
> The difference is practical: a child who understands that correct commands can produce a wrong result **reads their program as a sequence**, not as a list.

## 🔴 "What you mean" vs. "what you write"

This contrast is shown **twice** in the episode:

| Moment | Form |
|---|---|
| Scene 3 | The narrator states it: Robo runs what is written |
| Scene 5 | 🔴 It is **seen** — the commands are correct, and the result is wrong |

**Stating it alone is not enough.** The child is convinced by what they see, not by what they are told.

## Parent Guide

> **What is your child learning?**
> That a machine runs **what is written**, not **what is meant**. Robo took three steps because the program asked for three, and the goal was after two.
>
> **🔴 Why doesn't Robo talk?**
> On purpose. The common pattern in coding content is a cute robot that says "Oops, my mistake!" It is comforting, and **it ruins the lesson**: it makes the machine an agent to blame, so the child learns to look for the fault outside their own work.
>
> Robo is a **mirror**: it does exactly what it was told. So when it goes off course, no one is blamed, and one thing remains to review — **the command itself**.
>
> **🔴 And the failure in the episode is deliberate**
> The program fails first in every one of the six episodes. **The commands were all correct**, and the order is the mistake. Our message to your child: failure is a **step in doing things right**, not a verdict on their ability.
>
> **The four-word sequence**
> At the end of the episode they learn four words, repeated across the six episodes: **read · trace · locate · fix**.
>
> This is the opposite of what most beginners do: repeated random tweaking until it works by luck. **Success by luck teaches nothing.**
>
> **What this means for you in practice**
> If they come saying "it doesn't work," don't look at the screen first. Ask: "**What is the first command? And what happened after it?**"
>
> And if they want to delete everything and start over, ask: "**Which step first went off course?**" — the question itself is the lesson.
>
> **There is no single correct solution**
> If they reach the goal with seven commands and it could be done with three, **they still succeeded**. The extra star for the shorter solution is a **reward, not a requirement**, and there is never a penalty for the longer one.
>
> **The episode does not need your supervision** — it is designed for a child aged 9–12 working alone. And if they want to explain to you what they did, that is more useful than any question you could ask.

## Family Activity — optional

> **A human program — 10 minutes · no screen**
>
> 1. Your child writes commands on paper to move you around the room: **step forward · turn right · stop**.
> 2. 🔴 **You execute the commands literally, do not correct, and do not complete their intention.**
> 3. If they say "move forward" three times and there is a wall after two steps, **you stop at the wall** and do not turn.
> 4. They read their commands, locate the first command that went off course, and fix it themselves.
> 5. Run the round again **with fewer commands** — with no obligation.
>
