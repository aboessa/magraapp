## The Mission

An 8×8 grid. Robo must **collect three items** in three different corners, then reach the goal. The path to the third item **changes** depending on an obstacle's position.

## The Concept

**Decomposition:** splitting a big task into small tasks, and testing each one alone.

## Learning Objective

**One goal:** the child understands that a big project **is built and tested in stages**, not written whole then run.

Criterion: splits the task into three stages, runs after each stage, and completes Level 5 — in 3 of 4 tries.

## Terms Introduced

| Term | Definition in the episode |
|---|---|
| **decomposition** | turning a big task into small, independent tasks |
| **stage** | a part of the project that works and is tested on its own |

## Carried Over — all of it

**command · sequence · repetition · condition · function · pattern · logic error · debugging · the four-word sequence.**

🔴 **This episode introduces no new block.** All the tools are known, and what is new is **the way of working** with them.

---

## 🔴 Why a closing episode with no new tool?

| ❌ rejected choice | ✅ the adopted choice |
|---|---|
| a sixth new block | 🔴 **no new block** |
| an additional programming concept | 🔴 **a practical concept**: how you work |
| a bigger project with more tools | a bigger project **with the same tools** |
| a cumulative test of the concepts | 🔴 **composing** the concepts into one work |

> 🔴 **A child who finished five episodes knows five tools, and does not know how to start a big task.**
>
> And this is a real gap: they sit in front of an 8×8 grid with three items, write twenty blocks then run, fail, **and do not know where the error is among twenty possibilities**.
>
> The episode teaches them to **split and test**. And this is the most useful thing a beginning programmer is given, and the most neglected in teaching children.

## 🔴 The Signature Decision: planning on paper before the blocks

| The stage | The tool |
|---|---|
| 1. understanding the task | 🔴 **looking at the grid and counting** |
| 2. splitting | 🔴 **paper and pencil** — no screen |
| 3. building | blocks, one stage |
| 4. testing | run after each stage |
| 5. composing | joining the stages |

> 🔴 **Stages 1 and 2 have no blocks at all.** And the episode shows **a real sheet of paper** on which the path is drawn and the three stages are written.
>
> The reason is that screen tools push for immediate writing. The paper **slows** the work by a useful amount, and makes the plan visible before it becomes code.
>
> ⚠️ **And this is the only stage in the series where the child might use paper** — which is why the episode states explicitly that it is **optional**: whoever can plan mentally may do so.

## 🔴 The testing rule: run after each stage

| ❌ rejected method | ✅ the taught method |
|---|---|
| write twenty blocks then run | 🔴 **write six, run, then continue** |
| one error among twenty possibilities | one error among **six** |
| "it doesn't work" | "the second stage doesn't work" |
| rewriting everything | fixing **one stage** |

🔴 **This is the same rule as** [episode 2](./ep-02-debug-path.md) *(one edit)* **applied to building rather than fixing:** reduce the number of possibilities before you have to search through them.

```
31. The yes branch: it passes and arrives. Complete.
32. And the no branch: it turns, and advances two tiles.
33. And the distance to the goal is three tiles.
34. An off-by-one, as in the repetition episode.
35. We changed the number alone. Robo arrived.
36. Decomposition is not only in programming.
37. A big task overwhelms you when you look at it whole.
38. So you start with one part, finish it, then look.
    [pause]
39. And if you make a mistake, you know in which part you made it.
40. This is decomposition.
    [pause]
41. And it is the same idea as the experiment on the Science planet: you change one thing, and you measure.
42. Today's challenge is a full project: three items and a changing obstacle.
43. Write one stage, run, then move on.
    [pause]
44. And you have everything you learned: sequence, repetition, condition, and function.
45. And one method: read, trace, locate, and fix.
```

## Parent Guide

> **What is your child learning?**
> That a big project **is built and tested in stages**, and is not written whole then run. This is called **decomposition**.
>
> **🔴 And the episode introduces no new tool on purpose**
> Your child now knows five tools: sequence, repetition, condition, function, and debugging. **What was missing is how they start.**
>
> And this is a real gap: they sit in front of a big task, write twenty commands then run, fail, **and do not know where the error is among twenty possibilities**. So they despair or delete everything.
>
> **🔴 And the benefit of decomposition is not that the project becomes easier**
> The number of commands is the same, and the path is the same. **What changes is that the error becomes findable.**
>
> In the episode, the third stage failed. And the narrator said: "**But we know where the problem is**" — because the first and second stages were tested and passed a minute earlier. So the search was in **six commands, not twenty**.
>
> This difference is the whole episode, and it is the most useful thing a beginning programmer is given.
>
> **🔴 And the paper is optional**
> The episode shows planning on paper before touching the blocks, **and says explicitly it is optional**. Whoever plans mentally with efficiency may do so. We do not want the tool to become a burden.
>
> **What this means for you in practice**
> If they come to you with a project that does not work, ask one question: "**Which stage was the last one you tested and it passed?**"
>
> If they haven't tested anything, then the answer is the lesson: **they did not split**. And do not fix the program for them — ask them to keep the first stage alone and run.
>
> **And the error in the episode is an old error**
> "off-by-one" — the same as the repetition episode's error. And the episode names it as such explicitly, so your child knows that errors are **known types that recur**, not endless surprises.
>
> **🔴 And this is the last episode in the series, and there is no ceremony**
> No "you graduated," no "you became a programmer," no celebration. The last sentence is the same sentence they heard six times: **read, trace, locate, and fix.**
>
> On purpose. The skill is more important than the title, and an eleven-year-old knows the difference.

## Family Activity — optional

> **A task with three stages — 15 minutes · no screen**
>
> 1. Choose a real, medium home task *(organizing a bookshelf · packing a trip bag)*.
> 2. 🔴 **Before starting**, your child writes **three stages** on a sheet of paper.
> 3. They do the first stage, then **stop and look**: was it done as they wanted?
> 4. Then the second, then stop and look. Then the third.
> 5. 🔴 **They do not start a stage before checking the one before it.**
> 6. At the end, ask them: "If an error appeared now, **in which stage would you look?**"
>
> 🔴 **Steps 3 and 5 are the activity.** Stopping to check is the skill, not splitting alone.
>
> 🔴 **And do not interfere in choosing the stages** even if their split is not optimal. A non-optimal split **works**, and your split teaches them nothing.
>
> **And the sixth question links the activity to the episode**: splitting creates **a map to search** before you need it.
>
> ⚠️ A **safe** task: no climbing on chairs · no heavy objects · no glass.
