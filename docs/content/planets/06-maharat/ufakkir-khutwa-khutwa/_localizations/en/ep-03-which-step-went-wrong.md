## The Question It Answers

> **"The result is different — so which step is the cause?"**

## Learning Objective

**One goal:** the child identifies **the single step** that made the result of a five-step sequence different from what was required, and fixes it **alone**.

Criterion: identifies the wrong step in a five-step sequence and fixes it alone without rewriting the sequence, in 3 tries of 4.

## 🔴 The Signature Decision: "the wrong step" not "the error"

| ❌ rejected phrasing | ✅ the adopted phrasing |
|---|---|
| "Where is the error?" | 🔴 "**Which is the wrong step?**" |
| "You made a mistake" | 🔴 "**The third step is the cause**" |
| "The tower is wrong" | "The tower is different from what was required" |
| "Correct everything" | 🔴 "**Fix one step**" |

> 🔴 **The difference between "the error" and "the wrong step" is not softening the wording.**
>
> "The error" is **a general state** with no place, and a child who hears it redoes all the work or gives up. **And "the wrong step" is a specific place**, and it is by nature **fixable**.
>
> ⚠️ **And one step, not two.** The sequence in this episode has **only one error**, because two errors together make the diagnosis a guess.
>
> 🔴 **And the fix is one step, and it is half the goal.** A child who finds the step then rewrites the five **learned nothing** about diagnosis.

## 🔴 And the wrong step is a step that is **correctly worded**

The wrong step in this episode is not vague or missing, but **clear and linguistically correct**: "put the blue block **beside** the yellow." And what was required was **above** it.

> 🔴 **If the step were vague, the child would learn "write clearly" — which is episode 1's lesson.** And here the lesson is **diagnosis**: a fully understood step that does something other than what was required.

## Parent Guide

> **What is your child learning?**
> To find **the single step** that made the result different, and fix it **alone**. This is the opposite of what most children do: they demolish all the work and start over.
>
> **🔴 And we say "the wrong step" not "the error"**
> And the difference is not softening: "the error" is a general state with no place, and a child who hears it redoes all the work or gives up. But "the wrong step" **has a number and a place**, and what has a place is fixed on its own.
>
> **🔴 And the wrong step in the episode is a fully understood step**
> "Put the green **beside** the blue" — clear, and linguistically correct, and it does something other than what was required. If it were vague, the lesson would be "write clearly," which is the first episode's lesson.
>
> **🔴 And notice that the two steps after it were executed without objection**
> This is deliberate, so your child learns to look for the **first deviation**, not the last step. And "the error is in the last one" is the most common guess at this age, because it is the last thing they saw.
>
> **The red line**
> 🔴 **The tower is no higher than your child's chest**, and is built on the floor or a low table, with no standing on a chair. No scissors, no glue. 🔴 **And no device**.
>
> **How do you help them?**
> Ask one question: **"Which step does not lead to the goal?"** And do not say its number.
>
> And if they want to rewrite all the steps, ask them: **"How many words do you need to swap?"** — 🔴 **the question itself is the lesson.**

## Family Activity

> **One step is fixed — 12 minutes · no device**
>
> **Materials:** 5 blocks or closed cardboard boxes · a sheet · a pencil.
>
> 1. **You** build a tower **with five pieces** on the floor, and draw it or picture it mentally as a goal.
> 2. Write **five steps** to reach it, 🔴 **and put one error in one step** — a single word: "beside" instead of "above."
> 3. Demolish the tower, and give your child the steps and the goal picture.
> 4. They carry out the steps **as they are written**, and do not correct while executing.
> 5. They compare their result to the goal, and ask: **"Which step does not lead to it?"**
> 6. 🔴 **They swap only one word** in that step, and rebuild.
> 7. Swap roles: they write the steps with one error, and you execute.
>
> 🔴 **Step 6 is the activity.** Rewriting the five steps cancels it.
>
> 🔴 **And only one error** per round. Two errors together make the diagnosis a guess.
>
> **Variation for the older child (8 years):** six steps, with the error in the **second step** — so they learn that an earlier deviation produces a bigger difference.
>
> ⚠️ **The conditions:** 🔴 the tower shorter than their chest · on the floor or a low table · no chair · no small pieces · no scissors or glue · no device.

## Production Notes

- 🔴 **One error in the sequence** · not two · else the diagnosis becomes a guess.
- 🔴 **The wrong step is clearly worded and linguistically correct** · vagueness is episode 1's lesson, not this one.
- 🔴 **The two steps after the error are executed in an ordinary tone** · this is a condition of acceptance: it prevents "the error is in the last one."
- 🔴 **The fix is swapping one word** · and the other cards are not touched in the shot.
- 🔴 **The block colors are spoken with their position in the order** — for color blindness.
- 🔴 **`asset-step-fk-read/-find/-fix` are shared assets** appearing in the six episodes in the same form.
- ⚠️ **Translation:** "the wrong step" is phrased in each language with a construction that **carries the place**, not a general state · and any translation producing an abstract "error" **is rejected**.
- ⚠️ **Computational-thinking review** on the "first deviation" principle in its age-appropriate form.
