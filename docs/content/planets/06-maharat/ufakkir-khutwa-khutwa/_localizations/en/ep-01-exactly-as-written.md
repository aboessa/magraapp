## The Question It Answers

> **"Did I carry out what is written, or what I imagined?"**

## Learning Objective

**One goal:** the child carries out **four written steps in order**, and **says where the execution went off** from what was written when shown an execution with an extra step.

Criterion: carries out four pictured steps in order, and points to the extra step in a shown execution, in 3 tries of 4.

## 🔴 The Signature Decision: the deviation by addition, not omission

| ❌ rejected failure | ✅ the failure used |
|---|---|
| the hand **forgets** a step | 🔴 the hand **adds** a step that was not written |
| a written step is wrong | 🔴 **all four steps are correct** |
| the hand does not understand the step | the hand carries out **and adds of its own** |

> 🔴 **Omission is easily noticed by the child, and addition is not.**
>
> The child compares the result to the steps, so they see what is missing. **But addition looks like an improvement**, and this is exactly what the child does themselves: reads three steps, draws a fourth of their own, and thinks they executed.
>
> **That is why the deviation in this episode is an addition**: a door that was not asked for in the square. And the question is "what is extra?" not "what is missing?".
>
> ⚠️ **And no blame for the addition.** The narrator says "this is extra" not "this is wrong" — the difference is that "extra" is **information about the execution**, and "wrong" is a verdict on the one executing.

## Parent Guide

> **What is your child learning?**
> To carry out **what is written, not what they imagined**, and to compare the result to the steps to find the difference. This is the first skill in computational thinking, and it appears in everything after it: solving a problem, assembling a toy, and following a recipe.
>
> **🔴 And the deviation in the episode is an addition, not an omission**
> The hand draws **a door that was not asked for**. Omission is easily noticed by your child, but addition **looks like an improvement** — and it is exactly what they do themselves: they read three steps, add a fourth of their own, and think they executed.
>
> **🔴 And we do not say "wrong" but "extra"**
> The difference matters: "extra" is **information about the execution**, and "wrong" is a verdict on the one executing. A child who hears the first reviews their work, and one who hears the second defends themselves.
>
> **The red line**
> 🔴 **Paper and pencil only. No scissors, no glue, no craft knife.** And the pencil is not brought near the face.
>
> 🔴 **And no device.** This whole series is without a computer, tablet, or phone, and this is an educational decision: the concept is built with paper and movement **before** it is met in any editor.
>
> **How do you help them?**
> In the activity, **carry out their steps literally and do not complete their intention**. If they wrote "draw a square" and did not say its size, draw any square. And every time you understand what they meant, **you cancel the lesson**.
>
> And do not correct their steps while they write. Let them execute them and then see the result — 🔴 **seeing is more useful than correcting**.

## Family Activity

> **Write three steps — 12 minutes · no device**
>
> **Materials:** two sheets · two pencils · a table.
>
> 1. Your child writes or draws **three steps** for a simple drawing, one step per line.
> 2. They give you the sheet, **and do not explain anything aloud**.
> 3. 🔴 **You carry out the steps literally**, do not complete their intention, and do not ask.
> 4. You place your drawing next to their steps, **and they compare**.
> 5. They ask themselves one question: **"Which step was unclear?"**
> 6. They rewrite **that step alone** — not all three.
> 7. You execute again.
>
> 🔴 **Steps 5 and 6 are the activity.** Rewriting all the steps is the opposite of the skill: the fix is **one step**.
>
> 🔴 **And the only condition: do not understand their intention.** You are a literal executor in these minutes.
>
> **Variation for the older child (8 years):** four steps, executed by **someone who did not hear the conversation**.
>
> ⚠️ **The conditions:** paper and pencil only · no scissors or glue · no device · at a table · and the pencil away from the face.
