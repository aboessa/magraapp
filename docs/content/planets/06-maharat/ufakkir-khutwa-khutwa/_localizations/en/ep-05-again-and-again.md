## The Question It Answers

> **"I wrote the same steps four times — isn't there a shorter way?"**

## Learning Objective

**One goal:** the child replaces **a group of steps that returns as-is** by writing it **once with the number of times**, and knows that the number of **actions** did not change.

Criterion: finds the repeating group in 12 cards, writes it once with its count, and says that the number of actions did not decrease — in 3 tries of 4.

## 🔴 The Signature Decision: repetition shortens the writing, not the work

| ❌ misconception | ✅ the adopted phrasing |
|---|---|
| "repetition shortens the work" | 🔴 "**it shortens the writing, and the work is the same**" |
| "repetition makes it faster" | 🔴 "**the same number of actions, and fewer cards**" |
| "shorter is better" | "shorter is **easier to read**, and the longer is correct" |

> 🔴 **"Repetition makes the work faster" is the most common misconception on this topic** — and it is the fourth misconception that [the Robo Codes series](../series-bible-robo-codes.md#7--common-misconceptions--and-preventing-them) prevents on an upper track.
>
> **And we prevent it here with the visible count:** the actions are counted before *(12)*, and counted after *(12)*, **and both are shown on the screen**. And the number of cards drops from 12 to 4, **and the number of actions does not drop**.
>
> ⚠️ **And the difference is not a detail:** a child who thinks repetition shortens the work **expects a result that does not happen**, so they think they made a mistake. And one who knows it shortens the writing **understands why we use it**: because what is written is read and fixed, and the long form hides the wrong step.

## 🔴 And without `repeat` and without a symbol

| what we use | what we prevent |
|---|---|
| a card that says **"four times"** in words | 🔴 `repeat 4` |
| a paper bracket around the three steps | 🔴 a programming block or a loop drawn as a circular arrow |
| the word **repetition** | 🔴 the word "loop" |

🔴 **"loop" is forbidden in this series**, it is [Robo Codes](../README.md#terms--a-fixed-contract)' term on the 9–12 track. The child here learns **the idea** without its technical term.

## Parent Guide

> **What is your child learning?**
> To find **a group of steps that returns as-is**, and write it once with the number of times. This is the idea of repetition, **without any programming symbol**.
>
> **🔴 And the most important part of the episode is that repetition does not shorten the work**
> "Repetition makes it faster" is the most common misconception on this topic. And the episode prevents it **with the visible count**: twelve actions before, and twelve after. And the cards dropped from 12 to 4, **and the actions did not drop**.
>
> And the difference is not a detail: a child who thinks repetition shortens the work **expects a result that does not happen**, so they think they made a mistake.
>
> **🔴 So why do we use it then?**
> Because the repeated writing **hides the error**. And in twelve cards you need a search to find the different card, and in four there is no room for it. And the answer is **practical, not aesthetic**.
>
> **🔴 And the longer is correct**
> If your child writes all twelve steps, **they succeeded**. And the shorter writing is **easier to read**, and there is never a penalty for the longer.
>
> **The red line**
> 🔴 **Paper and pencil only. No scissors, no glue. And no device.**
>
> **How do you help them?**
> Ask one question: **"What returns as-is?"** And do not point to the rows.
>
> And when they count their hand's actions and find they did not decrease, **do not explain immediately**. Ask them: **"And did you expect this?"** — 🔴 **the surprise itself is the lesson.**

## Family Activity

> **Six stars and one card — 12 minutes · no device**
>
> **Materials:** two sheets · a pencil · 12 small slips of paper.
>
> 1. Your child draws **six similar stars** in a row on a sheet.
> 2. They write the steps of **one star** only — three steps, say.
> 3. They write beside it one card: **"six times."**
> 4. 🔴 **They count their hand's actions aloud** as they draw the six stars.
> 5. They compare: **how many cards did they write? And how many actions did they draw?**
> 6. They write one sentence at the bottom of the sheet: **what decreased, and what did not decrease?**
> 7. 🔴 **You introduce one difference** into one of the six stars, and they search for it.
> 8. They try: would they have found it faster if the steps had been written **once**?
>
> 🔴 **Steps 5 and 6 are the activity** · and steps 7 and 8 answer the "why."
>
> **Variation for the older child (8 years):** a pattern with **two different groups** that repeat — like a star then a square, three times.
>
> ⚠️ **The conditions:** paper and pencil only · no scissors or glue · the pencil away from the face · no device.

## Production Notes

- 🔴 **The visible counter is mandatory**, shown 12 before and 12 after · and the misconception is prevented by the count, not by words.
- 🔴 **Without `repeat`, without a circular arrow, and without the word "loop"** · the bracket is paper and the card is in words.
- 🔴 **The failure in this episode is a failure of expectation, not of execution** · and it is deliberate.
- 🔴 **"The longer is correct" is a mandatory sentence** · and no penalty for writing all twelve.
- 🔴 **The pack is rejected if it shows a step-saving message**.
- ⚠️ **Translation:** the "four times" card is phrased in each language **in words not a symbol**, and "repetition" is not translated with the equivalent of the technical "loop."
- ⚠️ **Computational-thinking review** on distinguishing the number of commands from the number of actions, and on the rationale for "why we use repetition."
