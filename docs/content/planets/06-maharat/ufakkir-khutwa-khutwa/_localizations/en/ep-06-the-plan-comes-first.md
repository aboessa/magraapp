## The Question It Answers

> **"Why do I write what I will do, when I know it in my head?"**

## Learning Objective

**One goal:** the child writes **their plan before they do**, then executes, then **compares the paper to what happened** and names one difference.

Criterion: writes a four-step plan before executing, then executes, then names one difference between their plan and what happened — in 3 tries of 4.

## 🔴 The Signature Decision: the plan is to be compared, not obeyed

| ❌ rejected phrasing | ✅ the adopted phrasing |
|---|---|
| "stick to your plan" | 🔴 "**compare your plan to what happened**" |
| "a good plan does not change" | 🔴 "**the plan changes, and we know where it changed**" |
| "your plan was wrong" | "here the paper differed from the hand" |
| the plan is a promise | 🔴 **the plan is a record** |

> 🔴 **This is the most important decision in the episode, and the most commonly mistaken in teaching planning to children.**
>
> A child who learns that the plan **is obeyed** is afraid to write it, because every deviation becomes a failure. And a child who learns that it **is compared** writes it without fear, **because it is a tool for looking, not a contract.**
>
> **And the real benefit is in the comparison:** without a paper written before, the child does not know **where** their work changed — they only know that the result is different. And the paper gives them **the place of the difference**, which is what they learned in [episode 3](./ep-03-which-step-went-wrong.md).
>
> ⚠️ **And the episode is not measured by sticking to the plan.** The criterion is **naming one difference**, and one who executes their plan literally and finds no difference **is asked about a step that took longer** — the difference is not in the steps alone.

## Parent Guide

> **What is your child learning?**
> To write their steps **before** they do, then do, then **compare the paper to what happened**.
>
> **🔴 And the plan is compared, not obeyed**
> This is the most important part of the episode, and the most commonly mistaken. A child who learns that the plan **is obeyed** is afraid to write it, because every deviation becomes a failure. And one who learns that it **is compared** writes it without fear.
>
> **And the benefit is in the comparison:** without a paper written before, your child does not know **where** their work changed — they only know the result is different. And the paper gives them **the place of the difference**.
>
> **🔴 And do not measure the episode by sticking to it**
> The requirement is that they name **one difference**. And if they executed their plan literally and found no difference, ask them: **"Which step took longer than you expected?"** — the difference is not in the steps alone.
>
> **🔴 And they do not tear up the paper**
> A child tends to hide a plan that differed. And that is the exact opposite of the purpose: **the paper is useful because it differed.**
>
> **The red line**
> 🔴 **Building on the floor or a low table, and shorter than their chest, with no standing on a chair.** No scissors, no glue. 🔴 **And no device**.
>
> **How do you help them?**
> Read their plan with them **after** executing, not before, and do not correct it while they write. And when their work differs from it, do not say "your plan was incomplete." Say: **"Where did the paper differ from your hand?"**
>
> **And this is the last episode in the series.** And your child now knows: the step, the order, the wrong step, decomposition, repetition, and the plan. 🔴 **And all of these are without a screen** — and they are what will make programming, after two years, **a naming of things they know.**

## Family Activity

> **A paper before, and a paper after — 15 minutes · no device**
>
> **Materials:** two sheets · a pencil · 5 blocks or closed cardboard boxes.
>
> 1. Your child chooses **a small task** with five pieces, with no goal picture — 🔴 **the goal is in their head**.
> 2. They write **a four-step plan** on the first sheet. And they do not touch the pieces yet.
> 3. 🔴 **You read the plan and do not correct it.**
> 4. They execute, **with the paper open beside them**.
> 5. After they finish, they write on the second sheet: **what actually happened**.
> 6. They place the two sheets side by side, and name 🔴 **only one difference**.
> 7. If they find no difference, ask them: **"Which step took longer?"**
> 8. 🔴 **They keep the two sheets, and do not tear them up.**
>
> 🔴 **Step 6 is the activity** · and step 8 is what makes it a habit.
>
> **Variation for the older child (8 years):** they write in their plan **one backup step**: "and if it tilts, support it" — and this is the first form of a condition, 🔴 **with no symbol and no word 'condition.'**
>
> ⚠️ **The conditions:** 🔴 building shorter than their chest · on the floor or a low table · no chair · no small pieces · no scissors or glue · no device.

## Production Notes

- 🔴 **The sentence "not to stick to it" at second 23** · and its place at the start of the episode is a **condition of acceptance**, not a detail.
- 🔴 **No goal picture in this episode alone** · for the plan becomes a plan when there is no model to copy.
- 🔴 **The deviation is a correct decision in its moment** *(supporting the tilting piece)* · and no blame on the plan or the hand.
- 🔴 **"The plan is a paper, not a promise" is said twice** — in scene 3 and in the closing, in the same wording.
- 🔴 **The pack does not give stars for sticking** · and it measures **the place of the difference** only.
