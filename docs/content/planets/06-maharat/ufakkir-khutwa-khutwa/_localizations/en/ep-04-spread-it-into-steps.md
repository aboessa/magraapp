## The Question It Answers

> **"'Tidy your bag' — where do I start?"**

## Learning Objective

**One goal:** the child breaks down **one task from their life into four ordered steps**, and distinguishes a step that **does not belong** to the task.

Criterion: spreads one task into 4 ordered steps, and excludes a step that does not belong to it, in 3 tries of 4.

## 🔴 The Signature Decision: the big task paralyzes, it does not merely make things hard

| ❌ rejected phrasing | ✅ the adopted phrasing |
|---|---|
| "tidy your bag" | 🔴 "**four steps to tidy the bag**" |
| "the big task is hard" | 🔴 "**a big task with no steps has no way in**" |
| "be organized" | "spread it into steps, then start with the first" |
| ten steps | 🔴 **four** |

> 🔴 **The big request does not tire the child, it paralyzes them.**
>
> "Tidy your bag" is not hard, but **has no way in**: they do not know where to start, so they stand still or move without a system. And decomposition gives them **the first step**, which is all they need to begin.
>
> ⚠️ **And four steps, not ten.** Over-decomposition produces a list that burdens rather than helps, a common error in teaching planning to children.

## 🔴 And the second half: a step that does not belong

In this episode six cards are shown, **four of them are part of the task and two are not** *(drink water · play for two minutes)*.

> 🔴 **And this is what makes decomposition a skill, not a narration.**
>
> A child who spreads a task into steps usually adds **what is not part of it**: they begin tidying the bag, find a toy, and play. And recognizing "this is not part of the task" is **half of decomposition**, and it is measured by a `sort_bins` pack, not by ordering.
>
> ⚠️ **And no blame for the two extra cards.** The narrator says "not part of this task" not "this is wrong" — playing and drinking **are not wrong**, they are just not part of this task.

## Parent Guide

> **What is your child learning?**
> To spread one task into **four steps**, and to know which step is not part of the task.
>
> **🔴 And "tidy your bag" does not tire your child, it paralyzes them**
> It is not hard, but **has no way in**: they do not know where to start, so they stand or move without a system. And decomposition gives them **the first step**, which is all they need to begin.
>
> So change the request: instead of "tidy your bag," say **"what is the first step in tidying the bag?"**
>
> **🔴 And four steps, not ten**
> Over-decomposition produces a list that burdens rather than helps. And this is a common error: it is assumed that more detail is more useful, and it is not, at this age.
>
> **🔴 And playing is not wrong**
> In the episode there are two cards that do not belong to the task: "drink water" and "play for two minutes." And the narrator says of them **"they are not wrong, and not part of it."** We do not teach your child that playing is wrong, but that they know **the boundaries of the task**.
>
> **The red line**
> 🔴 **No scissors, no open metal sharpener, and nothing sharp in the bag during the activity.** And the bag is not carried full or lifted onto the back. 🔴 **And no device**.
>
> **How do you help them?**
> Ask about **the first step only**, and do not ask for the whole list. A child who has started usually continues on their own.
>
> And if they write ten steps, ask them: **"Which four of them are enough?"** — 🔴 **condensing is a skill like detailing.**

## Family Activity

> **Four cards for one task — 12 minutes · no device**
>
> **Materials:** 6 small slips of paper · a pencil · the real task you will decompose *(tidying the bag · setting the table · organizing the shelf)*.
>
> 1. Choose **one task** your child does every day.
> 2. They write or draw **six cards**: four from the task, 🔴 **and two not part of it** — they choose them.
> 3. They mix the six cards.
> 4. They sort: 🔴 **what is part of the task, and what is not.**
> 5. They order the remaining four from first to last.
> 6. **They actually do the task** in the order they wrote.
> 7. They ask themselves: **"Did a step go missing?"** — and if so, they add one card.
> 8. Swap roles: you write six cards, and they sort and order.
>
> 🔴 **Step 4 is half the activity** — omitting it makes it an ordering, not a decomposition.
>
> **Variation for the older child (8 years):** a task of only **three** steps — 🔴 **and condensing is harder than detailing**.
>
> ⚠️ **The conditions:** no scissors or glue · no open sharpener or anything sharp in the bag · the bag not lifted onto the back or carried full · no device.

## Production Notes

- 🔴 **Four steps, no more** in the episode, the pack, and the activity.
- 🔴 **Two cards do not belong, and with no negative mark** · and the bin name is "not part of this task" not "wrong."
- 🔴 **The sentence "The task did not get smaller. But it has a way in."** is a condition of acceptance — it prevents a false promise.
- 🔴 **The failure from a step omitted in the ordering** not from a wrong step · and the episode gathers the three prior ones.
- ⚠️ **Translation:** the second bin's name **is not translated with the equivalent of "wrong"** in any language · and any translation doing so **is rejected**.
- ⚠️ **Translation:** the bag example is reviewed per market to stay familiar · and the condition is that it is a task **the child does daily themselves**.
- ⚠️ **A pedagogical review** on the four-step limit and on the phrasing "not part of it."
