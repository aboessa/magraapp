## The Question

> **"How do I describe a route to someone who does not know my neighborhood?"**

## Learning Objective

**One goal:** describes a route on a map **with a distance read from the scale and a cardinal direction**.

Criterion: describes a three-stage route, giving each stage **a distance read from the scale and a cardinal direction**, in 3 of 4 tries, with no help.

## New Vocabulary

| Word | Presented as |
|---|---|
| map scale | "how much real distance a small length on the paper stands for" |
| cardinal directions | north, south, east, and west — four directions everyone knows |
| north arrow | "the mark that tells you where north is on this sheet" |

## 🔴 The Truth Contract in This Episode

| Item | Application |
|---|---|
| Places | **Zanbaqan · the Brooks Quarter · the Pearl River · Rocky Plateau** — 🔴 all invented |
| Numbers | 🔴 **all illustrative** · and the scale in the episode: **one finger on the map = a ten-minute walk** |
| Tag | 🔴 `asset-aalami-tag-illustrative` with the first number · and the narrator says "in our invented world" |
| Real distances | 🔴 **none** — and the distance in the episode is **relative**: "a ten-minute walk" |
| Units | 🔴 **no kilometer and no mile** — [the localization rule](../series-bible-aalami-akbar.md#12-الترجمة-والتوطين) |

> 🔴 **Editorial decision: the scale in this episode is relative, not metric.**
>
> For two reasons. First, the metric or imperial unit is a **localization decision**, and the episode works in every market if the scale is relative. Second, and more important, it is **pedagogical**: map scale is a **ratio**, not a unit. A child who understands "one finger = ten minutes' walk" **understands the ratio**, and can use any unit later.
>
> ⚠️ **And the scale in centimeters is presented in the "Limits" part** as what real maps do, with no application.

### Pronunciation Notes

| # | Note |
|---|---|
| 1 | 🔴 clear, explanatory, **not dramatic** · ~130 wpm |
| 2 | 🔴 a real question · and written on screen with it |
| 3 | 🔴 "**for someone who knows the quarter**" with stress — the description is not wrong, it is private |
| 4 | 🔴 **the episode's key line** · slowly |
| 5 | 🔴 "**in our invented world**" clearly — a truth item, with the visual tag |
| 6 | 🔴 **the takeaway of the first concept** · at double the slowness |
| 7 | 🔴 slowly — and the visual proof with it |
| 8 | 🔴 **the moment the two concepts meet** · a pause before it |
| 9 | 🔴 a real question · then **total silence, 6 seconds** with no music |
| 10 | 🔴 "**not the first one's**" with stress — the most common error at this age |
| 11 | 🔴 a tone of **confidence, not apology** — the Limits part is not a belittling |
| 12 | 🔴 "and we did not go into it today" — with no embarrassment |

## Parent Guide

> **What is your child learning?**
> To describe a route **by distance and direction**, not by landmarks. This is a skill that starts with maps and ends at every precise description: **information understood by someone who does not share your experience.**
>
> **The central idea in one sentence**
> **The landmark is known to you alone. Distance and direction are known to everyone.**
>
> **🔴 Why not "right" and "left"?**
> Because "your right" is **information about you, not about the place**, and it changes every time you turn. The episode shows two people facing each other, and each one's right is on a different side. But north does not change for anyone.
>
> **🔴 The error the episode targets**
> That the child thinks **the scale is fixed on every map**. That is why the episode uses two maps with two different scales, and the second question asks about it explicitly. **And whoever measured without reading the scale first, measured with no meaning.**
>
> **🔴 All the places and numbers in this series are invented**
> Zanbaqan, the Brooks Quarter, and Rocky Plateau are **invented places**, and the scale in them is **relative**: "this bar equals a ten-minute walk." There is not a single real number in the episode, **and every number appears with an "illustrative number" tag.** This is deliberate: we teach the **principle**, and do not hand your child a number to repeat in class.
>
> **And why not kilometers?**
> Because map scale is a **ratio**, not a unit. Whoever understood "one finger = ten minutes' walk" understood the ratio, **and can then use any unit.** Units come in school mathematics and geography.
>
> **The "Limits of This Episode" part**
> At the end of every episode we tell your child **what we did not explain**: here, that the scale measures a **straight line**, while the real road winds and climbs, so it is longer. We do this because a child at this age builds a full explanation from a single rule and then defends it.
>
> **A question after the episode**
> "Describe the route to school for me **with no landmark at all**: with distance and direction only." — And they will find it hard, **and that difficulty is the lesson.**

## Family Activity

> **The room map — 25 minutes · screen-free**
>
> 1. Draw together **a map of one room** from above, on paper.
> 2. 🔴 **Choose a scale and announce it**: "the width of this small paper = one step's length." And write it in the corner.
> 3. 🔴 **Set north once** and agree on it, and draw an arrow. *(Any direction you choose is fine — the point is that it stays fixed.)*
> 4. Your child stands at the door, and **describes a route to something in the room**: a distance by the scale, and a direction from the arrow.
> 5. 🔴 **You carry out the description literally.** If they say "right," ask: "whose right?" and go from a different side.
> 6. Swap roles: you describe, and they carry it out.
> 7. **Then change the scale**: "the width of the small paper = **two steps**." And repeat the same description. What changed?
>
> 🔴 **Step five is the activity.** And carrying it out literally — with no verbal correction — is what makes the child discover their description is incomplete. **And do not explain the error to them, carry it out.**
>
> 🔴 **And step seven is what fixes the concept.** Changing the scale while keeping the same map **separates the ratio from the drawing** — which many adults do not understand.
>
> **A variation for the older (age 12):** draw a map of **a whole floor** at one scale, then ask for a description of a three-stage route. 🔴 **And calculate at the end: does the straight distance on the paper equal the number of steps you actually walked?** The answer is no — and that is the "Limits" part of the episode.
>
> ⚠️ An activity inside the home · no going out · no climbing on furniture.
