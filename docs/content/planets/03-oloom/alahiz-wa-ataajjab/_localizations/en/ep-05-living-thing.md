> 🔴 **Seeds are forbidden, and this separates this episode from [A Seed Grows](../try-it-at-home/ep-04-seed-grows.md)** in the `kids` track.
>
> The seed is an excellent teaching tool for growth, **and it is the size of a swallowable thing**. So a six-year-old uses it under supervision, **and a three-year-old does not**. Here it is replaced with **a standing plant in a pot** — the concept stays, and the danger is removed.
>
> ⚠️ **And this is a distinction stated to the parent explicitly**, because the first thing a well-meaning adult does at an episode about growth **is bring seeds**.

## The Question It Answers

> **"Why do we water the plant and not the spoon?"**

## Learning Objective

**One objective:** distinguishes a thing that **grows and needs water** from a thing that does not grow and does not need it.

Criterion: sorts 6 pictures into "grows and needs water" and "does not grow", correctly, in 3 of 4 attempts, with no help.

## New Vocabulary

| Word | Explanation offered |
|---|---|
| grows | "it becomes taller after days" — after seeing the paper strip |
| needs water | "if we don't water it, it dries" — after seeing the plant being watered |

## Parent Guide

> **🔴 Before you begin — the first thing you need to know**
> 🔴 **Do not bring seeds.** This is the first thing an adult does at an episode about growth, **and a seed is the size of a swallowable thing**. The episode is built on **a standing plant in a pot**, and it works completely without a seed.
>
> **🔴 And the plant item**
> - 🔴 **You choose the plant**, and it is a **known, non-toxic houseplant**.
> - 🔴 **No unknown plant, no plant from the street, no cactus, nothing thorny, no plant with white sap.**
> - 🔴 **Do not buy a plant for the episode without verifying.** If you don't have one you know, **watch the episode with pictures** and play the pack.
> - 🔴 **They don't touch or dig the soil.** And they don't bring their hand near their mouth.
> - 🔴 **No picking, breaking, tasting, or smelling** of any leaf or flower.
> - 🔴 **No fertilizer, pesticide, or container opened** in the room.
> - 🔴 **No trowel, scissors, or fork.**
> - 🔴 **You water.** A small cup and a little water, and no open vessel on the floor.
> - 🔴 **The child does not carry the pot** — and no heavy clay within their reach.
> - 🔴 **Hand-washing after is mandatory**, even if they did not touch the soil.
>
> **What is your child learning?**
> That some things **grow and need water**, and some do not. This is the first life-science sort they make in their life, **and it is a sort by observation, not by definition**.
>
> **🔴 Why don't we say "alive"?**
> Because the opposite of "alive" is "dead", a concept that worries a three-year-old **with no teaching benefit here**. So the episode says: **"grows and needs water"** — a description the child can **verify with their eye**, unlike "alive".
>
> **🔴 And why no animal in the episode?**
> Because an animal opens the question of food and death, **and it needs care not water only** — so the lesson becomes two, not one. The plant alone is enough.
>
> **Growth needs days, and the episode is minutes**
> The episode **draws** the plant taller, and does not claim it waited. **The real waiting is in the family activity below**, which is the part that truly teaches.
>
> **How do you help?**
> - **Let the silence pass** — four seconds after each prompt.
> - **Accept pointing as an answer.** They don't need to speak.
> - **Don't say "wrong".** Say: "Look again."
> - **Don't ask them "why?"** — the sort is enough at this age.

## Family Activity

> **A Mark on the Strip — 5 minutes today, and a minute each day**
>
> 1. 🔴 **You choose** a known, non-toxic houseplant, and put it on a low, stable table.
> 2. Put a long paper strip beside it, and put a **mark** at the tip of the tallest leaf. 🔴 **With a pen in your hand.**
> 3. 🔴 **You water**, with a small cup and a little water, and let them look.
> 4. Every day, **one minute**: look at the strip. Has the tip risen above the mark?
> 5. After five days, ask them: "**Has it become taller?**" and accept their pointing to the mark.
> 6. Put a spoon beside another strip, with a mark at it, and ask them after five days: "**And this one?**"
> 7. Wash hands every time.
>
> 🔴 **Step 6 is the activity.** The plant alone **proves nothing** — the comparison with a thing that **does not grow** is what makes the observation evidence.
>
> 🔴 **Step 4 is one minute, no more.** Waiting five days is very long for a three-year-old, **and the mark on the strip is what makes it bearable** — because it turns the waiting into something that is seen.
>
> 🔴 **No seeds · no soil in the hand · no picking · no tasting · no smelling · no fertilizer · no garden tools · no carrying the pot · and hand-washing every time.**
>
> **A variation for the older child (5 years):** they put **a second mark** after five days, then measure the distance between the two marks **with their finger** — 🔴 with no ruler and no numbers. The distance between two marks is a sufficient concept.
