cardboard · a bath loofah). And two wide baskets or boxes.

Supervision: 🔴 required — the adult chooses the six things themselves, tries them on the
back of their own hand before giving them to the child, and stays for the whole experiment.

Warnings:
🔴 "Rough" does not mean "scratches". Strictly forbidden: sandpaper · metal scouring pad ·
   wire brush · brick · cement · splintered wood · tree bark · any edge that scratches.
🔴 The adult runs every rough thing over the back of their own hand first. What scratches
   an adult's hand cuts a child's hand.
🔴 Everything is bigger than the child's fist. No small stone, no gravel, no button.
🔴 No tasting. No smelling. Nothing near the mouth or nose.
🔴 No glass, no ceramic, no sharp metal. The cup and spoon are plastic.
🔴 Nothing hot. No stove or kettle in the room.
🔴 No electrical device or cord within reach.
- Touch with fingertips and the palm. No pressing and no hard rubbing.

1. The adult removes the six things and the two baskets.
2. Checks the child's hands: no scratch, no redness.

## 🔴 The Governing Safety Item in This Episode

**The danger here is not the action, but choosing the thing.** The episode asks the child to touch something **rough**, and the most common thing an adult finds at home under the word "rough" is **sandpaper and a metal scouring pad** — both of which cut a three-year-old's hand.

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| 🔴 Choosing the rough thing | **The adult alone** · the child does not search |
| 🔴 The acceptance test | **The adult runs it over the back of their own hand** — what scratches is rejected |
| 🔴 Explicitly forbidden | sandpaper · metal scouring pad · wire brush · brick · cement · splintered wood · tree bark |
| 🔴 Size | bigger than the child's fist · **and no gravel** |
| 🔴 The stone | stays on the table and is not lifted |
| 🔴 Touching | with fingertips · **no pressing and no rubbing** |
| 🔴 Stopping | "it hurts" = the piece is removed at once |

> 🔴 **"Rough" in this series means "not smooth", not "scratches".** This is a distinction explained to the parent explicitly in their guide, because a mistake in it **produces a wound, not a misunderstanding**.

## The Question It Answers

> **"Why is this one smooth and this one not smooth?"**

## Learning Objective

**One objective:** sorts things into rough and smooth **by touch** and names the sort.

Criterion: sorts 6 things correctly, in 3 of 4 attempts, after touching each thing once, with no help.

## Parent Guide

> **🔴 Before you begin — the rough-thing choosing item**
> - 🔴 **"Rough" does not mean "scratches".** The episode asks for a surface that is **not smooth**, not a surface that cuts.
> - 🔴 **Strictly forbidden:** sandpaper · metal scouring pad · wire brush · brick · cement · splintered wood · tree bark.
> - 🔴 **The acceptance test:** run each rough piece over **the back of your own hand** first. **What scratches your hand cuts theirs.**
> - 🔴 **Everything is bigger than their fist.** No gravel and no small stone.
> - 🔴 **The stone stays on the table and is not lifted** — so it can't fall on a foot or hand.
> - 🔴 **No tasting and no smelling**, here and across the whole series.
> - 🔴 **If they say "it hurts", remove the piece at once and do not return it.** Then check their hand.
>
> **What exactly do you do?**
> **You choose the six, and offer each piece with your hand and take it away.** The episode is `required`, not `recommended`, **and the danger in it is in the choosing, not in the action**.
>
> **What is your child learning?**
> That **their hand gives them information**, and that things are sorted by it. This is the first sort in their scientific life: **one attribute, and two baskets**.
>
> **Why "your finger passes easily" and not "texture"?**
> Because "texture" is a word the child cannot see, and "your finger passes" is **a motion they see and repeat**. Meaning always comes before the word.
>
> **How do you help?**
> - **Let the silence pass** — four seconds after each prompt.
> - **Don't press their hand onto the thing.** They touch it.
> - **Don't say "wrong".** Say: "Touch it again."
> - **Don't ask for rubbing or pressing.** A fingertip touch is enough.
>
> **A question after the episode**
> "What is the softest thing in your room?" — 🔴 and look for it **together**, don't let them search alone.

## Family Activity

> **Smooth Basket and Rough Basket — 10 minutes · no screen**
>
> 1. 🔴 **You gather** six safe things from home: three smooth and three rough.
> 2. 🔴 **Run each rough piece over the back of your hand.** Whatever scratches is set aside at once.
> 3. Put out two baskets or boxes, and on each a **drawing**: a flat line and a jagged line.
> 4. You touch first, and say: "My finger passes easily… smooth."
> 5. Give them one piece, and say: "Touch it gently." 🔴 **Then stay silent for four seconds.**
> 6. Ask them: "Which basket?" and accept their pointing.
> 7. Return the things together, **check their hands**, then wash hands.
>
> 🔴 **Step 2 is the most important step in the activity.** Not step 6.
>
> 🔴 **No sandpaper · no scouring pad · no brick · no splintered wood · nothing smaller than their fist · no tasting · no smelling.**
>
> **A variation for the older child (5 years):** put the pieces in a wide cloth bag, and they put their hand in **and guess** before looking. 🔴 **On condition that they touched each piece a moment ago, and that you put your hand in first.**
