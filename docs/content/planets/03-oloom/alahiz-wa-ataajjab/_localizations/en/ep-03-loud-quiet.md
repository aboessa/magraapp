## 🔴 The Governing Safety Item: an Episode About Loud Sound Does Not Produce a Loud Sound

The most dangerous thing about this episode is **that it might actually be produced loudly**. That is rejected.

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| 🔴 Intensity ceiling | **one soft clap** — and nothing louder in the whole series |
| 🔴 How the difference is shown | **by relative comparison and a drawn wave**, big and small — not by raising the level |
| 🔴 Shouting | **forbidden** — and the child is never asked to raise their voice, not once |
| 🔴 Devices | **all forbidden** — sound comes from the child's hand, or from cloth or paper |
| 🔴 Balloon | **forbidden** — [decision documented in the planet](../try-it-at-home/ep-06-invisible-air.md) |
| 🔴 Headphones | **forbidden in this episode** |
| 🔴 Device level | the adult sets it to **half or less** before playing |
| 🔴 Stopping | covering the ears = **immediate stop** |

> 🔴 **The drawn wave is the editorial solution for this episode.** The child sees a **small wave for the whisper and a big wave for the clap**, and understands the difference **with their eye**, not with their ear alone. That way the difference stays clear **without any sound being raised**.
>
> ⚠️ **And this is also accessibility:** the child with hearing sensitivity, or the one watching at very low volume, **learns the whole episode from the wave**.

## The Question It Answers

> **"What is this sound? And where did it come from?"**

## Learning Objective

**One objective:** sorts sounds into loud and quiet, **and names the source of each sound**.

Criterion: sorts 4 sounds correctly **and names the source of at least 3 of them**, in 3 of 4 attempts, with no help.

## New Vocabulary

| Word | Explanation offered |
|---|---|

## Parent Guide

> **🔴 Before you begin — the sound item**
> - 🔴 **Turn the device sound down to half or less.** The episode is about loud sound, **and it is built to stay quiet**.
> - 🔴 **No shouting.** Don't ask them to raise their voice, not once, not even to show the difference.
> - 🔴 **No whistle, no horn, no hard banging, no clapping near their ear.**
> - 🔴 **No sound-making device:** blender, vacuum, dryer, alarm, bell.
> - 🔴 **No balloon** — blowing or popping. It is forbidden across the whole science planet.
> - 🔴 **No headphones on their ear** in this episode.
> - 🔴 **The spoon is plastic on wood.** No tapping on glass and no tapping on metal.
> - 🔴 **Nothing is put in the ear.** No finger, no cotton, no paper.
> - 🔴 **If they cover their ears: stop the sound at once** and lower the level further.
>
> **What is your child learning?**
> That sounds **differ in their size**, and that every sound has **a place it comes from**. The second part is the more important: knowing a sound **has a source**, not that it comes from nowhere.
>
> **Why the drawn wave?**
> Because we **refuse to raise the sound to explain sound**. So the difference is shown **by the size of a wave on the screen**, and your child understands it with their eye. This also means the episode **works at very low volume**, or even muted.
>
> **If your child is sound-sensitive**
> 🔴 Watch the episode **at very low volume**, or play the pack **with no sounds** — it works from the drawn waves. **And do not try to get them "used to" loud sounds.**
>
> **How do you help?**
> - **Let the silence pass** — four seconds after each prompt.
> - **Ask "where did it come from?"** after each sound, and accept "from my hand".
> - **One whisper, one clap, and one tap.** Repetition raises the noise.
> - **Don't say "wrong".** Say: "Listen again."
>
> **A question after the episode**
> "What is the quietest sound in the house now?" — 🔴 and go silent together for ten seconds and listen. That is an activity in itself.

## Family Activity

> **Hunting for Quiet Sounds — 8 minutes · no screen**
>
> 1. Sit in a quiet room. 🔴 **Turn off every sound-making device.**
> 2. Go silent together for **ten seconds**, and just listen.
> 3. Ask them: "**What did you hear?**" and accept any answer: a fan, a bird, a footstep, breathing.
> 4. For each sound ask them: "**Where did it come from?**" — and this is the activity.
> 5. Make two sounds together: **a whisper**, then **one soft clap**. And ask: "Which is bigger?"
> 6. Draw a small wave and a big wave on paper, and put each sound at its wave.
>
> 🔴 **Step 4 is the activity**, not step 3. **Naming the source** is higher than hearing the sound.
>
> 🔴 **No shouting · no whistling · no banging · no device · no balloon · no headphones · and nothing put in the ear.**
>
> **A variation for the older child (5 years):** close their eyes, and you make **one quiet sound** from three things in front of them, and ask: "Where did it come from?" 🔴 **With quiet sounds only, and from things they saw a moment ago.**
