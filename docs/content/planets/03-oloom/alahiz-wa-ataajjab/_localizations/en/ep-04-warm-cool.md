## 🔴 The Water Item — Mandatory

Drowning in shallow water is possible for young children, and it is **silent and fast**. The item here is **stricter** than its counterpart in [Floats or Sinks?](../try-it-at-home/ep-03-float-or-sink.md#-the-water-safety-item) because the child is three years younger.

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| 🔴 Depth | **one finger deep** — not a palm, and no more |
| 🔴 Vessel | **wide low plastic** · no sink, no bucket, no pail, no glass |
| 🔴 Presence | **the child is never left alone near water, not even for a second** |
| 🔴 Little ones | postpone if a child **younger than 3** is in the room |
| 🔴 Who pours | **the adult alone** · and empties **immediately** at the end |
| 🔴 Electricity | **no device, cord, or socket** near the water |
| 🔴 Slipping | a towel ready · wipe at once |
| 🔴 Drinking | **the water is for the experiment only** |

## 🔴 The Temperature Item — "Room-Warm" Means Room Temperature

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| 🔴 The upper end | **water at room temperature** — and nothing above it |
| 🔴 Forbidden | hot · scalding · **warm from the tap** · kettle · stove · steam · candle |
| 🔴 The lower end | **cold water from the fridge** · 🔴 **no ice cubes** |
| 🔴 Why no ice | it is swallowable · **and its cold hurts the skin** |
| 🔴 Contact time | **≤ 3 seconds** in the cold water |
| 🔴 What enters the water | **one finger** — not the whole hand and not the face |

> 🔴 **The word "hot" is forbidden in the whole episode's narration.** The reason is that a three-year-old **tries the word they hear**, and the nearest hot water in their home is **a tap or a kettle**. So the episode compares **room-warm with cool**, and never names a third end at all.
>
> ⚠️ **This means the episode does not teach "hot and cold".** It teaches **room-warm and cool** — the only safe difference at this age. **And the full range is offered at an older age.**

## The Question It Answers

> **"Why does my hand feel a difference?"**

## Learning Objective

**One objective:** compares two safe waters and names the room-warm and the cool **by touch**.

Criterion: distinguishes the room-warm bowl from the cool one and names the difference, in 3 of 4 attempts, after one touch of each bowl, with no help.

## Parent Guide

> **🔴 Before you begin — the water item**
> - 🔴 **The depth is one finger deep.** A wide low plastic bowl. **No sink, no bucket, no pail, no tub, no glass.**
> - 🔴 **Do not leave your child alone near water, not even for a second, not even a finger deep.** Drowning in the young is **silent and fast**.
> - 🔴 **If a child younger than three is in the room, postpone the experiment.**
> - 🔴 **You pour, and you empty.** And you empty **immediately** at the end, not a little later.
> - 🔴 **A towel is ready.** A wet floor is slippery.
> - 🔴 **No device, cord, or socket** near the water.
>
> **🔴 And the temperature item — and the most important part is what is NOT in the episode**
> - 🔴 **No hot water. No scalding. No warm from the tap.** "Room-warm" here means **room temperature**, no more.
> - 🔴 **The episode never says the word "hot", not once**, and this is deliberate: a three-year-old **tries the word they hear**, and the nearest hot water in your home is a tap or a kettle.
> - 🔴 **No ice cubes.** Their size is swallowable, and their cold hurts the skin. The cold water is **from the fridge only**.
> - 🔴 **One finger enters the water.** Not the whole hand, and not the face.
> - 🔴 **Their finger stays in the cold water no longer than three seconds.**
> - 🔴 **The water is for touch, not for drinking.**
>
> **What is your child learning?**
> That **their hand measures a difference the eye does not see**. The two bowls look identical, the water is the same, **and the difference appears by touch alone**.
>
> **What do you not expect from them?**
> Don't expect them to say "temperature". Nor to compare three levels. **It's enough that they say: this one is cool and this one is not.** The full comparison comes at an older age.
>
> **How do you help?**
> - **Do it yourself first** — one finger, and take it out in front of them after two seconds.
> - **Let the silence pass** — four seconds after each prompt.
> - **Dry their finger between the bowls.** The feel of the second becomes clearer.
> - **Don't say "wrong".** Say: "Touch it again."
>
> **A question after the episode**
> "What is the coolest thing you touched today?" — 🔴 and with no searching in the fridge or the kitchen.

## Family Activity

> **Two Bowls and a Finger — 10 minutes**
>
> 1. 🔴 **You bring** two wide low plastic bowls and two towels.
> 2. 🔴 **You pour** into each water **one finger deep**: one at room temperature, and one from the fridge. 🔴 **No ice.**
> 3. Put your own finger in each bowl first, and take it out after two seconds, and name what you felt.
> 4. Say to them: "**One finger.**" Then ask them to put it in the first. 🔴 **And stay silent for four seconds.**
> 5. Dry their finger, then the second bowl. 🔴 **And take it out after three seconds.**
> 6. Ask them: "**Which is cool?**" and accept their pointing.
> 7. 🔴 **Empty the two bowls at once**, and wipe the table and floor, and wash hands.
>
> 🔴 **Step 7 is not tidying. It is safety.** A bowl of water left on the floor is a bigger danger after the activity than during it.
>
> 🔴 **No hot · no warm from the tap · no ice · no drinking · no splashing · no sink or bucket · no glass · no electricity near the water · and with your constant presence.**
>
> **A variation for the older child (5 years):** after they know the two bowls, **swap their places while they look away**, and ask: "Which is the cool one now?" — 🔴 **with the same depth and the same presence, and one finger.**
