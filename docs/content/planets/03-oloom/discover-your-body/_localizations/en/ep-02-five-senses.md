## The Question It Answers

> **"How do I know the food is sweet?"**

## Learning Objective

**One objective:** the child links each sense to its organ and its function.

Criterion: classifies 6 things by the sense that detects them, in 3 of 4 attempts.

## New Vocabulary

| Word | Explanation offered |
|---|---|
| the sense | a way your body knows what is around it |
| the tongue | detects the taste |
| the skin | detects texture and temperature |

## 🔴 An Inclusion Rule — A Governing Editorial Decision

This episode **does not say** that every person has five working senses.

| ❌ Forbidden | ✅ Used |
|---|---|
| "All people have five senses." | "Most of us use five senses." |
| "Without eyes you see nothing." | "Some people read with their fingers." |
| "The five senses make you complete." | "Each sense helps, and the senses cooperate." |

**Reason:** the deaf, blind, or sense-limited child **watches this episode**. Linking senses to completeness wounds them and teaches others that a missing sense is a deficiency in the person. The episode plainly states that a sense may not work, and that others grow stronger.

## Parent Guide

> **What is your child learning?**
> That each sense has an organ and a function, and that the senses **cooperate** to know a single thing. They tried hearing and touch themselves.
>
> **Why weren't the senses of smell and taste tried?**
> Deliberate. Teaching a six-year-old to "smell to know" or "taste to know" is a dangerous behavior around a cleaning substance or medicine. We explained the two senses, but did not train on them.
>
> **The important sentence in the episode**
> "**Most of us** use five senses" — not "all of us". The episode states that a sense may not work in some people, and that their other senses grow stronger. If there is a blind or deaf person around your child, this is a good chance to talk about them with respect.
>
> **How do you help?**
> Play "the touch bag": put safe things in a bag, and they guess by touching. **With no small swallowable things, and nothing sharp.**
>
> **What not to do?**
> Don't have them smell or taste something "to try". And don't put anything into their nose or ear.
>
> **When do you consult a doctor?**
> If you notice they don't respond to your voice from afar, bring things very close to their eyes, or don't distinguish a clear taste. **This app does not diagnose and does not replace a doctor.**

## Family Activity

> **The Touch Bag — 15 minutes**
>
> 1. Gather 6 safe things from home: a spoon, a sponge, a towel, a ball, a comb, a large stone.
> 2. **Nothing smaller than a child's fist, nothing sharp, no glass.**
> 3. Put them in a cloth bag.
> 4. The child puts their hand in and touches **without looking**, and guesses.
> 5. They say what they noticed: "soft" "rough" "cold" "heavy".
> 6. Swap roles — the grown-up guesses too.
>
> **A clever question:** "Did you guess with one touch, or did you need to turn the thing?" — the child discovers that touch needs movement.
>
> **A variation for the older child:** they describe the thing with three attributes before naming it.
