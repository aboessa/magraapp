## The Question It Answers

> **"Why don't I bend everywhere?"**

## Learning Objective

**One objective:** the child distinguishes the rigid bone from the joint that bends.

Criterion: sorts 6 spots into "bends / does not bend", in 3 of 4 attempts.

## New Vocabulary

| Word | Explanation offered |
|---|---|
| the bone | rigid, and carries your body |
| the joint | the place of bending between two bones |
| the muscle | pulls the bone so it moves |

## 🔴 A Governing Visual Rule: the Skeleton Is Not Scary

| ❌ Forbidden | Reason |
|---|---|
| a full white skeleton | the image of death and terror in every culture |
| a skull with two empty eye sockets | directly frightening |
| separate scattered bones | suggest breakage and coming apart |
| a bone-cracking sound | disgusting and unsettling |
| a broken bone or a cast | links the bone to pain |

| ✅ Used | The form |
|---|---|
| the bones | **colored `#FFD34D`** inside a transparent body |
| the skull | **never shown** — we start from the neck |
| the joint | a glowing `#00D6F5` dot |
| the muscle | a simplified `#FF6FAE` shape that shortens and lengthens |
| the sound | a soft tone, no cracking |

**Reason:** the white skeleton is the symbol of death in children's visual culture. Showing it in an educational episode produces a child who fears their own bones. Warm colors inside a living body convey the same meaning with no terror.

## Parent Guide

> **What is your child learning?**
> Three elements and their functions: **a bone carries, a joint bends, and a muscle pulls**. And that they do not bend everywhere because bending happens only at a joint.
>
> **Why is there no white skeleton in the episode?**
> Deliberate. The white skeleton and skull are the symbol of death in children's visual culture, and showing them in an educational episode produces a child who fears their own bones. We showed the bones **colored inside a living body** — the same information without terror. And we never showed the skull.
>
> **The red line**
> 🔴 Don't let them bend a joint forcefully, bend their fingers backward, or try to bend what does not bend. The episode says "**gently**" in every movement prompt.
>
> **Why didn't we mention the number of bones?**
> Because it is a number memorized without understanding, and it changes with age anyway (a child has more bones than an adult, some fuse as they grow). Understanding matters more than the number.
>
> **How do you help?**
> Walk together and name the joints that move: the shoulder, the elbow, the hip, the knee, the ankle. **With natural movement only.**
>
> **When do you consult a doctor?**
> If they complain of persistent pain in a joint, swelling, difficulty moving a limb, or an unsteady walk. **This app does not diagnose and does not replace a doctor.**

> 1. Draw a simple body shape on a large sheet.
> 2. The child moves and notices **where their body bends**.
> 3. With each joint they find, they put a dot on the drawing and name it.
> 4. Then they touch a spot that **does not bend** and put a straight line there.
> 5. **Compare:** how many dots? How many lines?
>
> 🔴 **Natural movement only. No bending forcefully. No bending fingers backward.**
>
> **A clever question:** "Is the neck one joint or more?" — more. That is why it turns and tilts in many directions.
>
> **A variation for the older child:** compares the elbow joint (one direction) with the shoulder joint (many directions) — and concludes that joints come in kinds.
