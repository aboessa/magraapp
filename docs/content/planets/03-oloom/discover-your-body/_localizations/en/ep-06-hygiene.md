## The Question It Answers

> **"Why do I wash my hands?"**

## Learning Objective

**One objective:** the child orders the hand-washing steps, and knows **when** to wash.

Criterion: orders 4 steps correctly, and identifies 3 suitable times, in 3 of 4 attempts.

## New Vocabulary

| Word | Explanation offered |
|---|---|
| germs | very tiny living things not seen by the eye |
| soap | helps the water remove them |
| protection | to protect yourself before you get sick |

## 🔴 A Governing Editorial Rule: No Fear and No Obsession

This is the most psychologically delicate episode after the food one. The wrong hygiene message produces a child who **fears the world**.

| ❌ Strictly forbidden | What it produces |
|---|---|
| germs as monsters with faces and teeth | fear of every surface |
| "you will get very sick" | health anxiety |
| "everything you touch is contaminated" | 🔴 **contamination obsession** |
| "wash your hands always" | compulsive washing |
| images of illness, fever, or shots | linking the body to fear |
| "germs are everywhere attacking you" | a constant state of threat |
| exact mandatory counting or repetition | ritual behavior |

| ✅ Used | The form |
|---|---|
| germs | "very tiny, and some may tire you." |
| reassurance | "and most of what is around you does not harm you." |
| the limit | "you don't need to wash them every minute." |
| the times | **three specific times**, not "always" |
| the action | "you wash, so they leave." — a simple causality |

**Principle:** the child leaves **confident, knowing when to wash their hands**, not afraid of everything they touch. The sentence "most of what is around you does not harm you" is not filler but a **psychological safeguard**, and its presence is an acceptance condition.

## Parent Guide

> **What is your child learning?**
> Four steps to wash their hands, and three specific times: before eating, after the toilet, after playing outside. And that water and soap remove germs.
>
> **🔴 Why aren't the germs monsters in the episode?**
> A deliberate decision. Drawing germs as monsters with teeth and evil eyes is a common style, and it produces a child who **fears every surface they touch**. We drew them as neutral gray dots that appear calmly and leave with water. The same information, without terror.
>
> **The two sentences we hope you notice**
> "**And most of what is around you does not harm you**" and "**You don't need to wash them every minute. Play, then wash.**"
>
> These are deliberate sentences to prevent **contamination obsession** and compulsive washing. An exaggerated hygiene message in children can turn into real anxiety, and we specify **three** times, not "always".
>
> **And the episode mentioned no illness, no shot, and no hospital**
> With no exception. Protection is presented as **a simple thing a confident person does**, not as an escape from danger.
>
> **Why "water alone: good"?**
> Because a child may not find soap somewhere. We don't want them to feel they failed. The message is gradual: water is good, water with soap is better.
>
> **The red line**
> 🔴 **Water that is not hot** — the episode says it plainly, and burns are common. And **no alcohol sanitizer** in any activity: it is flammable, harmful to the eye, and swallowed by mistake.
>
> **When do you consult a doctor?**
> If you notice your child washes their hands too much, is bothered by touching things, or their hand skin cracks from washing. **Contamination obsession is treatable**, and noticing it early makes a difference. **This app does not diagnose and does not replace a specialist.**

## Family Activity

> **The Four-Step Hero — 10 minutes**
>
> 1. Go to the sink together.
> 2. **Turn on the lukewarm water — not the hot.** The grown-up sets it.
> 3. The child says the steps in their own voice and does them: wet, soap, between the fingers, rinse and dry.
> 4. Swap roles — the grown-up does it and the child watches and corrects.
> 5. Hang a small sheet by the sink with **the three times** in a simple drawing.
>
> 🔴 **Plain soap only. No alcohol sanitizer. No cleaning substances. No hot water.**
>
> **A clever question:** "Do we wash our hands before eating or after?" — before. Because the hand carries the food to the mouth.
>
> **A variation for the older child:** they draw the three times themselves and hang it. Drawing it by hand makes them remember it.
>
> 🔴 **What we don't do:** don't make washing a repeated contest, and don't require it after every touch. **Three times is enough.**
