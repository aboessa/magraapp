## The Question It Answers

> **"Where does my food go?"**

## Learning Objective

**One objective:** the child traces the food's path, and links it to the energy they move with.

Criterion: orders the food's path correctly, and links food to energy, in 3 of 4 attempts.

## 🔴 A Governing Editorial Rule: No Food Shaming

This is the most psychologically sensitive episode in the series, because talking about food with children builds their relationship with it for years.

| ❌ Strictly forbidden | Reason |
|---|---|
| "this is bad / harmful / forbidden food" | builds fear and guilt about food |
| "this food is healthy and this is not" | a moral classification that seeds eating disorders |
| any mention of weight, thinness, or fatness | 🔴 a direct psychological danger at this age |
| any quantity or number of servings | dietary advice that needs a specialist |
| "eat all your food" | teaches ignoring the feeling of fullness |
| "food is a reward" or "a punishment" | links food to emotion, not to need |
| images of gorging or excess | normalizing an unhealthy behavior |

| ✅ Used | The form |
|---|---|
| variety | "your body needs different kinds." |
| function | "each kind gives something different." |
| the feeling | "your body tells you when it is full." |
| description not direction | "food becomes energy." |

**Principle:** the episode **explains how the body works** and does not give dietary advice. Dietary advice for children is a medical specialty, and any directive sentence about food needs a **pediatric medical reviewer and a nutritionist**.

## Parent Guide

> **What is your child learning?**
> The food's path: mouth ← stomach ← intestines. And that their body takes the nutrient into their blood, where it meets the oxygen from their lungs, and their heart distributes both. Food becomes **energy**.
>
> **The episode that ties together everything before it**
> This is the first episode where your child sees that the heart, the lungs, and the intestines are **one system**, not three lessons. That is why it is the fifth, not the first — they need to have seen the organs before linking them.
>
> **🔴 Why doesn't the episode say a food is "bad"?**
> A deliberate decision. Classifying food morally (good/bad, healthy/unhealthy) at this age builds **guilt and fear** about food, and it is one of the clearest paths to later eating disorders. The episode talks about **variety**: each kind gives something different, and so we eat kinds.
>
> **And no mention of weight at all**
> Not a word about weight, thinness, or fatness. This is a red line in children's content, with no exception.
>
> **The sentence we hope you notice**
> "**Your body tells you when it is full, so listen to it.**" This is the opposite of "eat all your food". A child who learns to listen to the feeling of fullness builds a healthy relationship with food. A child who learns to ignore it loses that compass.
>
> **How do you help?**
> Ask them after eating: "How do you feel now?" with no judgment. And notice together that chewing well makes the journey easier.
>
> **When do you consult a doctor or nutritionist?**
> If they complain of recurring belly pain, refuse to eat for a while, have difficulty swallowing, or show a clear change in their growth. **This app does not diagnose, does not give dietary advice, and does not replace a specialist.**

## Family Activity

> **The Kitchen of Kinds — 20 minutes**
>
> 1. Open the food cupboard together.
> 2. Gather **6 different kinds** — any 6 you have.
> 3. Arrange them on the table, and name each one.
> 4. Ask: "Are they all alike?" — no. **Different, and that is the point.**
> 5. The child chooses what they like among them and says why.
>
> 🔴 **No classifying. No "this is good and this is not". No talk of quantity or weight.** The goal is to notice the variety.
>
> **A clever question:** "If we ate only one kind every day, would it be enough?" — no. Because each kind gives something different.
>
> **A variation for the older child:** they try chewing 5 times versus 20 times, and notice the difference in the feeling. **With a small safe bite and with your presence.**
