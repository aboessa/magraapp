## The Question It Answers

> **"Why do I hear beats in my chest?"**

## Learning Objective

**One objective:** the child links the heart to its function — **it pushes blood to the whole body**.

Criterion: explains the heart's function in a sentence, and links a fast pulse to movement, in 3 of 4 attempts.

## New Vocabulary

| Word | Explanation offered |
|---|---|
| the heart | the organ that pushes the blood |
| the pulse | the heartbeat you feel |

## Parent Guide

> **What is your child learning?**
> That the heart **pushes** the blood — it does not make it. And that its pulse speeds up with movement because its muscles asked for more food and air. This is a cause-and-effect relationship they tried **on their own body**, not memorized from a book.
>
> **Why is there no blood in the episode?**
> Because depicting blood or realistic anatomy frightens a six-year-old and links their body to fear. We used moving arrows instead. The goal is for your child to leave **admiring their body, not afraid of it**.
>
> **How do you help?**
> Try together: count your pulses while sitting, then after climbing the stairs. **But without exertion** — the goal is observation, not fitness.
>
> **If your child worries about their pulse?**
> This is common after they first notice it. Reassure them: "Your heart always works, you just weren't noticing it." The episode says this plainly, but reassurance from you is stronger.
>
> **When do you consult a doctor?**
> If your child complains of chest pain, dizziness with movement, or a fast pulse with no effort. **This app does not diagnose and does not replace a doctor.**
>
> **What not to do?**
> Don't let them hold their breath or press on their chest "to try it". And don't have them run to exhaustion to measure the pulse.

## Family Activity

> **The Pulse Lab — 15 minutes**
>
> 1. Sit calmly for a minute, then each person counts their pulse for **10 seconds**.
> 2. Write the numbers on a sheet.
> 3. Stand and walk around the room for a minute, then count again.
> 4. **Compare:** which activity raised the pulse most?
> 5. Notice that **the child's pulse is faster than the grown-up's** — this is completely normal.
>
> **No exertion. No contest. The goal is observation.**
>
> **A clever question:** "Is a cat's pulse faster or slower than ours?" — compare with a pet at home if there is one. Small animals have a faster pulse.
