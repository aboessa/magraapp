## The Question It Answers

> **"Why do I breathe faster after running?"**

## Learning Objective

**One objective:** the child traces the air's path, and links breathing speed to the body's need.

Criterion: orders the air's path correctly, and explains the faster breathing, in 3 of 4 attempts.

## New Vocabulary

| Word | Explanation offered |
|---|---|
| the lungs | take in the air |
| oxygen | the useful air your body needs |
| the windpipe | a tube that carries the air |

## 🔴 A Safety Rule Specific to This Episode

This is the most dangerous episode in the series, because the child might try breathing in harmful ways.

| ❌ Forbidden in the script and visuals | Reason |
|---|---|
| "hold your breath and count" | oxygen deprivation · fainting |
| "breathe fast a lot" | hyperventilation · dizziness · tingling |
| "blow as hard as you can" | pressure on the ear and chest |
| "how many seconds can you go without air?" | turns it into a dangerous contest |
| any scene of a child holding their breath | it is copied literally |

| ✅ Used | The form |
|---|---|
| "breathe slowly" | a calm, normal breath |
| "count your breaths" | observation with no intervention |
| "put your hand on your chest" | feeling the movement |

**Principle:** every breathing activity in the episode is **observation**, not **control**. The child watches their natural breathing and does not change it.

## Parent Guide

> **What is your child learning?**
> The air's path: nose ← windpipe ← lungs. And that their lungs take the oxygen, and their heart distributes it. And that their breathing speeds up after effort because their muscles asked for more oxygen.
>
> **Why didn't the episode ask them to run?**
> Deliberate. The heart episode asked for a little jogging because it measured the pulse. This episode measures breathing itself, and a child with asthma or a chest sensitivity might restrict their breath. So the episode plainly tells them: "**Remember, don't try**."
>
> **The red line**
> 🔴 Don't let them hold their breath, breathe fast on purpose, or blow as hard as they can. The episode mentions none of this, and if you hear it from them it came from elsewhere.
>
> **How do you help?**
> Sit calmly and count breaths for a full minute. Notice that **a child's breaths are faster than a grown-up's** — completely normal.
>
> **When do you consult a doctor?**
> If you hear a wheeze in their chest, their breath tightens with light effort, they cough at night persistently, or they breathe fast while at rest. **This app does not diagnose and does not replace a doctor.**
>
> **If they have known asthma?**
> The episode is safe to watch fully — it asks for no effort. But consult their doctor about any physical activity you plan together.

## Family Activity

> **The Breath Watcher — 10 minutes**
>
> 1. Sit comfortably on the floor or a chair.
> 2. Each person puts their hand on their chest.
> 3. Count the breaths for **one minute** with a phone or watch.
> 4. Write the numbers: the child, the mother, the father.
> 5. **Compare:** the child's number is bigger. This is normal — their body is smaller and their heart is faster.
>
> 🔴 **No running. No breath-holding. No contest.** The goal is observation alone.
>
> **A clever question:** "Which animal breathes faster, the elephant or the sparrow?" — the sparrow. The rule: the smaller is faster.
>
> **A variation for the older child:** they count their breaths before sleep and after waking, and compare.
