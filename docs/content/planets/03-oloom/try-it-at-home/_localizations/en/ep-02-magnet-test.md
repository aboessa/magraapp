## 🔴 The Most Serious Safety Item in the Series

**Swallowing two small magnets is a medical emergency.** The two magnets attract each other through the intestinal wall and pierce it, and it may require urgent surgery.

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| Number | 🔴 **One magnet only** — not two |
| Size | 🔴 **Bigger than the child's palm** — cannot go in the mouth |
| Type | Ordinary fridge magnet · 🔴 **no neodymium, no beads** |
| Handling | The parent holds it between tries |
| Storage | Out of children's reach after the experiment |
| Alternative | If no suitable magnet is available: **cancel the experiment** and use the simulation |

**Editorial decision:** the episode **offers an explicit digital alternative** rather than pushing a parent to use whatever they have. An episode that makes cancelling a stated option is safer than one that assumes availability.

## The Scientific Concept

**A magnet does not attract every metal.** It attracts iron and things like it, and does not attract aluminum or copper.

**Targeted misconception:** "Everything made of metal is attracted." — the aluminum foil exposes the error.

**What we say to the child:** "It attracts iron, not every metal." Precise terms are deferred to the Junior stage.

## Parent Guide

> **🔴 Read this before you begin**
>
> **Swallowing two small magnets is a medical emergency.** The two magnets attract each other through the intestinal wall and pierce it, and it may require urgent surgery. The symptoms may look like ordinary stomach pain.
>
> Therefore:
> - **One large fridge magnet only**, bigger than your child's palm.
> - **No magnetic beads and no neodymium** — not in this experiment and not in the home.
> - **You hold the magnet** between tries, not the child.
> - After the experiment, **store it out of children's reach**.
> - If you suspect a child swallowed a magnet: **go to the emergency room immediately**, and don't wait for symptoms to appear.
>
> **And other matters**
> - Keep the magnet away from phones, tablets, computers, and bank cards.
> - **Away from anyone with a heart pacemaker.**
>
> **If no suitable magnet is available?**
> **Cancel the hands-on experiment.** The linked game has a full simulation that serves the same purpose. The episode says so, and we don't want you to use anything unsafe.
>
> **What is your child learning?**
> That a magnet **does not attract every metal** — it attracts iron and things like it. This breaks a very common misconception, and the aluminum foil is what breaks it.
>
> **The important moment in the episode**
> Karim's line: "**Has something changed in your head now?**" after the aluminum fails to be attracted. This is training in **revising a hypothesis** — and it is the most precious thing in the episode, more precious than the fact itself.
>
> **What if their prediction is wrong?**
> That is exactly what we want. Tell them: "Your prediction is part of science."

## Family Activity

> **A Magnet Tour of the Home — 20 minutes**
>
> 1. **The grown-up holds the magnet** for the whole tour.
> 2. Walk through the home and try 10 large, safe objects.
> 3. The child **predicts first** for each object, then you try.
> 4. Record on a sheet: prediction · result · did they match?
> 5. Count at the end: how many correct predictions? And how much did we learn from being wrong?
>
> 🔴 **Away from screens, phones, cards, and any pacemaker.**
> 🔴 **Nothing smaller than the child's fist. No sharp edges.**
>
> **A clever question:** "Does a magnet attract through a sheet of paper? Through a book?" — yes through the paper, and weaker through the book. The force weakens with distance, not with the material.
>
> **A variation for an older child:** they look for **a metal that is not attracted** other than aluminum. The copper in some coins is a good example.
