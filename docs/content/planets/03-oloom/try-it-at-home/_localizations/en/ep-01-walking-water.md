## The Scientific Concept

**Capillary action:** water moves through the tiny gaps between the paper fibers, **even upward**, with no pump and no push.

**What we say to the child:** "The water walks through the paper." — the description comes before the term, and the term is deferred to the Junior stage.

| two drops of food coloring | the parent adds them · optional |
| a sheet of paper under the cups | protects the table |

## Parent Guide

> **Your presence is not optional in this series.**
> `supervision_level = required`. The experiment is safe, but the water and coloring need your hand.
>
> **What exactly do you do?**
> You pour the water, add the two drops of coloring, and empty the two cups at the end. The child cuts the paper, places the ends, and wipes the table.
>
> **What is your child learning?**
> That water moves through the tiny gaps between the paper fibers, **even upward**, with nothing pushing it. And they learn something more important first: **to predict before they see**.
>
> **What if their prediction is wrong?**
> Excellent. Tell them: "Your prediction is part of science." A wrong hypothesis is not a failure, and it is the first thing we build in this series.
>
> **The result takes time**
> Minutes, not seconds. The episode says so plainly and shows a "time-lapse" caption. Don't let them think their experiment failed because it's slower than the screen.
>
> **🔴 Before you begin**
> - Water **lukewarm, not hot**.
> - **Plastic, not glass**.
> - **The water is for the experiment only, not for drinking** — say it yourself once.
> - If there is a child younger than three in the room, **postpone the experiment** or move them away. The colored water looks like a drink.
> - A sheet of paper under the cups — the coloring stains.
>
> **A no-coloring alternative**
> If food coloring isn't available, do it with plain water. You'll see the paper get wet and the wetness rise; the result is less clear but still correct.

## Family Activity

> **The Paper Contest — 20 minutes**
>
> The same experiment, but with different papers. **One variable, everything else held constant.**
>
> 1. Try: paper towel · printer paper · a paper tissue.
> 2. **Same water, same two cups, same strip width.**
> 3. Watch: in which paper did the water walk fastest?
> 4. Write the order down on a sheet.
> 5. Ask: "Why did they differ?" — because each paper's gaps are different.
>
> 🔴 **With a parent present. The parent pours every time.**
>
> **This is the platform's first "one variable" experiment.** The child changes the paper and holds everything else fixed — and that same rule returns in Future Lab for the Junior stage.
>
> **A clever question:** "Does water walk through cloth too?" — try a piece of cotton cloth. Yes, and for the same reason.

## Localization Notes

- Capillary action and the observe-then-predict method are universal and translate fully.
- English uses regular plurals (cups, fibers); the Arabic dual forms ("two cups", "two fingers", "two drops") are rendered with explicit "two", so the counting stays exact.
- Safety-boundary lines ("the parent pours", "lukewarm not hot", "not for drinking") are re-phrased naturally but keep their emphasis and meaning exactly.
- 🔴 The materials list is reviewed per market — availability of food coloring and paper towels varies.
- 🔴 Safety notes are reviewed legally per market and are not machine-translated. Requirements for children's content that includes activities differ by country.
