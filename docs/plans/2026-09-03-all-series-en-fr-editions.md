## Binding editorial rules

1. Read the full Arabic source file before authoring either locale.
2. Translate meaning and learning objective, not Arabic syntax. Adapt names, examples, idioms, rhythm, question wording, and word order to a native child audience.
3. Preserve age band, safety constraints, objective, story facts, scene/page order, visual continuity, and exact declared duration.
4. Translate every child-visible or production-relevant textual section: metadata, title, description, narration/dialogue, vocabulary, questions, answers, mastery criterion, parent guide, family activity, safety notes, scene briefs, alt text, and production notes. Preserve stable IDs and asset keys.
5. Keep visual prompts free of text, letters, logos, watermarks, captions, branding, UI, and overlays. Any deliberate language-learning glyph is structured language data, never baked visual text.
6. Preserve the approved visual identity references and do not redesign recurring characters. Luna remains `luna-discovers-words/luna-v2` wherever present.
7. Do not claim linguistic, pedagogical, religious, audio, or production approval. Non-Islamic editions use `status: review_lang`. Islamic editions use `status: review_sharia` and explicitly require both native-language review and independent sharia review for that locale.
8. `is_machine_translated: false` for every edition: these are editorially adapted drafts, not machine-translation output.
9. For normal translated editions, `translated_from: ar`. For language-specific re-authored editions, `translated_from: null` and `adapted_from_concept: <Arabic source path>`.
10. Do not invent scientific, historical, religious, or safety facts. Preserve explicit fact-check markers and unresolved questions.
11. Do not create or modify DB migrations or import localized content into D1.

## Language-specific re-authoring

Treat all 12 episode units in these two Abjad series as native re-authoring, not literal translation:

- `01-abjad/abni-kalima` — 6 episodes.
- `01-abjad/luna-discovers-words` — 6 episodes.

For English, use age-appropriate English phonics, Latin letters, punctuation, syllables, and vocabulary. For French, use age-appropriate French phonology, accents, letter-sound relations, punctuation, syllables, and vocabulary. Preserve the same high-level objective progression, age, characters, duration, safety, and interaction pattern, but set `translated_from: null`. Do not transliterate Arabic teaching examples.

All other non-Islamic units are editorial translations/adaptations unless their source explicitly marks a subsection language-specific; re-author that subsection natively and record it in `adaptation_notes`.

## Islamic localization rules

For all 66 Islamic editions in each locale:

1. Source only from the final Arabic `draft-v2` file and its cited source boundary.
2. Keep `review_sharia`; leave all reviewer, approval, and license fields empty.
3. Do not translate Quran/adhkar recitation text. Represent it as the same licensed human-recitation placeholder and keep it `language_neutral`.
4. Translate only presenter/narrator explanation and activity text as a draft.
5. State that the meaning/explanation requires independent `review_sharia` in English or French; Arabic review would not approve another language.
6. Preserve the exact source reference and all unresolved source checks.
7. Keep recitation and narration tracks separate; no music with recitation; no synthesized recitation.
8. Preserve every anti-depiction restriction and prohibition on religious text inside imagery.

## Timing and narration quality

- Preserve source duration and beat/page sequence.
- For video beats, every beat remains 4, 6, 8, or 10 seconds and total duration remains exact.
- Recalculate spoken word budgets per localized beat; target natural child pacing and never exceed 1.5 spoken words/second without an explicit locale calibration proving a safer higher rate.
- Do not pad with filler. Use explicit observation/response silence.
- Existing approved English text fragments in Arabic source files are canonical seeds; preserve their meaning and wording unless a conflict is documented.
- French copy must read as native French, not English-mediated translation.
